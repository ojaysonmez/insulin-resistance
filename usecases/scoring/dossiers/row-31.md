## 1. The decision and who makes it
- Decision: in an adult on insulin whose diabetes type is doubtful (long-duration "type 1", or insulin started for type 2, or atypical course), can insulin be stopped, avoided or reduced (replaced by non-insulin drugs) or must it continue. Reclassification is the first half; the withdrawal step is the second half and is the primary decision in the row text.
- Who: the treating clinician. Primary care or general internal medicine orders the single draw (V4, V5 setting "routine care"); the stop/de-escalate decision is usually shared with or sent to endocrinology (V2 tag "specialist outpatient"). The ranking shows both settings.
- Measure type scoped here: random (non-fasting) plasma C-peptide with a concurrent plasma glucose. Fasting and stimulated (glucagon, mixed-meal) C-peptide are covered only where stated. Insulin itself is not used: exogenous insulin makes insulin assays uninterpretable, which is why C-peptide is the test.

## 3. Established vs not
Established (consensus level, not outcome level):
- Random C-peptide with concurrent glucose, in insulin-treated adults more than 3 years from diagnosis, separates absolute insulin deficiency from preserved secretion: at 600 pmol/L or more the person is not absolutely insulin-deficient; below 80 pmol/L, deficient; 200 to 600 pmol/L is ambiguous (Holt 2021, as recorded in the project). Measure type: random C-peptide.
- A C-peptide check is expected before stopping insulin in an insulin-treated person (ADA 2026 sec 2 wording, as recorded by V1/V6). This is a "check first" rule, not a validated "stop at this value" rule.
- Invalid or unreliable in the first 3 years, within about 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency, with renal impairment, or with glucose below 4 mmol/L (project row 6).

Not established:
- That a given random C-peptide cutoff predicts safe, sustained insulin withdrawal (no ketoacidosis, HbA1c at target at 6 to 12 months). Retrieved data: Funakoshi 2011 (fasting and glucagon-stimulated, inpatient, LR about 2) and Gavigan 2023 (random, n = 30 with values, retrospective, GLP-1 RA add-on). No prospective study with a pre-specified cutoff and a ketoacidosis endpoint was found.
- Whether fasting or stimulated C-peptide outperform random for the withdrawal decision. The consensus prefers random (practical); stimulated values come from LADA/classification work (project row 6), not from withdrawal studies.
- Incremental value over antibodies, clinical features and genetic risk score for the withdrawal decision (D8 never quantified; V2).
- The 200 to 600 pmol/L zone: Holt says usually T1D or MODY but possibly T2D; no pathway for de-escalation in that zone.

## 5. Concrete viability pathway
Missing piece (ranking): a prospective study of C-peptide-guided withdrawal or de-escalation with safety outcomes and a stated cutoff and timing. Type: evidence; effort: medium (my judgement; two stages).
- Stage A, prospective multicentre cohort (US, 3 to 5 sites, 12-month follow-up). Population: adults 18+ on insulin more than 3 years (or with a documented clinical question of type), no hyperglycaemic emergency in the previous 2 weeks (the project's 2-week rule), eGFR above a stated floor (renal impairment invalidates the value), not on sulfonylurea at sampling, glucose 4 mmol/L or higher at draw. Measurements: random plasma C-peptide with concurrent glucose, GAD (and IA-2/ZnT8) antibodies, type 1 genetic risk score if available; repeat at baseline in a subset to check stability.
- Intervention: pre-specified algorithm. C-peptide 600 pmol/L (1.8 ng/mL) or more [per Holt; Gavigan's 0.8 ng/mL = about 265 pmol/L is a much lower candidate and is untested prospectively]: step-down insulin over a defined schedule with GLP-1 RA, SGLT2 inhibitor excluded or used with ketone monitoring as the protocol states, and weekly glucose and ketone contact for 4 weeks. 200 to 600 pmol/L: de-escalate basal only, never to zero, with antibody result as modifier. Below 200 pmol/L (or below 80): continue insulin, no withdrawal.
- Primary safety endpoint: diabetic ketoacidosis (pH below 7.30 or bicarbonate below 18 mmol/L with ketones) or hospital admission for hyperglycaemia/ketosis within 12 months. Secondary safety: severe hypoglycaemia, symptomatic hyperglycaemia needing rescue insulin. Efficacy: proportion off insulin at 6 and 12 months with HbA1c within a margin (for example not more than 0.5 percentage points above baseline or at an individual target; Davis 2007 used a rise below 0.5%), body weight, insulin dose.
- Size and time (rough, judgement): to bound DKA above 2% with 95% confidence at zero observed events needs about 150 withdrawn patients (rule of three); to estimate off-insulin success in each C-peptide band with a usable interval, 300 enrolled with about 100 per band is a reasonable first target. Recruitment 12 to 18 months plus 12 months follow-up: about 2.5 to 3 years; cost class "medium" as in the ranking.
- Stage B (only if A is clean): pragmatic randomised comparison of C-peptide-guided de-escalation vs continued insulin in the band at or above 600 pmol/L, HbA1c non-inferiority and DKA as safety; large effort, 4 to 5 years.
- Guideline target: ADA Standards of Care section 2 (Figure 2.1 notes and the associated text) to move from "must measure" to a stated cutoff and timing; section 9 (pharmacologic approaches) for a de-escalation pathway; ADA/EASD adult T1D consensus (Holt) update. Assay target: harmonised random-C-peptide reference interval with concurrent-glucose rule (D4 3, low-end concentrations less affected by method disagreement per V3).

## 6. Risks and why the composite fell
- S lowered to 1 (clarification 18, RS-D1S): the default action without the result is to continue insulin, which is not itself dangerous; the marginal harm is unnecessary insulin (injections, hypoglycaemia), symptomatic and reversible. Mean of V4 (1) and V5 (2) was 1.5; correction applied 2026-10-23 and noted in V5.json. S/4 fell 0.375 to 0.25; at weight 0.20 the composite fell about 0.025, from about 0.604 (tier A) to 0.579 (tier B). The fall is a consequence of the default-action rule, not of new evidence. Under the same rule the harm of the opposite error (wrongful withdrawal, DKA) is not scored in S; it is carried in D6 (2). If the use case is evaluated as "empirical withdrawal guided by C-peptide" S would be higher (V4 note); that reading is not the current one.
- Clinical risks of the use itself: falsely high C-peptide (renal impairment, sulfonylurea, sampling soon after hyperglycaemia or on high glucose, hypoglycaemia sampling), leading to insulin withdrawal in a truly insulin-deficient patient and ketoacidosis; LADA/slowly progressive T1D and honeymoon-like periods in which C-peptide is present but falling (Section 3); misreading the ambiguous 200 to 600 band; assay variation at higher concentrations; SGLT2 inhibitor use as the replacement drug in a misclassified patient increases ketoacidosis risk [recall; not retrieved this session].
- Evidence risk: circularity (C-peptide defines the reference outcome, V2); retrospective and inpatient data; GLP-1 RA add-on results (Gavigan) may not transfer to withdrawal in doubtful-type patients.
