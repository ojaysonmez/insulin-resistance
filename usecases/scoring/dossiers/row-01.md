## 1. The decision and who makes it

- Decision: in an adult without diabetes who has documented Whipple's triad and no evident cause (drug, critical illness, hormone deficiency, non-islet tumour), is the hypoglycaemia caused by endogenous hyperinsulinism (most often insulinoma) or by something else (accidental or surreptitious insulin or secretagogue, insulin autoimmune hypoglycaemia, non-insulin-mediated causes)? A positive answer leads to imaging for localisation and, for insulinoma, surgery. A negative answer ends the insulinoma pursuit and redirects the work-up.
- Who: an endocrinologist (outpatient or admitted for a supervised fast), or an emergency or inpatient team that catches a critical sample during a spontaneous episode and refers on. Primary care refers after Whipple's triad is documented.
- Sample sources: a spontaneous episode (critical sample), a fast of up to 72 h, or a mixed-meal test for postprandial symptoms (row 23 and row 5 cover the postprandial variants; not scored here).
- Setting tag in the score files: specialist outpatient.

## 3. What is established and what is not (fasting-induced or spontaneous-event insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, BOHB; not post-load)

Established (judgement: high confidence unless noted)
- The physiology: at glucose below about 55 mg/dL endogenous insulin secretion is suppressed, so insulin, C-peptide and proinsulin that stay at or above the thresholds show inappropriate secretion. No other test shows this. Source: guideline text, PMID 19088155.
- A strong recommendation (grade 1) in a 2009 Endocrine Society guideline and a restatement by ENETS 2016 to measure these analytes at documented hypoglycaemia in an adult without diabetes and without an evident cause. Quality is moderate by the guideline's own upgrade argument, on indirect and expert evidence.
- Sample timing is part of the test: Whipple's triad first; insulin, C-peptide and proinsulin are sent only from samples with glucose below 60 mg/dL (guideline Table 4); the fast ends at glucose below 45 mg/dL with symptoms, or at 72 h. About two thirds of insulinomas meet criteria within 24 h and most within 48 h (guideline text, citing Service and O'Brien-type series).
- Accuracy in confirmed cases: sensitivity above 90% in 237 operated insulinomas (PMID 19141587, retrospective, abstract level).

Not established
- Specificity in consecutive, unselected patients with Whipple's triad. No prospective accuracy study with a pre-specified reference standard was found in the sources read. The only specificity figure (above 70%) comes from a case series.
- Thresholds on current US-marketed assays. The 3 uU/mL, 0.6 ng/mL and 5 pmol/L cut-offs date from assays of the 1990s and 2000s. Insulin immunoassays differ in analogue and proinsulin cross-reactivity (0% to 264% for analogues across 16 assays, abstract level) and are not fully harmonised; C-peptide harmonisation is further along (excerpt, PMID 30802485, identifiers unverified). One critical appraisal (excerpt) argues for a higher insulin cut-off of 5-6 uIU/mL on older assays and says newer assays are more specific.
- Any outcome benefit of measurement-guided work-up. The guideline's "moderate" quality rests on the logic that the alternative is a less thorough work-up; there is no randomised or comparative outcome study.
- BOHB and glucagon-response cut-offs on current assays. Not checked this session beyond the guideline text.
- Whether a shorter fast (48 h) or an outpatient fast is as accurate. A secondary source (Medscape, 2025-08-26 excerpt) says 90-95% of insulinomas are diagnosed within 48 h; not verified in a primary source here.

Limits on use (from the guideline text and the project row): exogenous insulin (insulin high, C-peptide and proinsulin suppressed; analogues may be undetected or over-read by the insulin immunoassay), sulfonylurea or glinide (insulin, C-peptide and proinsulin all high, so a drug screen is needed), insulin autoantibodies (insulin autoimmune hypoglycaemia; antibodies need not be drawn during the episode), renal failure (C-peptide and proinsulin clearance falls), sampling at glucose above the threshold, recent partial pancreatectomy (BOHB), hypoglycaemia not documented by a precise laboratory glucose (not a meter). Renal-failure and partial-pancreatectomy caveats come from the project row and were not rechecked here.

## 5. Concrete viability pathway

Aim: move D2/D8 up by validating thresholds and added value on current US assays, and move D3/D4 up through an updated guideline and assay-specific cut-offs. Two steps, the first cheap.

Step A (small effort, months, existing data): retrospective re-analysis of stored critical-sample or fast-sample aliquots and result tables from one or two high-volume US referral laboratories, linked to final diagnosis. Output: sensitivity and specificity of each analyte on the platforms used, and ROC-derived cut-offs. Limits: referral bias, assays changed over time, analogue exposure poorly recorded.

Step B (medium effort, a prospective multicentre study):
- Design: prospective, consecutive-enrolment, STARD-style diagnostic-accuracy study with a pre-specified reference standard.
- Population: adults (18 and over) without diabetes, with documented Whipple's triad and no evident cause after history and review; exclude known insulin or secretagogue use that is confirmed, and record analogue exposure separately. Include spontaneous-episode, supervised-fast and mixed-meal sampling in separate strata.
- Index tests: insulin and C-peptide (each on at least two to three of the main US immunoassay platforms from split aliquots), proinsulin, BOHB, glucagon response, sulfonylurea and glinide screen, insulin antibodies, all at glucose below 55-60 mg/dL; frozen aliquots for a mass-spectrometry comparator (as in PMID 38549041 style workflows).
- Reference standard: histology of a resected insulin-producing tumour with resolution of hypoglycaemia; for non-operated patients, a final diagnosis by a blinded panel after at least 12 months of follow-up; documented surreptitious or autoimmune causes classed as non-insulinoma.
- Endpoints: primary, sensitivity and specificity of each analyte and of the combined panel for endogenous hyperinsulinism (and for insulinoma) at the guideline cut-offs and at assay-specific cut-offs; secondary, added value over clinical features alone, share of insulinomas missed by insulin alone but caught by C-peptide or proinsulin, time to biochemical confirmation (a 48 h versus 72 h fast), the effect of analogue cross-reactivity and renal function on false results, and inter-platform agreement (Bland-Altman) on paired samples.
- Assay step for current US immunoassays: (1) list the US-cleared insulin, C-peptide and proinsulin assays in use at the sites from the manufacturers' inserts (not done here); (2) record each insulin assay's cross-reactivity with human insulin, proinsulin, and the common analogues (lispro, aspart, glulisine, glargine, detemir, degludec) from inserts or from a spiking experiment; (3) calibrate or align C-peptide to the reference method and report the traceability; (4) derive assay-specific cut-offs by ROC and give the ES cut-offs a bridging check on each platform; (5) report results in both conventional and SI units.
- Size (a rough judgement): sensitivity 0.90 with a 95% confidence half-width of about 0.09 needs about 40 insulinoma cases, and about 0.07 needs about 70. With insulinoma at 6-13% of evaluated adults in referral cohorts (the figures V8 uses), 40-70 cases means roughly 400-900 evaluated adults. Specificity would then be estimated on the 350-800 others with the same width.
- Sites and time: 6-10 US referral centres with a supervised-fast programme. At a few insulinomas per centre per year (the Mayo series is about 11 per year, an upper end), 40-70 cases take about 2-3 years of accrual; add about 1 year for set-up and about 1 year for follow-up and analysis: roughly 4-5 years in total. These are planning estimates, not figures from a source.
- Guideline target: an Endocrine Society update of the section on hypoglycaemia in people without diabetes (the 2023 update did not touch it), asking for assay-specific cut-offs and naming the analogue/MS caveat. ENETS and the NANETS consensus are secondary targets. Realistic: a guideline revision will follow the study by one to three years (judgement).
- What the score gain would be (judgement): D2 stays 3 or reaches a firm 3; D8 2 to 3; D4 2 to 3; D3 3 unchanged unless the guideline text makes the panel a precondition (then 4). Composite effect about +0.03 to +0.06; the largest single move would still be the D7 sourcing.

## 6. Risks

- Rare condition: slow accrual and few insulinomas per centre; mitigated by pooling centres, but 40 cases is already a thin base for combinations of analytes.
- Reference-standard bias: non-operated patients have no tissue diagnosis; follow-up diagnoses can be wrong (surreptitious use, autoimmune cases).
- Assay churn: manufacturers change assays during a multi-year study; cut-offs derived on one platform version may not carry over.
- Analogue and proinsulin cross-reactivity produce false patterns; patient insulin use is hard to establish and the patient may be concealing it.
- A supervised fast carries hypoglycaemia risk (burden already counted in D6) and may need ethics review for the added research sampling.
- The study may show that C-peptide plus proinsulin outperforms insulin, which would leave the guideline wording on insulin outdated and may lower the D4 or D3 reading for insulin specifically.
- Positive-predictive-value inflation if enrolment draws on referral cohorts; the result would not transfer to emergency-department critical samples.
- Funding and sponsor interest are low (no product driver); the guideline target and the laboratory harmonisation work are the real gating steps.
