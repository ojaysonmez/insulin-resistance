# Row 6 dossier: random C-peptide to reclassify insulin-treated adults of uncertain diabetes type

Stage 3 deep dossier (D-32). Written 2026-10-23. Measure type throughout: **random (non-fasting) serum or plasma C-peptide with a concurrent glucose. Not fasting-only, not post-load or mixed-meal, not urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio, not an insulin-resistance index.** Class of use: classification / secretion (not insulin resistance). Scores in `usecases/scoring/` and the deliverables were not edited.

Current scores as read from the unit files (U1 to U5, V7, RS-D1S): D1 4, D2 3, D8 3, D3 4, D4 3, D5 3, D6 3, D7 3 (low; V7 chain; alternative bins 2 and 4), S 2 (low). Tier A, rank 1, composite 0.705 (`usecases/scoring/ranking.md`).

## 1. The decision and who makes it
- **Decision:** in an adult already on insulin whose diabetes type is uncertain (clinician label of type 1 but features of type 2 or monogenic diabetes; or insulin-treated type 2 with doubt), is there enough endogenous insulin secretion to (a) change the label, (b) replace or stop insulin, (c) send MODY genetics. Absent C-peptide confirms insulin dependence.
- **Who:** endocrinologist or diabetologist in a specialist clinic in most pathways; primary care or an NP can order it in a stable patient (U4 and U5 disagree on setting tag; the test is a routine laboratory test, the decision usually sits with a specialist). Ordering is one blood draw plus a paired glucose.
- **Population (narrowest, per Holt 2021):** insulin-treated adults, more than 3 years from diagnosis, after islet autoantibodies (GAD first) were done or were not conclusive. Newly diagnosed adults are not the population.
- **Default without the test:** continue insulin and the current label (U4 reasoning, RS-D1S). Harm of the default: unnecessary insulin burden and hypoglycaemia, missed MODY or non-insulin options. The acute harm (ketoacidosis) comes from a wrong result or wrong withdrawal, not from absent information.

## 3. Established versus not (measure type: random serum C-peptide with concurrent glucose)
**Established (consensus level, not outcome-trial level):**
- Two consensus documents (Holt 2021; ADA Standards 2026, which reproduces it) place random C-peptide in a defined step: insulin-treated, >3 y from diagnosis, autoantibodies first. Same documents say it must be measured before stopping insulin (secondhand via U6).
- C-peptide below about 80 to 200 pmol/L confirms absolute insulin deficiency; a high value (>=600 pmol/L) makes insulin dependence unlikely (Holt, Jones). The biology and the reference-standard role are consistent across cohorts (31558459, 32634859).
- Single-centre implementation data show a yield: 6.8% reclassified, 1.5% stopped insulin, 1.6% monogenic diabetes found (33131101).

**Not established:**
- No controlled study shows that C-peptide-guided management beats usual care on HbA1c, hypoglycaemia, ketoacidosis or quality of life. The one cohort is uncontrolled, UK, one centre, in people already labelled T1D, not "uncertain type".
- The Holt "11% reclassified" figure is from a source I could not trace (Holt ref 31, per U6); it differs from 6.8% in 33131101. Do not treat 11% as confirmed.
- Incremental value over autoantibodies plus clinical features plus genetic score is not quantified; the accuracy papers use C-peptide as the reference.
- Thresholds are assay-agnostic in the consensus; Holt and Jones differ; the 200-600 pmol/L band is expert opinion; LADA bands are expert opinion only.
- Not covered by this dossier: fasting-only C-peptide, post-load or glucagon-stimulated C-peptide (kept in rows 31 and 5-type uses), urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio, children.

## 4. Three weakest points in the current scores
1. **D7 = 3, confidence low.** Four of five chain links are assumptions (insulin-treated 25%, >3 y 85%, unclear type 12%, evaluated share 5%); point 37,000, range 7,000-120,000, so bins 2 to 4 (V7, V6 skeptic). The UK 14%/86% figure (27080317) is not US and uses urinary C-peptide. Note the other unit (U1) gave 4 and S4 gave 4 with different chains. *What would change it:* a US source for insulin-treated adults with unclear type (NHANES or T1D Exchange/registry data on insulin-treated adults with C-peptide or antibody status; claims or laboratory data on C-peptide order volume in insulin-treated adults). A measured US annual testing rate would settle the bin; I estimate it stays 3 or 4 but cannot show this from sources. Confidence in the bin 3 is low (about 40%).
2. **D8 = 3 (low) and D2 = 3 (medium), linked.** Anchor 19 wants a reclassification rate given the alternatives. The only rate is 6.8% (33131101) in T1D-labelled people in one UK clinic, tested with no comparison arm, and nobody compared C-peptide against antibodies plus clinical model in the same patients. The 6.8% is a rate of reclassification, not of better outcomes; 3.4% (13 + 16 of 859) had a documented treatment benefit. Strict reading of the anchor gives D8 2. *What would change it:* a US or multi-site cohort in which every patient gets antibodies, clinical model and random C-peptide, reporting how often C-peptide alone changed the label or treatment and what happened to HbA1c, hypoglycaemia and ketoacidosis. If the incremental yield were small, D8 falls to 2; if a controlled comparison showed benefit, D2 could reach 4 only with hard outcomes.
3. **D4 = 3 (low) and S = 2 (low).** D4 rests on a Chinese 94-laboratory survey (CV 24.5%, 5.7% recalibrated; 35177563) and a Chinese commutability study (37141618); no US cross-platform data at 80, 200 and 600 pmol/L. Thresholds are used without assay-specific cut-offs, and the 200-600 band is where disagreement of 20 to 25% matters. S is low because the unit could not name a quantified harm of the default; the "must be measured before stopping insulin" footnote implies a real ketoacidosis risk when insulin is withdrawn untested, but no event rate exists. *What would change it:* a US split-sample comparison on current US-marketed C-peptide assays at the decision thresholds against an ID-LC-MS/MS reference (D4 to 3 with medium confidence, or to 2 if disagreement crosses thresholds); an event count of ketoacidosis after insulin withdrawal in untested versus tested patients (S).

## 5. Concrete viability pathway
Missing piece (matches the ranking's evidence-type path): outcome evidence plus assay harmonisation, then guideline wording. All sizes and durations below are my judgement, not computed from a source.
- **Step A, assay harmonisation sub-study (assay; medium effort, 9 to 12 months).** Split fresh residual sera (at least 120 samples spanning 30 to 1,000 pmol/L with at least 40 in the 150-700 pmol/L band) across 4 or more US-marketed immunoassay platforms and the TaMADOR-type ID-LC-MS/MS reference procedure (38549041). Use commutable frozen human serum pools, not WHO ISR 13/146-derived materials, for calibration checks (37141618). Outputs: bias and agreement at 80, 200, 600 pmol/L; platform-specific equivalent cutoffs; rule for paired glucose (>=4 mmol/L) and eGFR. Tests whether the consensus cutoffs transfer across platforms.
- **Step B, prospective pragmatic cohort or stepped-wedge clinic trial (evidence; medium to large, 3 to 4 years).** Setting: 8 to 12 US diabetes clinics (academic and community), including a T1D Exchange-type network. Population: adults 18+, insulin-treated, 3 or more years from diagnosis, type uncertain by pre-specified criteria (for example T1D label with BMI >=30 or diagnosis age >=35, or insulin-treated T2D with no insulin requirement in the first year), no ketoacidosis in 2 weeks, eGFR above a stated limit, glucose >=4 mmol/L. All get autoantibodies, clinical model and random C-peptide. Cluster stepped-wedge (usual care then C-peptide-guided pathway) allows a comparison; a single-arm cohort with matched untested controls from the same EHR is the cheaper version.
- **Endpoints.** Primary: proportion with reclassification and documented treatment change at 12 months; co-primary safety: ketoacidosis and severe hypoglycaemia events through 12 months after any insulin reduction (non-inferiority on HbA1c, margin 0.5%). Secondary: MODY genetic yield and treatment change, HbA1c, time in range if CGM, insulin dose, patient-reported burden, healthcare use. Pre-specify the C-peptide band (<200, 200-600, >=600 pmol/L on harmonised units) and report by band.
- **Size and time.** To estimate a roughly 7% reclassification rate within +/-1.5 percentage points needs about 1,100 tested; allow 1,500 to 2,500 for platform, band and clinic strata. That gives about 100 to 170 reclassified, enough for a descriptive safety signal but not for a rare ketoacidosis rate (the upper 95% bound with zero events in 100 withdrawals is about 3%). Enrolment 18 to 24 months, follow-up 12, analysis 6: roughly 3 to 4 years. A randomized trial of testing versus no testing would be larger and is probably not needed for tier movement.
- **Guideline target.** ADA Standards of Care section 2 (and the next ADA/EASD adult T1D consensus update) with platform-specific cutoffs, a stated eGFR rule and a statement on what to do in the 200-600 band. Without outcome data the wording stays at consensus level; with it, a graded recommendation becomes possible.
- **Movement in scores if done.** D2 3 to 3 or 4 (4 only with controlled hard-outcome data), D8 clarified (2 or 3 with data), D4 confidence up, D7 sourced if registry linkage gives a US testing rate, S sourced. Judgement: probability that Step A is completed within 12 months if funded about 70%; probability that Step B shows non-inferior HbA1c with no excess ketoacidosis about 65% (low confidence).

## 6. Risks and what would make it fail
- Assay disagreement near the 200 and 600 pmol/L cutoffs gives platform-dependent labels. If agreement is poor and cannot be fixed by recalibration, the thresholds fail and per-platform cutoffs become necessary.
- Wrong-condition use: tested within 3 years of diagnosis, within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency, with glucose <4 mmol/L, or with renal impairment (C-peptide high with low clearance) gives false labels (Holt, via project file). Exogenous insulin does not affect C-peptide the way it affects insulin assays, but the patient must be taking their usual insulin; sulfonylurea or recent glucose-lowering changes can raise C-peptide.
- False-high C-peptide leading to insulin withdrawal in a person with T1D (ketoacidosis). The consensus rule not to repeat <80 pmol/L and to confirm before discontinuation limits this; no event data exist.
- Small yield: 1.5% stopped insulin in 33131101. If a US cohort shows a lower yield, the use may be judged not worth a visit-level step outside referral clinics.
- Autoantibodies plus a clinical model already classify most patients (AUC 0.90 to 0.97); if they resolve most doubtful cases, C-peptide's added value falls.
- Uncontrolled design: any new cohort without controls will not lift D2 above 3.
- Evidence base is UK-heavy; a US multi-ethnic cohort could show different rates.
