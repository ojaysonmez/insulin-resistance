## 2. US-marketed C-peptide immunoassay platforms and manufacturers

**Regulatory point (verified in 21 CFR 862.1135 via the NyquistAI regulation search):** a "C-peptides of proinsulin test system" is Class I and exempt from premarket notification. So current C-peptide **assays** normally have no 510(k). The 510(k) records that exist are for the Class II **calibrators** and for pre-exemption assays from the 1980s and 1990s. "No 510(k) found" therefore does not mean "not US-marketed". US marketing status has to come from manufacturer US pages or FDA establishment listings, which I did not search.

## 5. Suggested first approaches (ranked by fit; judgement, not endorsement)

1. **University of Missouri DDL / C-peptide Standardization Program (Step A reference and materials).** It is the only US laboratory with a JCTLM-listed C-peptide RMP, commutable serum materials and a record of multi-manufacturer studies (PMID 39803820, FT). Ask about RMP value assignment for at least 120 residual sera, access to secondary materials, and whether the NIDDK committee would co-design the 80, 200 and 600 pmol/L analysis.
2. **University of Washington Hoofnagle lab / TaMADOR (second mass-spectrometry arm).** An independent LC-MS/MS method that has already been compared with the Missouri RMP (PMIDs 34723236, 38549041). Ask whether a CLIA-validated service exists or whether the work would be a research collaboration.
3. **Manufacturers, through the standardisation committee rather than one by one.** Roche, Abbott, Siemens (Centaur and Atellica), DiaSorin and Tosoh have US-documented C-peptide products (FDA-db or Web). Beckman, QuidelOrtho, Fujirebio, ALPCO and Mercodia need US status checked. Hörber 2023 suggests the Roche and Centaur arms matter most, because they diverge most. An alternative is an independent US hospital or reference lab that runs several platforms on its own split samples.
4. **RADIANT (Step B population and case-finding).** It is the closest match to "insulin-treated, type uncertain" (PMIDs 37104866, 42892882). An ancillary study is likely more feasible than a separate network.
5. **T1DX-QI (Step B scale and stepped-wedge infrastructure).** It has adult clinics and a shared data platform, but its population is T1D-labelled and mostly paediatric. Use it for the "T1D label with BMI 30 or more, or diagnosed at 35 or older" arm.
6. **CAP accuracy-based survey (field baseline).** If the ABGIC survey still runs, its summaries would give US platform bias against reference values at low cost. Status unverified.
7. **Single academic centres** (Johns Hopkins, Indiana, Penn, Chicago) as founding Step B sites or protocol co-authors.

## 6. Gaps and cautions

- No published US split-sample comparison of current US-marketed C-peptide assays at 80, 200 and 600 pmol/L was found. Rohlfing 2025 is the closest, but it is anonymised and covers 0.05 to 4.23 nmol/L without decision-threshold reporting. Hörber 2023 is German.
- The Class I exemption (21 CFR 862.1135) means FDA records cannot confirm which C-peptide assays are on the US market. Confirm with manufacturer US labelling.
- Within-person biological variation, reference change value and repeatability of random C-peptide in insulin-treated adults were not covered by any source here. Rohlfing 2025 (FT) says biological variability, allowable error and universal thresholds "still need to be thoroughly studied". Step A cannot set allowable bias without them.
- C-peptide stability (no carrier protein in 13/146; pre-analytical handling) is flagged in Rohlfing 2025 but not quantified here. Step A sample handling needs its own validation.
- CDC and NIST roles were not found, and Labcorp, TrialNet and ARUP's method are unverified.
- No contact was made with any organisation. Willingness, capacity, fees and data-sharing terms are all unknown.
- The look-up limit (30) limited further checks on adult ketosis-prone diabetes cohorts (Baylor), US C-peptide testing volumes and registry trials.
