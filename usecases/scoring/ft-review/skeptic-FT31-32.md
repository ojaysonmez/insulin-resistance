## 2. (a) The score proposal: row 47 D4, 3 to 2 (C196)

**Score in force.** V3 row 47 D4 = 3, no correction (confidence medium). FT12 moved rows 6 and 31 to 2 and did not mention row 47. Across all score files row 47 is the only C-peptide row left at D4 = 3: rows 6, 13, 28, 29, 31, 32 and 49 are 2 (checked by scan of V3, V10, V16, V22, U3, V27 entries). So the reader's "rows 6 and 31 already at 2" is correct.

**Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CHANGE.** Move to 2 (confidence low), but on corrected reasoning. Reasons, each checked:

1. Consistency is the strong argument, and the reader gives it only second place. Row 47 scores a fixed low cut-off (fasting C-peptide below 0.6 ng/mL, about 0.2 nmol/L, calculated), which is the region where the FT12 evidence (C080, inter-system CV 20.1-31.6%, worst at the lowest-concentration sample; Jones 2013) says assays disagree most. Clarification 25 scores the assay only, and the same analyte sits at 2 in seven other rows. A 3 for row 47 alone is an outlier, not a judgement that the low end is better standardised.
2. "The tested calibrator range 0.43-1.81 nmol/L excludes the 0.2 nmol/L cut-off" is true but overstated as "never tested". The text says the 8 serum calibrator samples were "chosen from preliminary C-peptide measurements to cover a wide concentration range (final LC-MS concentration range, 0.43-1.81 nmol/L)". That is the range of the 8 calibrators. The range of the other 32 samples is not stated in the stored text, so I cannot confirm that no sample was below 0.43. What can be said: the paper reports no result near 0.2 nmol/L and, being healthy volunteers (fasting and 60 min after a liquid meal), it could not have, because 0.2 nmol/L is a severe-deficiency value. Write: "calibrators 0.43-1.81 nmol/L; no result at or near 0.2 nmol/L reported; healthy volunteers only".
3. C196 alone does not justify the move and partly argues the other way. Its abstract says results "do not always agree, especially at higher C-peptide concentrations" (this is the sentence V3 relied on), and after normalisation to LC-MS laboratory means no longer differed (P=0.24). The "about 1.5-2 times the LC-MS reference" figure is the reader's ratio of LS means 1.55-1.95 to 0.99: the text gives no unit for 1.55-1.95 and the means come from a log-transformed analysis, so label it "reader's ratio, unit not stated in the extraction". The primary citations for the new D4 reason should be C080/C026 (as in FT12 for rows 6 and 31); C196 is supporting.
4. The reader's own flag is right: 2008 data, healthy volunteers, 2007 methods, no US platform identifiable. Clarification 20 caps confidence at medium; low is correct.
5. Consistency with row 49: row 49 D4 = 2 (V3) is stated on the timed-sampling and index grounds, not on C196; no change to row 49 follows. Row 31 D4 = 2 already; no change.
6. Effect: D4 3 to 2 lowers the row's UQ by a fraction of a point; the proposal is not a rank-driver. Recommend the user decide it together with a note in `DECISIONS.md` that fixed low-cut-off C-peptide uses (rows 6, 31, 47) share one D4 score of 2.
