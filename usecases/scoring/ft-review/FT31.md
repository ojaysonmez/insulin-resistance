# FT31 full-text review (7 papers)

Read only local full texts in `corpus/fulltext/`. Current scores were taken from the score files after corrections (FT12 changes included). JSON companion: `usecases/scoring/ft-review/FT31.json`.

## Per paper

- **C190** Service 1991 (PMID 1677058, DOI 10.1016/s0025-6196(12)62083-7; text good): adds nuance. Both V8 inputs are in the full text (MEN1 17/224 = 7.6%; stable-period incidence 0.396 per 100,000 person-years), but the incidence rests on 8 Olmsted County cases and has a 95% CI of 0.16-0.82 per 100,000, i.e. about 540-2,800 insulinomas per year in the US (calculated, population [recall]) against the point 1,360. Table 1 (age by sex) is garbled, so no paediatric count (row 24) could be read. Measure type: none (patients with proven insulinoma). Quote: "0.396 per 100,000 person-years in Olmsted County (95% confidence interval, 0.16 to 0.82)".
- **C194** Stenstrom 2005 (PMID 16306343, DOI 10.2337/diabetes.54.suppl_2.s68; text good): adds nuance. Narrative review of a Swedish cohort: LADA about 10% of incident type 2-phenotype diabetes aged 40-75, 25% under 35; stimulated C-peptide (IV glucose plus glucagon) and fasting C-peptide; meal-based responses "unknown". The "10.2% in African-Americans" cited in V1 is not in this text. Prevalence of about 10% sits inside the 2-12% range used for row 47. Quote: "No comparison between oral versus intravenous responses of insulin secretion in LADA patients has been published".
- **C196** Little 2008 (PMID 18420730, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2007.101287; text good, about 2,700 words, complete 4-page paper): adds nuance. 40 serum samples from 24 healthy volunteers (fasting and post-meal), 15 laboratories, 9 methods of 2007; calibrators 0.43-1.81 nmol/L, so the 0.2 nmol/L (0.6 ng/mL) and 80 pmol/L levels were never tested. Pre-normalisation laboratory means 1.55-1.95 vs a reference mean of 0.99 nmol/L; after calibration to LC-MS no difference (P=0.24). Measure type: C-peptide, fasting and post-meal serum from healthy people. Quote: "improved the comparability among laboratories" (two-column extraction interleaves the key sentence).
- **C197** Placzkowski 2009 (PMID 19141587, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-2031; text good): adds nuance. The 6% postprandial-only share is 13 of 214 patients with a reliable history (not 237); only 3 of the 13 had a negative 72-h fast. Noninvasive imaging found the tumour in 71-80%; EUS or SACST used in 21-39% in 1993-2007; MEN1 14/237 (6%). Insulin >=3 uU/mL 93%/95% sensitivity/specificity, C-peptide >=0.6 ng/mL 100%/60%, in 69 cases vs 20 healthy women after a 72-h fast. Measure type: fasting or spontaneous-hypoglycaemia insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, BOHB, IV glucagon response. Quote: "Hypoglycemia was reported solely in the fasting state in 73%, the fasting and postprandial state in 21%, and exclusively postprandially in 6%."
- **C235** Cosma 2019 (PMID 30978327, DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2019.04.057; text good, about 2,500 words and complete; unit symbol lost, read as ug/L): adds nuance. One Italian laboratory, 165 sera, three platforms (Mindray CL-2000i, Tosoh AIA-CL2400, Immulite 2000): C-peptide bias -4.0% to -8.6%, CVs 1.4-4.65%, but the Mindray vs Tosoh intercept is -0.23 ug/L (CI -0.34 to -0.14) while the text calls the pair free of constant bias, and manufacturer reference intervals differ about 2-fold. Measure type: random serum C-peptide and insulin (analytical only). Quote: "Overall, results emphasize the need for standardization/harmonization efforts for both C-peptide and IRI measurement."
- **C259** Holt 2021 (PMID 34593612, DOI 10.2337/dci21-0043; text good): confirms. Consensus text recommends random C-peptide with concurrent glucose within 5 h of eating beyond 3 years, and before any insulin discontinuation; thresholds 80/200/600 pmol/L in the flowchart footnote; no test within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency. The report says it is consensus opinion; no evidence grading; support for insulin replacement is small older studies. Measure type: random plasma C-peptide. Quote: "represent the consensus opinion of the authors".
- **C268** Thomas 2023 (PMID 36368478, DOI 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2022.10.022; text good): adds nuance. Ten classification approaches for research datasets, scored against C-peptide (DARE, n=1,296, non-fasting <200 pmol/L at more than 3 years) or a genetic risk score (UK Biobank); best accuracy 85-88%, crude cut-offs 68-82%. C-peptide is the reference, so the paper cannot show what C-peptide adds; one author co-developed two of the models. Measure type: random C-peptide as reference only. Quote: "classifying insulin-treated diabetes within research datasets without measured classification biomarkers".

## Claim issues

1. V3 row 31 D4 reason (also rows 47, 49): says the low end near 0.2 nmol/L is less affected and that a reference system exists; C196 did not test below 0.43 nmol/L and its LC-MS method is a research-laboratory one. Suggested wording is in the JSON.
2. V1 row 47 D7: "10.2% in African-Americans" is not in Stenstrom 2005 (searched); the paper gives about 10% (Swedish, UKPDS) and 25% under 35.
3. V8 row 23 D7 and usecases-verified.md row 1: the 6% and 73% are of 214 patients with a reliable history, not 237; only 3 of the 13 postprandial-only patients had a negative 72-h fast. Score unaffected.
4. V8 rows 1, 22, 23: incidence given as a point value; the CI is 0.16-0.82 per 100,000. Bins unaffected.
5. V8 row 22: Mayo imaging missed 20-29% and EUS/SACST was used in 21-39% (tertiary rates), above the 5-10% used; still bin 1.
6. usecases-verified.md row 6: Thomas 2023 is not a comparator for C-peptide; C-peptide is its reference standard.
