All four full texts were read locally. No score changes are proposed. Details are in FT3.json.

## C134 (PMID 42318218, DOI 10.3389/fendo.2026.1784299), text good, verdict: adds nuance
Single case (67-year-old man) with 75 g OGTT insulin and C-peptide (fasting insulin 804 mU/L, IAA 115.57 COI) and a six-case review. PEG precipitation was not done, so the IAS diagnosis rests on IAA and the pattern; the PPI link is only suggested.
Quote: "The interference testing (PEG precipitation, dilution linearity, and heterophile blocking) was not performed"

## C139 (PMID 42662728, DOI 10.1016/j.metop.2026.100491), text good, verdict: confirms
Narrative review with one case and 12 ALA cases. Calls IAA the cornerstone and PEG cheap and widely available, but says no IAS guideline or randomized trial exists, which fits Limited status.
Quote: "no standardized treatment guidelines are available for IAS, largely because of its rarity and the absence of randomized controlled trials"

## C141 (PMID 42697708, DOI 10.1177/00045632261489517), text good, verdict: adds nuance
Expert narrative review; no infant counts or accuracy data. The PEG recommendation (first-line in neonatal hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia; about 10% fall normal, 20% indicates antibodies) is the authors' own. Interference frequency 0.5% to 4% is for general populations. Supports row 25 directly, row 20 only as background.
Quote: "A PEG screening test precipitates all classes and subclasses of antibodies and should be included among first-line hormonal investigations."

## C137 (PMID 42649063, DOI 10.2169/internalmedicine.7675-26), text partial, verdict: adds nuance
One 96-year-old woman: insulin 0.5 U/mL, C-peptide 0.03 ng/mL, IGF-I 11 ng/mL, 13 cm thoracic mass. IGF-II was not measured, so the diagnosis is suspected only.
Quote: "IGF-II was not measured in the present case because it was not available as a routine clinical assay"
