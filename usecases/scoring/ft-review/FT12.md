# FT12 full-text review (C026, C074, C080, C127, C169, C216, C231, C239)

## Per paper

- **C026** (PMID 23413806, DOI 10.1111/dme.12159; text good): confirms. Review (PubMed search to Aug 2012; no systematic method or grading) says random C-peptide with concurrent glucose >8 mmol/L was the best classifier in one large study and is 'likely' sufficient in most situations; stimulated <0.6 nmol/L suggests marked deficiency, <0.2 nmol/L absolute deficiency. For insulin withdrawal it says the role is mainly to exclude absolute deficiency, and that the withdrawal studies (Table 3) are short, use lax glycaemic targets and rarely include low C-peptide groups. About half of C-peptide is renally cleared, so blood values are falsely elevated in renal impairment and correction methods are poorly validated; assay standardisation has not been achieved, mainly at higher concentrations. Quote: "Mechanisms to correct for renal function when measuring C-peptide have been suggested, but are currently poorly validated"
- **C074** (PMID 34590174, DOI 10.1007/s00125-021-05568-3; text good): confirms. The text says a random C-peptide with concurrent glucose within 5 h of eating is recommended beyond 3 years where type is uncertain, and that in insulin-treated people it should always be done before insulin discontinuation. The Figure 1 note adds thresholds (>=600 pmol/L circumstances do not matter; <80 pmol/L no repeat; 200-600 usually T1D or MODY but can be insulin-treated T2D; no test within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency). Autoantibodies (GAD first) are the primary investigation. For suspected monogenic diabetes it says to test antibodies and C-peptide and to consider genetic testing only if antibodies are negative and non-fasting C-peptide >200 pmol/L. Renal impairment is named only as a confounder of the urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio. The authors state the report is consensus opinion because evidence is incomplete. Quote: "Where a person is treated with insulin, this test should always be performed prior to insulin discontinuation to exclude severe insulin deficiency."
- **C080** (PMID 35177563, DOI 10.3343/alm.2022.42.4.428; text partial): adds nuance. The local text has no abstract or tables; 24.5% and 5.7% are not found in it. It gives, for C-peptide, inter-laboratory CV 13.6-21.8% and inter-measurement-system CV 20.1-31.6% against a desirable 8.3%, worst for the lowest-concentration sample (31.6%); intra-system CV was acceptable (mean 6.6%). After recalibration all C-peptide inter-system CVs met 8.3%. For insulin inter-system CV was 10.7-58.3% (44.1% after recalibration at the low sample), and no insulin reference procedure is listed in JCTLM. Quote: "inter-measurement system %CVs ranging from 10.7% to 58.3% for insulin and from 20.1% to 31.6% for C-peptide"
- **C127** (PMID 41358893, DOI 10.2337/dc26-s002; text good): confirms. The C-peptide statements are in the footnote to the Figure 2.1 flowchart (web text), copied from Holt 2021; they are not in the numbered graded recommendations (2.5 only says to classify). No numbered recommendation or text names C-peptide for MODY (2.29b is genetic testing), ketosis-prone diabetes (only 'generally transient' insulin need), LADA (term acceptable, no C-peptide criterion) or staging (2.6-2.8b use autoantibodies and glucose). The text says misdiagnosis can occur in up to 40% of adults with new type 1 diabetes. Low C-peptide appears only as a late-stage marker and in fulminant checkpoint-inhibitor diabetes. Quote: "Where a person is insulin treated, C-peptide must be measured prior to insulin discontinuation to exclude severe insulin deficiency."
- **C169** (PMID 33131101, DOI 10.1111/dme.14449; text good): confirms. Single-centre routine clinic programme (Edinburgh) in people with clinician-diagnosed T1D of 3+ years who all started insulin within 3 years. Random non-fasting serum C-peptide on Abbott Architect; a value at or above about 200 pmol/L (symbol lost in extraction) triggered antibodies, T1 genetic risk score and genetic testing. 114 of 859 (13.2%) triggered; yield rose with age at diagnosis (2.5% under 18, 15.4% at 18-30, 25.9% over 30). 58 (6.8%) were reclassified (44 T2D, 14 monogenic); 13 (1.5%) stayed off insulin and 16 (1.9%) gained from co-therapy, with no severe hypoglycaemia or DKA reported. The threshold was pragmatic, and Abbott and Roche values differ (Abbott = 0.8755 x Roche - 40.6, so Abbott 200 is Roche 269). Quote: "The cause of diabetes was reclassified in 58 individuals (6.8% of the tested cohort)."
- **C216** (PMID 27080317, DOI 10.3399/bjgp16x684961; text good): adds nuance. 601 white European insulin-treated adults with 5+ years' diabetes, recruited by letter (volunteers) in three UK centres; 514 (86%) correctly classified by the UK guideline against a urinary C-peptide:creatinine gold standard (2 h after the largest meal). Of 87 misclassified, 57 (66%) were labelled T1D but made substantial insulin and 30 (34%) were labelled T2D but severely deficient. The authors state the 14% would be much lower across all people with diabetes. Quote: "most (n = 57, 66%, Figure 2) were misclassified as having type 1 diabetes and were producing substantial endogenous insulin"
- **C231** (PMID 29596402, DOI 10.15585/mmwr.mm6712a2; text good): confirms. Self-reported, 33,028 adults, response rate 54.3%; 3,519 with diabetes: T1D 0.55% (1.3 million), T2D 8.6% (21.0 million), other 0.31%. T1D means self-reported T1 plus current insulin use; self-reported T1 without insulin (n=182), unknown type and refusals were put in T2D. Type is not validated and the authors say insulin-secretion tests are unsuitable for surveys. It gives no count of insulin-treated adults with unclear type. Quote: "the estimated prevalences of diagnosed type 1 and type 2 diabetes were 0.55% (corresponding to 1.3 million U.S. adults)"
- **C239** (PMID 31558459, DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2019-031586; text good): confirms. Models built on 1,352 white European UK adults (179 T1D) diagnosed at 18-50 years; outcome defined by insulin within 3 years plus non-fasting C-peptide <200 pmol/L (T1D) or >600 pmol/L at 5+ years (T2D). Age and BMI alone gave AUC 0.904 in development and 0.865 in external validation (n=582); adding GADA, IA-2A and genetic risk score reached 0.97. In the 71 hardest cases antibodies alone gave 0.72 and genetic risk score alone 0.89. C-peptide is the outcome, never a predictor, so the paper cannot show what C-peptide adds. Quote: "Type 1 diabetes: Insulin treatment within ≤3 years of diabetes diagnosis and severe insulin deficiency (non-fasting C-peptide <200 pmol/L)."

## Claim issues

- usecases-verified.md row 6 and U4:D5 reason (renal impairment as an invalid condition for the test) (C074): Holt 2021 names impaired renal function only as a confounder of the urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio; the plasma-test caveat comes from Jones and Hattersley 2013, not Holt or ADA 2026 Figure 2.1. Wording: Plasma C-peptide can be falsely raised in renal impairment (Jones and Hattersley 2013; correction methods poorly validated); Holt 2021 mentions renal impairment only as a confounder of the urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio.
- usecases-verified.md rows 6 and 31 (ADA Standards 2026 as a second consensus source) (C127): In C127 the C-peptide advice is a footnote to Figure 2.1 (a copy of the Holt note), not a numbered, graded recommendation; the numbered recommendation 2.5 only says to classify. Wording: ADA Standards 2026 section 2 carries the Holt C-peptide advice in a note to Figure 2.1 (ungraded; based on White European data).
- usecases-verified.md row 6 (6.8% reclassified, abstract level) (C169): Full text adds that reclassification followed antibody, genetic-risk-score and sequencing work-up after a C-peptide trigger on Abbott (about 200 pmol/L; Roche equivalent 269), in a clinic of people who all started insulin within 3 years; 13 (1.5%) stayed off insulin, 16 (1.9%) added co-therapy, none had severe hypoglycaemia or DKA; yield rose with age at diagnosis (2.5%, 15.4%, 25.9%). The 11% in Holt is not printed in this paper. Wording: In one uncontrolled Edinburgh clinic (859 tested), random C-peptide followed by antibody, genetic-score and genetic testing reclassified 58 people (6.8%); 13 (1.5%) stopped insulin.
- usecases/scoring/U3.json row 6 D4 and V3.json row 31 D4 (C080): 'Inter-system CV 24.5%, 5.7% after recalibration' is not in the local C080 text (partial text, no abstract or tables). The text gives C-peptide inter-system CV 20.1-31.6% and says low-concentration samples were worst, which conflicts with V3's statement that the low end is less affected (Jones 2013 says disagreement is mainly at higher concentrations). Wording: In a survey of 94 Chinese laboratories, C-peptide inter-measurement-system CV was 20.1-31.6% (worst at the lowest concentration) against a desirable 8.3%; after recalibration all met 8.3%.
- usecases/scoring/V7.json row 6 D7 (14% misclassified used as an anchor) (C216): C216 says 14% applies to insulin-treated adults with 5+ years' diabetes who volunteered in UK centres and would be much lower across all diabetes; 'misclassified' is against a UK algorithm, with 66% labelled T1D but insulin-producing and 34% labelled T2D but deficient. Wording: In 601 UK insulin-treated adults (5+ years, volunteers), 14% were misclassified by UK clinical criteria against urinary C-peptide:creatinine; the authors expect a lower rate across all diabetes.
- usecases/scoring/U1.json row 6 D7 (C231): C231 gives 1.3 million adults with T1D in 2016 (self-report plus insulin use), not 1.8 million (that is a later CDC figure); the 5.8%/90.9%/3.3% split is confirmed. Self-reported T1D without insulin was reclassified to T2D, so the survey cannot size an uncertain-type pool. Wording: NHIS 2016: 5.8% of diagnosed diabetes was type 1 (1.3 million adults), 90.9% type 2; type is self-reported and unvalidated.
- usecases-verified.md row 28 (Jones 2013) (C026): Jones is a narrative review and supports the renal-clearance caveat (about half cleared; poorly validated correction) but has no statement on secretagogue hypoglycaemia in kidney disease. Wording: Jones and Hattersley 2013 (review): about half of C-peptide is cleared by the kidney, so blood values are falsely raised in renal impairment; correction methods are poorly validated.
