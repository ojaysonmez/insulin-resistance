# FT13 full-text review (rows 4-7, 31, 33)

Eight papers read in full text (C025 and C033 from PDF extraction, partial layout). No score change is proposed; the papers mainly produce wording and claim corrections. Measure types are named per paper.

## Verdicts
- **C250** (PMID 32634859, DOI 10.1111/dme.14361, text good): **adds nuance**. Confirmed: 153 nPOD organ donors (111 histological type 1, 42 non-type 1); clinical-only model AUC 0.95 (95% CI 0.91-0.98), full model 0.97 (0.95-1.00), P=0.03. The paper did NOT run a primary analysis against C-peptide (renal failure and storage confound donor sera); C-peptide appears only as a descriptive comparison (median <17 vs 1037 pmol/L, non-overlapping) and in the 5 discordant donors, where C-peptide matched histology. The adult white-European 18-50 y subgroup was only 31 donors (AUC >0.84). The model is not a C-peptide test, so the paper supports C-peptide as a valid reference for insulin deficiency, not C-peptide's added value over alternatives. Quote: "progression to C-peptide deficiency is an appropriate surrogate endpoint" Measure type: C-peptide (random donor serum, descriptive only); non-insulin surrogates (antibodies, genetic risk score, clinical features) are the modelled variables.
- **C273** (PMID 36843403, DOI 10.3343/alm.2023.43.4.345, text good): **adds nuance**. A new ID-LC-MS/MS method (CRM NMIJ 6901-c, total imprecision 1.3-2.2%, LOQ 33.3 pmol/L) was compared with six routine immunoassays on 76 leftover Beijing samples (173-3,612 pmol/L). All R2 >0.98, but four assays (Roche, Abbott, Mindray, Snibe) showed mean positive bias of 49.9, 35.4, 32.2 and 81.7%; Siemens and Beckman agreed better. After recalibration with 5 pooled sera the bias fell to -6.0 to +1.2%. The 24.5% inter-system CV and 5.7% after recalibration quoted in the U3 row 6 reason are NOT in this paper (they presumably come from the separate 94-laboratory survey, PMID 35177563, not checked here). Quote: "Among the six routine assays, the Beckman and Siemens assay showed better agreement with our ID-LC–MS/MS method than the other assays." Measure type: C-peptide (serum, immunoassay vs ID-LC-MS/MS); no clinical decision tested.
- **C283** (PMID 38252849, DOI 10.2337/dc23-1815, text good): **adds nuance**. The outcome is FASTING C-peptide >=250 pmol/L (random value used in <0.5% of measures) 3-10 y after diagnosis in 2,966 US youth; the models PREDICT C-peptide from baseline features, so C-peptide is the reference, not a tested alternative. Clinical-only model AUC 0.95, plus antibodies 0.972, plus GRS 0.979; provider diagnosis AUC 0.887 (sensitivity 79.7% for retained C-peptide); antibodies alone AUC 0.878, PPV 53%. Only internal validation; the authors state the lack of external data and that the threshold is mainly from adult data. Duality of interest: one author has a Randox R&D grant on genetic risk scores. Quote: "A limitation is the lack of other data sets for external validation." Measure type: Fasting C-peptide (with concurrent glucose when low); non-insulin predictors (clinical features, antibodies, GRS).
- **C299** (PMID 39805939, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-80348-8, text good): **adds nuance**. The paper estimates, with ancestry-corrected polygenic risk scores, that 3.6-10.2% (median 5.9%; 95% CI up to 15.2%) of 839 insulin-treated, clinically ambiguous people in Genes and Health (East London) have type 1 diabetes; 1.9-7.8% (median 4.5%) after removing coded T1D. C-peptide and autoantibodies were recorded in only 0.7% and 2.5% of diabetes cases, so the paper contains no evaluation of C-peptide testing. The authors say the PRS was not used to classify individuals. Quote: "Diabetes autoantibodies and measures of beta cell function (serum C-peptide) were rarely recorded." Measure type: Non-insulin surrogate (polygenic risk scores, clinical codes); C-peptide only descriptive (0.7% recorded).
- **C309** (PMID 40587435, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0312065, text good): **adds nuance**. Confirmed but narrow: 8 non-diabetic adults in Uganda (2 of 10 excluded for haemolysis), non-fasting, one platform (Cobas 601), baseline levels not low. Uncentrifuged K2EDTA whole blood at room temperature stayed >=90% of baseline at 24 h (C-peptide 90%, insulin 98%); uncentrifuged plain serum fell to 51% (C-peptide) and 64% (insulin); on ice, uncentrifuged serum insulin fell to 52%. The authors list as limitations the absence of low-concentration samples, a single platform and no interference testing. C-peptide at exactly 90% sits on their own 10% acceptance limit. Manufacturers cite 4 h at 20-25 C. Unit labels in the results look inconsistent (C-peptide given in uU/mL; insulin baseline 864 pmol/L is high for non-diabetic samples); I read this as labelling, not as proof of error. Quote: "Limitations for this study include the absence of samples with low insulin or C-peptide levels" Measure type: Random (non-fasting) insulin and C-peptide in healthy people; no post-load or stimulated samples.
- **C020** (PMID 21270186, DOI 10.2337/dc10-1293, text good): **confirms**. Confirmed in detail: adults with diabetes >=5 y and eGFR >=60; 77 HNF1A/4A MODY, 69 type 1, 54 type 2; urine collected 120 min after the largest meal, in boric acid, mailed, analysed only if received within 72 h. UCPCR >=0.2 nmol/mmol: AUC 0.98, sensitivity 97%, specificity 96%, LR 24; versus type 2 diabetes AUC 0.64 (81% / 44%; poor). 3 of 69 type 1 patients were UCPCR-positive (2 had serum C-peptide >=0.2 nmol/L, 2 were antibody positive). The authors compute a 14% post-test probability of MODY at an assumed 0.7% prevalence (not 20%). They say it cannot be used within 5 y of diagnosis or in renal impairment, and that antibodies are probably better near diagnosis. No comparison with antibodies or a combined model; only parental history was compared. Quote: "UCPCR showed excellent discrimination between HNF1A/HNF4A MODY and type 1 diabetes in patients >5 years after diabetes diagnosis" Measure type: C-peptide, post-meal (2 h) urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio; mixed-meal serum C-peptide at 90 min in 3 patients.
- **C025** (PMID 23289766, DOI 10.1111/pedi.12008, text partial): **adds nuance**. Confirmed: 264 patients <21 y (160 type 1, 41 type 2, 63 MODY), home sample 2 h after the evening meal in boric acid, mailed to Exeter. After 2 y duration (about 177 patients: 120 type 1, 57 non-type 1), UCPCR >=0.7 gives 100% sensitivity (92-100) and 97% specificity (91-99), AUC 0.997; MODY versus type 2 AUC 0.57. Within 2 y specificity is only 33%. Reproducibility CV of UCPCR was 31% and values near the cutoff should be repeated. The home meal was not standardised. Not validated in renal impairment. Probability of non-type 1 diabetes rises from 2.2% to 40.3% if positive (assumed prevalence), <0.004% if negative. 26% of MODY probands had been treated with insulin before diagnosis. NOTE: the n=264 in the row 33 D2 reason is the whole cohort; the >2 y result rests on about 177. Quote: "UCPCR testing has limited use in patients with diabetes duration <2 yr" Measure type: C-peptide, post-meal (2 h, home) urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio.
- **C033** (PMID 25792383, DOI 10.1159/000375410, text partial): **adds nuance**. 27 genetically confirmed MODY (16 GCK) versus 42 type 1 (no type 2 group), single Turkish centre. Urine was collected in hospital 2 h after a standardised lunch (not at home). UCPCR >=0.22: sensitivity 96.3%, specificity 85.7%, AUC 0.93; the paediatric 0.7 cutoff had only 59% sensitivity (91% specificity). Fasting C-peptide 0.62 ng/mL: sensitivity 93%, specificity 90%, AUC 0.967, i.e. at least as good as UCPCR. 6 of 42 type 1 children were UCPCR-positive, all antibody positive. The authors name small sample and unequal groups as limitations. Quote: "The limitations of our study are its small sample size and the unequal distribution of patients in the groups" Measure type: C-peptide, post-meal urinary C-peptide:creatinine ratio (standard lunch, in hospital) and fasting serum C-peptide.

## Claim issues
- usecases-verified.md row 33, caveats column ('cut-offs 0.2-0.28'): The paediatric studies used UCPCR cutoffs of 0.7 nmol/mmol (PMID 23289766, children, after 2 y) and 0.22 nmol/mmol (PMID 25792383); 0.2 is the adult cutoff (PMID 21270186). The range 0.2-0.28 omits 0.7, and the 0.7 cutoff had only 59% sensitivity in the Turkish cohort. Proposed wording: Cut-offs are data-derived and not transferable: 0.7 nmol/mmol in UK children with diabetes >2 y (PMID 23289766, DOI 10.1111/pedi.12008); 0.22 in a small Turkish cohort (PMID 25792383, DOI 10.1159/000375410; 0.7 gave 59% sensitivity there); 0.2 in UK adults >5 y (PMID 21270186, DOI 10.2337/dc10-1293); 0.28 is reported by PMID 32052124 (abstract, not checked in this unit). (C025)
- usecases-verified.md row 33 test column ('Home 2-h post-meal UCPCR') and V9 row 33 D2 reason: Only PMID 23289766 used a home sample (non-standardised meal). PMID 25792383 used a standardised lunch in hospital and included only MODY and type 1 (no type 2). The D2 reason's 'n=264 after 2 years' is the whole cohort; the >2 y result is about 177 patients (120 type 1, 57 non-type 1). Proposed wording: Post-meal (2 h) UCPCR: home sample in UK children (PMID 23289766, 264 patients, about 177 with diabetes >2 y); hospital standard lunch in a Turkish cohort of 27 MODY and 42 type 1 children (PMID 25792383), with no type 2 group. (C033)
- usecases/scoring V9 row 33 D8 reason ('Fasting C-peptide did worse than UCPCR in one cohort (sensitivity 63%)'): Not supported by PMID 25792383: fasting C-peptide (0.62 ng/mL) had 93% sensitivity and 90% specificity, UCPCR 0.22 had 96.3% and 85.7%. The 63% was not found in C025 or C033. Proposed wording: In one small cohort fasting serum C-peptide (cutoff 0.62 ng/mL, AUC 0.967) performed about as well as UCPCR (AUC 0.93) for MODY versus type 1 (PMID 25792383); no study compared UCPCR with antibodies or genetic testing. (C033)
- usecases-verified.md row 7 ('20% PPV' remark) and V2 row 7 D2 reason ('PPV ... 20 percent (project file)'): The adult UCPCR paper gives a post-test probability of 14% for HNF1A/4A MODY if UCPCR >=0.2 nmol/mmol, computed from an assumed 0.7% prevalence and LR 24 (PMID 21270186); the paediatric paper gives 40.3% for non-type 1 diabetes at an assumed 2.2% prevalence (PMID 23289766). A 20% figure is in neither text. These are computed from assumed prevalence, not observed pathway PPVs. Proposed wording: Authors' calculated post-test probabilities (assumed prevalence): about 14% for HNF1A/4A MODY at UCPCR >=0.2 nmol/mmol in adults with diabetes >5 y (PMID 21270186, 0.7% prevalence assumed); 40.3% for non-type 1 diabetes at UCPCR >=0.7 in UK children >2 y (PMID 23289766, 2.2% prevalence assumed). These are not observed pathway yields. (C020)
- usecases/scoring U3 row 6 D4 reason ('inter-system CV 24.5% in one 94-laboratory Chinese survey, 5.7% after recalibration') citing PMID 36843403: These figures are not in PMID 36843403. That paper compares six immunoassays with a new ID-LC-MS/MS method on 76 samples: mean bias +32 to +82% for Roche, Abbott, Mindray and Snibe, Siemens and Beckman closer, -6.0 to +1.2% after recalibration. Proposed wording: Six routine C-peptide immunoassays compared with an ID-LC-MS/MS method on 76 Chinese samples: four read 32-82% higher, two agreed better, and recalibration with five pooled sera reduced the mean difference to -6.0 to +1.2% (PMID 36843403, DOI 10.3343/alm.2023.43.4.345, full text). The 24.5% inter-laboratory CV should be cited to its own source (PMID 35177563, not checked here). (C273)
- usecases/scoring U2 row 6 D8 reason, source entry for PMID 39805939: PMID 39805939 does not evaluate C-peptide; it estimates misclassification with polygenic risk scores (C-peptide recorded in 0.7% of cases). It supports that clinical features cannot reliably separate T1D from T2D in ambiguous South Asian insulin-treated cases, not that C-peptide adds value. Proposed wording: In 839 clinically ambiguous insulin-treated British Pakistani and Bangladeshi adults, polygenic risk scores estimated that 3.6-10.2% (median 5.9%) had type 1 diabetes; C-peptide was recorded in 0.7% and was not evaluated (PMID 39805939, DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-80348-8, full text). (C299)
- usecases/scoring U2 row 6 D8 reason, 'clinical models in youth reached AUC 0.95-0.98 for retained C-peptide' (PMID 38252849): The range merges clinical-only (0.95) and biomarker-augmented (0.972-0.979) models; the outcome is fasting C-peptide in US youth (not random C-peptide in adults); there is no external validation. Proposed wording: In 2,966 US youth, internally validated models predicted fasting C-peptide >=250 pmol/L 3-10 y after diagnosis with AUC 0.95 (clinical features only) to 0.98 (with antibodies and genetic risk score); provider diagnosis AUC 0.887; no external validation (PMID 38252849, DOI 10.2337/dc23-1815, full text). (C283)
- D4 reasons for rows 4, 5, 6 and 31 citing PMID 40587435: The stability result comes from 8 healthy adults on one platform without low-concentration samples; C-peptide in EDTA at 24 h was 90% of baseline, at the authors' own 10% limit. It should not be read as validation for critical hypoglycaemia samples or low C-peptide values. Proposed wording: In 8 healthy non-diabetic adults (Cobas 601), unspun K2EDTA whole blood kept C-peptide at 90% and insulin at 98% of baseline at 24 h at room temperature, while unspun serum fell to 51% and 64%; low-concentration samples were not tested (PMID 40587435, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0312065, full text). (C309)
