### Row 5 D1 (2 to 1)
What I confirmed in `corpus/fulltext/C054.txt`:
- The quote offered ("documented hypoglycemia (venous glucose <54 mg/dL) at time of neuroglycopenic symptoms") is exact, and the three criteria are as FT27 states: history 1 to 3 hours after meals at least 6 to 12 months after surgery, documented glucose below 54 mg/dL with relief, "no hypoglycemia after a prolonged fast of at least 12 hours". Insulin is not one of the criteria.
- "laboratory testing at the time of symptomatic hypoglycemia has been used by those with access to endocrine testing centers to assess β-cell peptide responses. Again, there are no normative data in this situation ..." is exact.
- Inpatient fasting is "reserved for (and is essential for) patients with fasting hypoglycemia, hypoglycemia occurring early after bariatric surgery (e.g., <6 months), or other atypical features, to exclude the rare insulinoma".

Why the 2 to 1 move is not supported:
1. It misapplies clarification 15. Clarification 15 gives D1 = 2 "if it chooses among accepted options or only supports a diagnosis", 3 if the result "determines the decision". The anchor for 1 is "Counselling only". FT27 argues that insulin "neither determines the diagnosis nor is needed to support it". That is an argument against 3, not for 1. A test that is not required but is used to support or refine a diagnosis sits at 2 by the literal rule. S1, U4 and U5 all scored 2 with exactly this reasoning ("the diagnosis rests on documented low glucose"; U4 and U5 already say management starts from glucose alone), so the paper adds no new fact on this point, as FT27's own proposal text concedes ("U4 and U5 already say ...").
2. The review does not exclude insulin from the diagnosis; it does not use it as a criterion and says there are no normative data. It also says insulin is "not fully suppressed at the time of hypoglycemia" in post-RYGB patients (glucose <50 mg/dL with insulin above the lower limit of the assay), and refers to postprandial hyperinsulinaemia throughout. Silence about insulin as a criterion in one expert review is not evidence that insulin does not support the diagnosis. The reader's statement "insulin is not among them" is accurate; the inference "insulin plays no role" is not in the text.
3. FT27 reads one source. The project's own guideline source for the same row, Cryer 2009 (C014, read in FT11; verified here: "The patient with a history suggestive of postprandial hypoglycemia should undergo a mixed-meal test ... conducted over 5 h", and the findings of symptoms plus glucose below 55 mg/dL, insulin of at least 3.0 uU/mL, and C-peptide criteria "after a mixed meal" indicate endogenous hyperinsulinism) puts insulin and C-peptide in the post-meal diagnosis. C089 (FT28) restates this. FT11 proposed D3 from 2 to 3 for row 5 on that basis (not applied, per FT11-12 skeptic file and the rescore log, which shows only row 1 D1 and D3 applied). A D1 downgrade from one paper that is silent, while the guideline text for the same use recommends the test, would be inconsistent with the project's evidence.
4. The "weak case" caveat in FT27 itself ("a fasting-test insulin (a different measure) would keep 2") confirms the reader's reasoning mixes measures. Row 5 is scored for insulin and C-peptide at nadir during symptoms or after a mixed meal (U1, U2, U4 wording), which includes the spontaneous-episode sample.
5. Clarification 29 (D1 at most 1 for research endpoints) does not apply: row 5 names a patient decision (diagnosis of postprandial hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia, and the work-up path).
6. Effect size: D1 2 to 1 lowers UQ by one third of a point on the strength of an expert review's silence. The ranking flag for row 5 is already "weak evidence (D2 of 0 or 1)"; the weakness is correctly sitting in D2 (= 1, U2 over S1 D2 = 2), not in D1.

### Row 5 D8 (2 to 1)
- Anchor 2 is "Useful in a defined subgroup"; 1 is "Marginal". Clarification 19: "3 requires data ... mechanism alone scores at most 2. 'Not determinable' is allowed." There is no clarification that makes absence of added-value data equal to 1. U2's reason says the defined subgroup is where insulinoma, exogenous insulin or sulfonylurea use must be excluded or the picture is atypical, and calls the whole score "low-quality inference from reviews and case reports".
- FT27 says the full text sends atypical cases "to a prolonged inpatient fast and imaging, not to a post-meal insulin". That is accurate for the text (fasting hypoglycaemia, onset under 6 months, atypical features). But it does not remove the subgroup: the fast in that route is itself an insulin/C-peptide test (FT28 C089 and Cryer 2009), and U2's measure type includes insulin and C-peptide at documented hypoglycaemia, not only post-meal. The review does not discuss exogenous insulin or sulfonylurea exclusion at all (zero hits for "sulfonylurea" and "factitious"), so it neither supports nor weakens that part of U2's reason.
- The same batch contradicts itself: FT28 (C077, C089) keeps row 5 D8 at 2 and notes that its glucose-based CGM vs mixed-meal figures "neither raise nor lower" the insulin-specific argument. The main session would be asked to accept 1 from one unit and 2 from another on the same row without either unit reading the other (independence rule).
- Recommendation: keep 2, low confidence; if the user prefers to reflect the doubt, record "partly not determinable" in the reason (U6's suggestion) rather than move the number. If the user does want a change, it should be "not determinable", not 1, because nothing read shows insulin is marginal.
