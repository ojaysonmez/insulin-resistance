# FT23 full-text review (C284, C289, C290, C293, C295, C296, C297)

All seven texts were read in full and extracted cleanly. No score change is proposed. Details are in `usecases/scoring/ft-review/FT23.json`.

## Per paper

- **C284 Clapp 2024 (PMID 38448343, DOI 10.1016/j.soard.2024.01.012), row 11 V20 D7: adds nuance.** The paper is the 2022 ASMBS estimate only: 280,000 procedures (2021: 262,893). It counts procedures including revisions (about 11 percent) and endoscopic procedures. It holds no diabetes share and no 2023 or 2024 total, so those stay at web-excerpt level. Quote: "approximately 1% of all qualified patients undergo surgery".
- **C289 Hsu 2024 (PMID 38902405, DOI 10.1007/s00464-024-10985-7), row 11 V20 D7: adds nuance.** Table 1 gives diabetes at baseline by procedure (SG 28.6%, lap RYGB 37.1%, BPD/DS 37.5%). Summing the table counts gives 109,476 of 343,727, about 31.9% overall (my calculation). Commercial claims only, 2000-2020. This fills the gap V20 noted and sits inside its 28 to 37 percent range. Quote: "Laparoscopic RYGB and BPD/DS patients had a higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus at baseline when compared to SG (37.1% and 37.5% vs. 28.6%, respectively)".
- **C290 Pedersen 2024 (PMID 38950997, DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2024-084808), row 8 V22 D4: confirms.** The trial protocol uses a 2-h MMTT (6 timed samples, research unit, frozen sera) with C-peptide AUC as the primary endpoint. No assay is named, and Box 1 calls the endpoint "fasting" C-peptide, which conflicts with the text. Quote: "The MMTT is the gold standard test to determine residual β-cell function by measurement of stimulated C-peptide."
- **C293 Elemian 2024 (PMID 39219958, DOI 10.7759/cureus.65825), row 21 V15 D2: confirms.** Case report (n=1): glucose 24 mg/dL, insulin <0.4 uU/mL, C-peptide 0.1 ng/mL, IGF-II 842 ng/mL (within the quoted range), IGF-II/IGF-I >32. Case-report level, so D2=1 holds. Quote: "patients with IGF-induced hypoglycemia typically demonstrate depressed levels of serum insulin and C-peptide during hypoglycemia".
- **C295 Chida 2024 (PMID 39390979, DOI 10.3892/ol.2024.14701), no score entry: not relevant.** GIST series (195 patients, 8 with hypoglycaemia, 2 recurrent). It reports no insulin or C-peptide values. IGF-II was a Mayo Clinic RIA, which supports IGF-II being a send-out. Quote: "Eight patients out of 195 patients met this criteria (4.1%)."
- **C296 Astudillo 2024 (PMID 39510599, DOI 10.1136/bmjdrc-2024-004471), row 33 V8 D7: confirms.** The 38 of 680 (5.6%) antibody-negative new-onset T1D figure is exact. The paper itself cites a prevalence as high as 19%, which would move V8's chain to bin 3 (about 14,000 per year, my arithmetic). V8 is already low confidence with that alternative noted. C-peptide is reported for 6 cases only. Quote: "Out of 680 patients with new-onset T1D, 38 (5.6%) were negative for islet autoantibodies (GAD, IA-2 and IAA)".
- **C297 Okazaki-Hada 2025 (PMID 39689018, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgae879), row 21 V16 D4: adds nuance.** Of 145 suspected NICTH sera, 100 had big IGF-II on Western blot. The IGF-II ELISA is research use only. AUC was 0.73 for IGF-II and 0.70 for IGF-II/IGF-I. C-peptide was lower in the NICTH group (0.033 vs 0.123 nmol/L), but insulin and C-peptide were entry assumptions, not tested as classifiers. Quote: "no clinically validated IGF-II assay is available; therefore, the measurement must be performed with an ELISA kit for research use".
