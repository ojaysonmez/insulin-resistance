## Row 6 (score 3)
Random plasma C-peptide is a routine automated immunoassay with no timed protocol. Within-laboratory imprecision is small (intra-laboratory CV 2.3%, inter-laboratory 16.4%, inter-system 24.5% in 94 Chinese laboratories; recalibration with two serum pools cut inter-system CV to 5.7%; PMID 35177563, DOI 10.3343/alm.2022.42.4.428, abstract). A JCTLM-listed ID-LC-MS/MS procedure exists (project files; PMID 38549041, DOI 10.1093/clinchem/hvae034, abstract: antibody-free LC-MS/MS median inter-laboratory imprecision 13.4% for C-peptide). WHO reagent 13/146 exists but materials made from it were not commutable on over half of six immunoassays; frozen human serum pools were (PMID 37141618, DOI 10.1515/cclm-2023-0215, abstract). A 2026 study notes inter-assay variability persists despite harmonization (PMID 42893716, DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2026.122908, abstract). Pre-analytical: in 8 healthy volunteers, EDTA plasma held at least 90% of baseline for 24 h while uncentrifuged room-temperature serum fell to 51% (PMID 40587435, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0312065, abstract; small). Jones and Hattersley note C-peptide is more stable than once thought and a urine C-peptide:creatinine ratio is an option (PMID 23413806, DOI 10.1111/dme.12159, abstract). Validity conditions (insulin-treated, over 3 years from diagnosis, concurrent glucose, renal function) belong to protocol and interpretation and are in `usecases-verified.md` row 6.
Why 3 and not 2: the thresholds are wide (80, 200, 600 pmol/L), so a 20-25% between-assay spread changes classification only near a cutoff, and sampling is simple. A strict reading of "unstandardized" gives 2 (anchor issue below). Evidence is from China and Korea, not US platforms, and from older recalibration designs.

## Row 5 (score 2)
Needs a sample at symptomatic nadir or a timed mixed-meal test, and an insulin assay. Insulin is not standardized: twelve current commercial immunoassays differed from an ID-MS comparator by -298 to +303 pmol/L despite WHO 66/304 traceability claims (PMID 40802520, DOI 10.1515/cclm-2025-0552, abstract; project files full text). Older-generation: among-assay CV 12-66%, median 24% (PMID 17272483, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2006.082214, abstract), and a common reference preparation did not lower it. Analogue detection differs by assay: three categories in PMID 37598742 (DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2023.117516, abstract); one Siemens platform detected five analogues at 92-121% while LC-MS/MS detected only glargine (PMID 39030378, DOI 10.1007/s12020-024-03970-6, abstract). This matters when insulin or analogue use must be excluded. Insulin loss in uncentrifuged serum at room temperature (PMID 40587435, abstract, n=8). No accepted insulin threshold for this use (PMID 30101281, DOI 10.1210/jc.2018-00528, abstract; via project row 5). A standard mixed-meal protocol was not found in the screened records.

## Row 13 (score 2)
HOMA-IR = fasting glucose (mmol/L) x fasting insulin (uU/mL) / 22.5; threshold above 3.0; median entry value 4.6-4.7 (PMID 26886418, DOI 10.1056/nejmoa1506930, abstract and full text via PMC4887756 keyword windows). One fasting draw is easy. However, HOMA-IR for the same sera ranged 0.8-2.0 (normoglycaemia), 1.9-3.2 (prediabetes) and 1.5-2.9 (T2D) across 11 assays, and insulin in heparinized plasma was 15% lower than serum (PMID 18535197, DOI 10.2337/dc08-0097, abstract; older assays). IRIS used central laboratories because insulin is not standardized (project files, skeptic read of the full text). The 3.0 value was chosen as the top quartile of non-diabetic populations, not tied to an assay. Between-kit spread in another study was 8.3-fold on one pool, mostly from two ELISAs (PMID 35622611, DOI 10.1002/jcla.24521, abstract; replicate RSD 1.7-23.2%).

## Anchor issues
1. Row 6: anchors 2 and 3 overlap for a routine automated assay that is widely available but not fully standardized. Chose 3.
2. Row 5: the anchor mixes assay quality and protocol burden. Chose 2.
3. Row 13: ease of single fasting draw against an assay-dependent threshold; no anchor guidance. Chose 2.
4. The anchor does not say whether to score the assay as marketed in the US or as studied; most inter-assay data come from outside the US or older generations.
