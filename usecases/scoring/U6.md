# Unit U6: skeptic review of pilot units U1 to U5 (rows 6, 5, 13)

Date 2026-10-20. Role: skeptic. Machine-readable findings: `usecases/scoring/U6.json`. Coverage and reimbursement out of scope. Tool calls used: about 24.

## Verdict

The three sets of scores are mostly defensible and no two roles differ by more than one point on any dimension (differences of one point: row 13 D5, D6, S; row 6 S). The identifiers I checked (19) all resolve to the stated titles and DOIs, and the IRIS numbers match the source. The main failure is row 6 D3 (and the D1 reasoning): both consensus documents say C-peptide must be measured before insulin discontinuation, which U1 says does not exist. Other weaknesses: row 6 D8 and D2 lean on mechanism and on papers that use C-peptide as the reference standard; row 6 D7 rests on unsourced shares so the bin is not determined; row 13 D8 uses a different outcome and misses the AACE alternative trigger; path-to-viability entries give different pieces per role and need one chosen piece each. Strongest rows by evidence: row 6 (consensus-backed, but evidence for decision impact is reclassification cohorts only). Row 5 and row 13 are weak on D2 and D8 as scored, which fits the pilot expectations. No score was found to be inferred from the status column, but several reasons cite the project file instead of the source read. Tool calls: about 24 (shell, file reads, one web search, two failed Scholar Sidekick calls, one tool load, two writes).

## Findings (ranked by severity)

- **must fix | row 6 | D3**: U1 says nothing makes C-peptide required and scores 3. Both ADA/EASD 2021 (PMC8481000) and ADA Standards 2026 s2 Figure 2.1 (PMC12690183) footnote 4 say verbatim: 'Where a person is insulin-treated, C-peptide must be measured prior to insulin discontinuation to exclude severe insulin deficiency.' That is a required step for one decision (stopping insulin). Under clarification 7 (4 only when the guideline requires the test for the decision) the reason as written is wrong, and the score depends on which decision is scored. Fix: Quote the 'must be measured prior to insulin discontinuation' sentence in the reason. Either score 4 for the insulin-withdrawal decision, or keep 3 and state that the use is scored as classification (where autoantibodies come first) and that the must-statement applies only to withdrawal. Remove 'nothing makes C-peptide required'.

- **should fix | row 6 | D1**: U4 and U5 give 3 because 'autoantibodies come first, so not a required step'. That is true for diagnosing type 1 diabetes, but the row question includes 'can insulin be replaced', where the same consensus says C-peptide must be measured first (see D3 finding). Reasons are therefore incomplete, not necessarily the score. Fix: Keep 3 but state the decision scored. If the decision is insulin withdrawal, D1 4 is arguable (required step); the user should choose, and the same choice must be used for D3 and S.

- **should fix | row 6 | D8**: U2 gives 3 mainly because C-peptide 'measures the construct directly' (mechanism). The cited accuracy studies (PMIDs 32634859, 38252849, 36368478; titles/identifiers verified) show clinical models predict type 1 status; they do not show C-peptide adds beyond antibodies and clinical features. The decision-relevant evidence actually in the sources is in Holt: routine C-peptide at 3 or more years reclassified 11% of adult-onset diabetes (verified in PMC8481000, citing ref 31), and values of 600 pmol/L or more are 'strongly suggestive of type 2 diabetes' with insulin often replaceable. U2 does not cite this. U2's own anchor note admits the 2 vs 3 call is a judgement and its literal reading ('useful in a defined subgroup') is 2. Fix: Cite Holt's 11% reclassification study (get its PMID/DOI) as the added-value evidence and keep 3 at low confidence, or score 2. Do not rely on the AUC studies for D8.

- **should fix | row 6 | D2**: Score 3 (consensus backed by studies) fits the anchor, but three of the five sources U2 cites (PMID 31558459 and the accuracy papers) use C-peptide as the reference standard and say nothing about the decision. The supporting studies were not named. Holt itself says the recommendations 'represent the consensus opinion of the authors' (abstract, verified). Fix: Replace the reference-standard papers with the reclassification cohort behind Holt ref 31 and state that no controlled outcome study of C-peptide-guided changes was found. Keep 3, confidence medium.

- **should fix | row 6 | D7**: The point estimate (about 120,000, bin 4) rests on three unsourced numbers: 'several million insulin-treated adults', 2 to 10 percent of uncertain type, one third tested per year. The state of the point 20 percent above the 100,000 boundary with a 30,000 to 400,000 range means bin 3 versus 4 is not determined by the data. Rubric: estimates must cite a source and a range wider than one bin is low confidence. The CDC figures were checked (web search of the CDC report: 1.8 million adults 'reported both having type 1 diabetes and using insulin', 6.1 percent of diagnosed adults; 3.8 million adults started insulin within a year of diagnosis, 13.3 percent). U1 wrote '1.8 million adults with type 1', which drops the self-report plus insulin-use definition. No US count of insulin-treated adults with uncertain type is given. Fix: Anchor the chain on the CDC insulin-use figures (insulin-treated adults = 1.8 million type 1 plus insulin-treated type 2 from a cited NHIS table), cite the Holt 11 percent reclassification as the share who change class on testing, and mark the score as 3 or 4, low confidence, or 'not determinable at bin level'. Fix the wording of the 1.8 million figure.

- **should fix | row 5 | D3**: Score 2 is supported by clarification 5 (ADA 2026 rec 8.26 names the condition, not the test; verified verbatim in PMC12690172), but U1's stated reason leans on the Endocrine Society 2009 abstract and a paediatric PES text. The Endocrine Society abstract (verified) does recommend 'measure plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate ... during an episode of hypoglycemia' in suspected endogenous hyperinsulinism. That is a recommendation for the measurement in a general adult population, so 3 under a general reading is possible if post-bariatric hypoglycaemia counts as endogenous hyperinsulinism; U1 did not test that and did not quote the passage. The PES text (verified) is about infants and children and describes, not recommends. Fix: Keep 2 but quote the Endocrine Society sentence, say it is general adult hypoglycaemia and not post-bariatric specific, and drop the PES text as support for an adult US score (note only).

- **should fix | row 13 | D8**: U2 uses the IRIS diabetes-prevention paper (PMID 27465265, verified) to argue that benefit is concentrated in people with impaired fasting glucose. That is the diabetes-prevention outcome, not the stroke or myocardial infarction outcome the decision is about, and the abstract is truncated at 'impaired fasting glucose (FPG >100' so the 'or raised HbA1c' part could not be verified. The better-supported point is in AACE R9.4 (verified verbatim): 'In persons with insulin resistance, prediabetes, or T2D and a prior transient ischemic attack or stroke, pioglitazone should be considered', i.e. prediabetes or T2D alone is an accepted trigger, so no insulin measure is required. IRIS enrolled only HOMA-IR above 3.0 (mean HOMA-IR 5.4 at baseline) so no contrast exists. Fix: Base D8 on the AACE alternative-trigger wording and the absence of any within-trial contrast. Drop the diabetes-prevention argument or label it as a different outcome. Keep 1 or record as 'not determinable'.

- **should fix | row 13 | D4**: U3 cites PMID 18535197 for 'HOMA-IR ranged 0.8-2.0, 1.9-3.2, 1.5-2.9 across 11 assays'. The numbers match the abstract, but the abstract gives P=0.842 for the prediabetes group (no significant difference between assays) and P=0.0007 and P<0.0001 for the others. The study used 12 normoglycaemic, 18 prediabetic and 67 type 2 diabetic subjects and older assays. The group most relevant to the row (prediabetes) is the one where assays did not differ significantly. The IRIS statement that laboratories must adopt the IRIS assays or calibrate to them is verified (PMC4887756 discussion) and is the stronger support. Fix: Cite the IRIS discussion sentence as the primary support, report the P values, and state the older-assay limitation. Score 2 stays defensible.

- **should fix | row 6 | D4**: Score 3 relies on the statement that values are used 'far from the cutoffs for most patients'. No distribution of C-peptide values in the target group is cited. ## Anchor rewrites

- **D3**: Row 6: a consensus algorithm with a 'must' footnote for one decision and a conditional step for another; anchor 4 does not say whose decision is scored. Proposed: D3 is scored for the decision named in the use case. 4 only when a guideline, consensus or label says the test must be done before that decision; if the use case spans several decisions, score the primary one and note any decision where the test is required.
- **D3**: Row 5: a general (non-specific) guideline recommends the measurement in a broader population; clarification 5 covers drugs, not general-population recommendations. Proposed: A recommendation for the measurement in a broader population that includes this one scores 2 unless the guideline text covers the use-case population explicitly; then 3.
- **D1**: Rows 6, 13: anchor 4 (required step) versus 3 (determines classification) does not say which decision is meant. Proposed: Name the decision. 4 if a guideline requires the result before that decision; 3 if the result determines the decision but other tests can substitute; 2 if it chooses among accepted options or only supports a diagnosis.
- **D5**: Row 13, row 6: anchors 1 and 2 describe protocol complexity, but scorers also penalised unstandardised assays (that is D4) and unclear ownership. Proposed: D5 scores who orders the test, where, and how many steps. Do not score assay standardisation (D4) or consequences of a wrong result (D6). Score 0 only if no specialty owns the decision.
- **D6**: Anchors mix patient burden of the test and consequence of a false result; U5 took the worse of the two. Proposed: D6 = the lower of (test burden score) and (false-result consequence score); write both numbers in the reason. 3 = low (reversible, minor), 2 = moderate (reversible harm needing treatment), 1 = notable, 0 = serious.
- **S**: Marginal harm of not having the measurement versus harm of the disease; units split 2 versus 3 on rows 6 and 13. Proposed: S = the harm that results when the decision is made without the measurement, assuming the usual default action (for example continue insulin, or give pioglitazone on the other guideline trigger). Do not score the severity of the disease. If the default action is itself harmful, say so.
- **D8**: Anchor 2 versus 3 is ambiguous when the whole use is already a subgroup (row 6). Proposed: Score against the alternatives within the stated population. 3 requires data or guideline text showing the measurement changes the answer given the alternatives (for example reclassification rate); mechanism alone scores at most 2. 'Not determinable' is allowed.
- **D4**: Anchor does not say whether to score the assay as marketed in the US or as studied (non-US or older data). Proposed: Score US-marketed assays. Where only non-US or older-generation data exist, score from them, cap confidence at medium, and say so. Separate protocol burden (D5) from assay standardisation (D4).
- **D7**: Chronic-condition shares are unsourced assumptions that decide the bin. Proposed: Each share in the chain needs a source or is labelled an assumption with its own range. If the range of the bin-deciding share crosses a bin boundary, record 'bin 3 or 4, low confidence' (or not determinable) instead of a single bin.
- **setting**: The tag may follow the test or the decision. Proposed: Tag by who orders the test in the guideline or evidence pathway for the decision.
