## Evidence base for the assay facts
- Insulin: ADA workgroup, 12 commercial methods: within-assay CV 3.7-39.0%, among-assay CV 12-66% (median 24%); a common reference preparation did not harmonize; 9 of 10 assays cross-reacted over 40% with des(64,65) proinsulin (PMID 17272483, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2006.082214, abstract; older assay generation, may not apply to current platforms). No insulin reference measurement procedure exists (project file `analysis/clinical-chemist.md`, from the full text of PMID 38549041).
- HOMA-IR across 7 European laboratories: inter-laboratory CV about 25%, driven by insulin (PMID 28660493, DOI 10.1007/s00125-017-4340-1, abstract; 10 individuals).
- C-peptide: five immunoassays, all traceable to WHO 84/510, mean bias up to 36.6% between two platforms (PMID 36630986, DOI 10.1055/a-1998-6889, abstract). Ten manufacturers disagreed despite a common WHO calibrator; recalibration with matrix-appropriate serum reduced it (PMID 39803820, DOI 10.1515/cclm-2024-1260, abstract). LC-MS/MS inter-laboratory imprecision 13.4% (single) for C-peptide, 22.2% for insulin (PMID 38549041, DOI 10.1093/clinchem/hvae034, abstract).
- Pre-analytics: unspun at room temperature, C-peptide fell 46% (plasma) and 74% (serum) at 48 h; insulin fell 23% (plasma) and 80% (serum) at 72 h (PMID 37409980, DOI 10.1515/cclm-2023-0339, abstract; one Roche platform, 10 adults).

## Per-row reasoning
- **Row 14 (2).** Fasting insulin is routine, but the assays are unstandardized and the guideline says so. OGTT-derived estimates add a timed protocol. Sources above plus EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 (PMIDs 38851997, 38852583, 38869512, project files). Confidence capped at medium: the largest harmonization study is the 2007 ADA workgroup.
- **Row 9 (2).** C-peptide is available everywhere but platforms disagree; the stimulated option needs a timed MMTT. No type 3c cutoff to standardize against.
- **Row 28 (2, low).** Serum C-peptide as row 9. UCPCR is less available, the evidence is one small cohort, and renal clearance is a confounder the assay cannot fix (PMID 30794834, DOI 10.1016/j.diabres.2019.02.015, abstract; PMIDs 36465621 and 23413806 from project files). Alternative: 1 if UCPCR is the measure.
- **Row 12 (1).** The limiting component is the anti-insulin-receptor antibody: only a research bridge assay at NIH was found (PMID 36869714, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgad125, abstract). Genetics is a send-out. Fasting insulin >30 uU/mL has no named assay (PMID 35463863, project files).
- **Row 21 (2).** Insulin and C-peptide are routine. Cutoffs depend on the assay: in a prolonged-fast study, the old 36 pmol/L cutoff for non-specific assays did not carry over to a specific ELISA (PMID 15026285, DOI 10.1016/j.cccn.2003.12.023, abstract; older assay generation). IGF-II confirmation is a specialised test (big IGF-II by Western blot and ELISA in PMID 39689018, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgae879, abstract). Alternative 3 if only insulin and C-peptide count.
- **Row 46 (3).** One fasting serum C-peptide at a low cutoff (0.3 ng/mL) is a standard draw; platform bias is largest at higher concentrations. Functional sensitivity per US platform at 0.3 ng/mL was not checked. Criteria: PMID 24843620, DOI 10.1111/jdi.12024 (abstract here; criteria text read by the project skeptic). Alternative 2 if the stimulated test is required.
- **Row 35 (2, low).** Scored for (b) only. No pregnancy-specific insulin assay or reference interval was found in the screened records.
