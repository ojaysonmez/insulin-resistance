## Reasoning
- **Row 1.** D1 4: biochemical proof of endogenous hyperinsulinism is the defining step before localisation and surgery, and nothing substitutes. Thresholds come second-hand via ENETS and the Endocrine Society text was only read at abstract level, so 3 is the alternative. D6 is 2 on both burden (prolonged fast) and consequence (mistimed sample, antibodies, renal failure, secretagogues). S 3: missed insulinoma gives recurrent severe hypoglycaemia, curable by surgery.
- **Row 2.** D1 3: the pattern separates exogenous from endogenous hyperinsulinism, but drug screen, antibodies and history partly substitute. D6 2: assays that miss analogues and renal failure can mislead. S 3: the default (insulinoma work-up) is itself harmful and the exposure continues.
- **Row 24.** D1 3 on guideline Rec 1.4 (low-quality evidence). S 3 for neurological injury risk in a child. D6 confidence low.
- **Row 29.** D1 4 because the FDA label makes peak MMTT C-peptide of at least 0.2 pmol/mL an eligibility criterion (project text, skeptic-verified). ADA 2026 does not endorse the Stage 3 use and predates the supplement, so 4 depends on the label being current. D5 1: timed MMTT, autoantibodies and an infusion course at a specialist centre. S 2: marginal harm is a lost surrogate benefit or drug exposure in an ineligible child.
- **Row 22.** D1 3: determines the territory to resect, but other localisation can substitute. D5 and D6 1: interventional procedure at specialist centres, arterial catheter, a wrong territory means a wrong resection. S 3.
- **Row 23.** D1 2: helps avoid excluding insulinoma on a negative fast, small subgroup, limited single-centre data. D6 2: induced hypoglycaemia, and false positives in healthy people. S 3, confidence low.
- **Row 33.** D1 2: supports a non-T1D diagnosis, and autoantibodies or genetics can substitute. D6 2: burden is low (4) but a false-high value may lead to insulin withdrawal and ketoacidosis risk (2). S 2: overtreatment and missed targeted therapy.
