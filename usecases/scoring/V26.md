Setting: specialist outpatient (child psychiatry prescribes and orders monitoring in the CAMESA pathway).

### D2 = 1 (evidence for the decision impact)
The decision is the work-up step: OGTT and specialist consultation when fasting insulin is above the assay's upper limit. The CAMESA management paper grades that step 3 (expert opinion). The monitoring guideline's STRONG 1A for olanzapine grades evidence that the side effect occurs, and its authors state there is no evidence on the outcome of monitoring (project files, PMC excerpts). New search: the studies found are association data (1-year cohort of 117 youths, PMID 27209421: baseline insulin and HOMA-IR associated with later weight gain, abstract) and one small study (Kakko 2022, PMID 34431750, n = 55: HOMA-IR cut point 1.98 with sensitivity and specificity 83%; reference standard not stated in the abstract). No study of decision impact was found in the screened records.

Sources: PMID 21804853 (no DOI in record; PMC excerpts, project files); PMID 21804854 and PMID 23115501 (same paper, no DOI; excerpts, project files); PMID 34431750, https://doi.org/10.1080/08039488.2021.1968489 (abstract); PMID 27209421, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-016-0866-x (abstract).

### D3 = 1 (guideline status, US)
CAMESA is a Canadian national guideline (clarification 6: counts as 1 unless a US or international body adopts it). US documents found use glucose and lipids, not insulin: AACAP 2011 practice parameter for atypical antipsychotics in children and adolescents (baseline fasting blood glucose, fasting lipid profile; search excerpt), AACAP 2013 schizophrenia parameter (fasting glucose and lipids; search excerpt), and the NCQA HEDIS APM measure (blood glucose and cholesterol testing, ages 1-17; search excerpt). The AHRQ 2021 PQMP toolkit reproduces CAMESA's recommendation ("fasting glucose, fasting insulin, and fasting lipid profile"; "Fasting insulin is not recommended for youth on aripiprazole") as one of eight guidelines, which I read as a listing, not an adoption (anchor issue).

### D7 = 4 (US patients per year)
Chain: Olfson 2015 (PMID 26132724, https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2015.0500, abstract) reports 2010 antipsychotic use of 0.11% (ages 1-6), 0.80% (7-12), 1.19% (13-18) and 0.84% (19-24). With about 24-26 million US residents per six-year band (assumption, Census 2010 approximate): ages 1-18 about 530,000. Minus aripiprazole users, for whom CAMESA does not recommend insulin (assumed 25-45%), and allowing a decline since 2010 (assumed 0-40%; Bushnell 2023, PMID 37406239, https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2022.01625, title only, reports a Medicaid decrease 2008-2016): about 175,000 to 400,000, point estimate about 300,000. Assumption: everyone on an SGA is evaluated at least once a year. The whole range is in bin 4 (100,000 to 1,000,000). Broader reading with ages 19-24: add about 218,000, still bin 4.

### D8 = 1 (added value over alternatives)
Alternatives within SGA-treated youth: BMI and waist, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids, and an OGTT done directly. No study showing that fasting insulin changes the answer given these alternatives was found in the screened records. Kakko 2022 (n = 55, abstract) reports that HOMA-IR rose earlier than single laboratory values. In adults on antipsychotics (Mitchell 2019, PMID 31513598, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0210674, abstract, n = 798), insulin and HOMA-IR were measured but the proposed diabetes screening algorithm is HbA1c 5.7% or more followed by fasting glucose and OGTT; insulin's performance is not reported in the abstract. Under clarification 19 this is marginal at most; low confidence because the paediatric evidence is one small study.

## Path to viability
A cohort of SGA-treated children and youth testing whether fasting insulin adds to fasting glucose, HbA1c and BMI for detecting dysglycaemia on OGTT (incremental detection or reclassification), then a US guideline position. Type evidence; effort medium.
