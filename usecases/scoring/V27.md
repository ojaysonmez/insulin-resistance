Setting tag: **routine care**. The test is scheduled monitoring ordered by the prescriber, who may be a paediatrician, a family physician or a child psychiatrist. It is not a specialist protocol. See the anchor issues.

Path to viability: not part of this unit's task (list left empty).

## Limits of the test in this population
- Antipsychotics change insulin sensitivity within weeks. CAMESA reports olanzapine raised insulin by 2.71 µIU/mL. A single value is therefore a moving target, and within-person variation of about 27% makes a crossing of 100 pmol/L on one sample partly noise.
- Fasting insulin in obese youth largely tracks adiposity. The Endocrine Society 2017 Rec 1.7 (PMID 28359099, recorded in `usecases-verified.md` row 16) recommends against insulin measurement in the paediatric obesity work-up. The population and purpose differ, but the direction conflicts.
- Puberty raises insulin resistance, and the 100 pmol/L cutoff is not age- or puberty-specific (inference, [recall]).
- CAMESA's "STRONG 1A" for olanzapine grades evidence that the side effect occurs, not evidence that measuring helps.

## Anchor issues
1. D1: the result triggers work-up, not a diagnosis or treatment. I read it as "selects a diagnostic step" (2). I did not apply the national-criteria reading of 3, because the insulin result is a referral trigger, not a diagnostic criterion.
2. D5: whether anchor 4 applies depends on whether the existing draw is fasting. I chose 3 for the US, with 4 as the alternative.
3. Setting tag: the prescriber may be a specialist or a generalist. I tagged routine care.
4. S: the boundary between 1 and 2 for delayed detection of dysglycaemia is unclear. I chose 1, low confidence.
