## Common inputs

- US population: about 340 million (Census Bureau Vintage 2025 estimates exist at census.gov; the number was not read from the page) [recall for the value].
- Insulinoma incidence: 4 per million person-years in Olmsted County, Minnesota, stable over the last two decades of a 60-year study (Mayo Clin Proc 1991, PMID 1677058, DOI 10.1016/s0025-6196(12)62083-7; journal-page search excerpt only, abstract not fetched; 7.6% had MEN1, 5.8% malignant). Other sources quote 1 to 4 per million (PMC3574879) and 1 to 32 per million (StatPearls NBK544299); both are secondary and were seen as excerpts only. Applied to the US: about 1,360 new insulinomas per year (range 340 to 10,000 with the quoted spread).
- Yield of insulinoma among adults evaluated for non-diabetic hypoglycaemia: 12 of 92 (13%) in a tertiary cohort with supervised fast (PMID 42810406, DOI 10.1055/a-2961-6659, abstract); 4 of 70 (6%) in an endocrinology department where factitious hypoglycaemia was 16% (PMID 36117266, DOI 10.1002/edm2.375, abstract). Both are non-US single centres.
- Finnish EMS data: 1,082 non-diabetic hypoglycaemia patients per 100,000 per year, mostly alcohol and hypothermia (PMID 29391050, DOI 10.1186/s13049-018-0480-7, abstract). Scaled to the US this is millions of encounters; it is not a count of patients evaluated for hyperinsulinism and was not used in the chain.
- Not found in the screened records: any US national count of adults worked up for non-diabetic hypoglycaemia (HCUP or NEDS counts for E16.x were searched and not found; one ED surveillance paper, JAMA Intern Med 2018, covers diabetes only).

## Row 1 (D7 = 3, D3 = 3)

Measure type: insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin and BOHB at spontaneous or fasting-induced hypoglycaemia (secretion measures; not an insulin-resistance index).

D7. Situation: non-diabetic adults with documented hypoglycaemia who get the hyperinsulinism panel. Chain: 1,360 insulinomas per year divided by a yield of 6 to 13% gives about 10,000 to 23,000 adults evaluated per year; point estimate 15,000. Range 5,000 to over 100,000, so low confidence. Alternative bin 4 if community yield is below about 1.4% (more than 100,000 evaluated). The yield shares are assumptions (single non-US centres, tertiary referral yield is higher than community yield, which pushes the true count above the estimate).

D3. Endocrine Society 2009 guideline (Cryer), PMID 19088155, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-1410. Read level: full text of the summary of recommendations, recommendation 2.1, evidence and remarks via the publisher page extract. Rec 1.1 (1, high quality): evaluate only when Whipple's triad is documented. Rec 2.1 (1, moderate quality): in a seemingly well person with unexplained hypoglycaemia, measure plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin and BOHB and screen for oral hypoglycaemic agents during spontaneous hypoglycaemia, then give glucagon; thresholds insulin at least 3 uU/mL, C-peptide at least 0.6 ng/mL, proinsulin at least 5 pmol/L, glucose below 55 mg/dL. ENETS 2016 (PMID 26742109, DOI 10.1159/000443171, PMC4849884) restates these thresholds (full text, keyword windows). Graded recommendation, not a mandatory step: 3. The guideline is 17 years old; two PubMed searches for a newer version returned 0 records (the search tool handles natural-language strings poorly, so this is a weak negative).

## Row 2 (D7 = 2, D3 = 3)

Measure type: insulin with C-peptide (and proinsulin) at hypoglycaemia.

D7. Factitious hypoglycaemia was 11 of 70 (16%; 6 insulin, 5 sulfonylurea) in the endocrinology department series and 5 of 104 hyperinsulinaemic adults (4.8%) in a single Italian centre (PMID 38766827, DOI 10.2174/0118715303322628240509094537, abstract). Applied to the row 1 base: about 750 to 2,400; point about 1,500. Low confidence because both the shares and the base are unsourced for the US. Alternative bin 3 if the base is over 100,000 or suspicion is raised in more than about 65% of the base.

D3. The same Endocrine Society Rec 2.1 panel; Table 3 lists the exogenous insulin pattern (insulin well above 3 uU/mL, C-peptide below 0.2 nmol/L, BOHB 2.7 mmol/L or lower, positive glucagon response) as an interpretation, so the population is covered explicitly: 3. PES 2015 (children; PMID 25957977, DOI 10.1016/j.jpeds.2015.03.057, PMC11891912, full text keyword windows): high insulin with low C-peptide suggests exogenous insulin, and some assays do not measure analogues, so low insulin and low C-peptide may not exclude it.

## Row 24 (D7 = 1, D3 = 3)

Measure type: insulin and C-peptide at hypoglycaemia (secretion).

D7. No US figure found. Evidence found: 23 paediatric insulinomas treated at one academic paediatric hospital over 2006 to 2024, median age 13, 22% with MEN1 family history (PMID 42149805, DOI 10.1159/000552145, abstract; country not stated in the abstract); congenital HI birth prevalence 1 in 17,394 in Western Australia (PMID 42396735, DOI 10.1515/jpem-2026-0122, abstract; infants, not counted). Estimate about 150 per year (range 30 to 600): assumed 20 to 60 paediatric-referral centres at about 1 per year plus a similar number of other acquired hyperinsulinism cases. Both multipliers are assumptions. Note (not scored): if the situation is read as every child over 2 years who gets a hypoglycaemia critical sample, the count is probably in the thousands (unsourced), bin 2 or 3.

D3. International Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Hyperinsulinism (PMID 37454648, DOI 10.1159/000531766, PMC11124746; full text, keyword windows): Grade 1 (moderate quality) recommendation to diagnose HI from insulin and fuel/hormone measurements at hypoglycaemia (insulin by high-sensitivity assay); Grade 1 (low quality) recommendation to screen all children whose HI occurs after age 2 years for insulinoma, with genetic investigation for MEN1. Age group named explicitly: 3. C-peptide is not named in the sections I read; the recommendation names insulin.

## Row 29 (D7 = 3, D3 = 4)

Measure type: mixed-meal-stimulated peak C-peptide (alternative method allowed by the label when no MMTT is available).

D7. Chain: about 18,291 new T1D cases per year under 20 years (SEARCH-based estimate for 2014 to 2015, seen on diabetesresearch.org and a Montana state bulletin, not on the CDC page itself). The SEARCH 2002 to 2018 paper (PMID 36868256, DOI 10.1016/s2213-8587(23)00025-6; author manuscript full text on CDC Stacks) gives 22.2 per 100,000 in 2017 to 2018 and an increase of about 2% per year; new cases in the registry in 2017 to 2018 by age were 179 (0-4), 341 (5-9), 445 (10-14) and 194 (15-19). Prorating ages 8-9 and 15-17 gives about 60% aged 8 to 17 (my derivation). Times 18,300 to 19,000 gives about 11,000 to 11,400; times 90% with at least one autoantibody (assumption, range 85 to 95%) gives about 9,900 to 10,300. Range 8,500 to 12,500 crosses 10,000. I score bin 3 and record bin 2 as the alternative (flat incidence or antibody-positive share under about 91%). Note: one Diabetes in America excerpt (NBK619992) says 18,169 youth are diagnosed each year, but that number is the 17-year SEARCH registry total, so I did not use it as an annual figure. The eight-week window is not a reduction: all children newly diagnosed are in it at diagnosis.

D3. FDA Tzield label (BLA 761183, label date 2026-06-15), read through Amass RegulatoryCore (record AMRC_RFkMLpxCLNsmIK0gPCnOjfh5kLn; sections 1 and 2.1 in full). Section 2.1: select pediatric patients aged 8 to 17 years recently diagnosed (within 8 weeks) with Stage 3 T1D based on confirmation of at least one positive islet autoantibody and peak C-peptide of 0.2 pmol/mL or more on an MMTT. Indication is under accelerated approval on reduced C-peptide decline; continued approval may depend on confirmatory trials. Score 4 (label requires confirming the measurement for the decision). EMA Teizeild covers Stage 2 only (record snippet). ADA Standards 2026 position not re-read (the project row records that it predates the supplement and does not endorse this indication).

## Row 22 (D7 = 1, D3 = 2)

Measure type: hepatic venous insulin gradient after selective arterial calcium injection.

D7. About 1,360 insulinomas per year times imaging-negative share of under 5 to 10% (ENETS 2016 full text) gives 70 to 140. A surgical series reports 14 of 80 tumours not visualised (17.5%) (PMID 31369096, DOI 10.1210/jc.2019-01204, abstract), giving up to about 240. MEN1 insulinomas (7.6%) overlap. ## Row 23 (D7 = 2, D3 = 2)

Measure type: post-load (OGTT) insulin with glucose; the Endocrine Society mixed-meal test is a related but different stimulus.

D7. Postprandial-only presentation: 6% of 237 surgically confirmed insulinomas at Mayo, 1987 to 2007 (PMID 19141587, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-2031, abstract), and 'up to 18%' in the single-centre series ENETS cites. That is about 80 to 245 insulinomas per year. Number tested: assuming insulinoma is about 5% (range 1 to 10%) of adults tested for postprandial hypoglycaemia after a negative fast gives about 1,600 (800 to 5,000). The 5% is an assumption; the nearest sourced yields are 13% in a fasting cohort and a retrospective 5-hour OGTT inpatient series with 57 insulinomas and 28 reactive hypoglycaemia (PMID 42221390, DOI 10.1016/j.aed.2026.01.015, abstract). Alternative bin 1 if yield is above about 8%; bin 3 if below about 0.8%.

D3. ENETS 2016 full text describes an altered insulin pattern during an OGTT as a way to diagnose some insulinomas when the fast is negative (single-centre study); no graded recommendation. Endocrine Society 2009 gives a mixed-meal protocol in its remarks. Score 2.

## Row 33 (D7 = 2, D3 = 2)

Measure type: C-peptide (urinary C-peptide/creatinine ratio, or fasting or post-meal serum C-peptide).

D7. 314,000 US youth with diagnosed T1D (CDC National Diabetes Statistics Report, web page). Autoantibody-negative T1D label: 38 of 680 (5.6%) new-onset T1D cases in one US children's hospital (PMID 39510599, DOI 10.1136/bmjdrc-2024-004471, abstract). MODY prevalence at least 1.2% of the US pediatric diabetes population, mostly misdiagnosed and treated with insulin (SEARCH, PMID 23771925, DOI 10.1210/jc.2013-1279, abstract); 2.5% monogenic in UK clinics (PMID 27271189, DOI 10.2337/dc16-0645, abstract); 1.2% in Sweden (PMID 31704690, DOI 10.2337/dc19-0747, abstract). Prevalent antibody-negative pool about 15,000 (314,000 x 0.88 with duration over 2 years x 5.6%); about 1,000 per year newly enter it. Share evaluated or retested per year: 25% (assumption, range 10 to 100%) gives about 5,000 (2,500 to 16,600). Alternative bin 3. The 0.88 duration share and the 25% are my assumptions.

D3. ADA Standards of Care 2026 section 2 (Diabetes Care 2026;49 Suppl 1:S27; DOI 10.2337/dc26-S002; PMC12690183; web-page text, keyword windows; PMID not looked up): Figure 2.1 footnotes say a C-peptide test is only indicated in people on insulin, a random sample within 5 hours of eating can replace a stimulation test, results of 600 pmol/L or more settle the question, and 200 to 600 pmol/L is usually T1D or MODY. This is a broader population (all ages) and a figure, not a numbered recommendation: 2. ISPAD 2022 monogenic guideline (PMID 36537518, DOI 10.1111/pedi.13426, PMC10107883; full text, keyword search): detectable C-peptide in blood or urine over a long remission phase is a clue that T1D may be wrong; no recommendation to measure it. ISPAD 2022 classification chapter (PMID 36537527, DOI 10.1111/pedi.13454): full text not obtained. UK national criteria are not counted.
