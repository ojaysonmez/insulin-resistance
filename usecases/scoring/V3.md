## Shared analytical evidence
- C-peptide: 15 labs, 9 methods, within-run CV <2% to >10%, between-run <2% to >18%; calibrating with mass-spectrometry-valued serum removed between-lab differences (PMID 18420730, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2007.101287, abstract). A reference measurement system exists (NIDDK-led, PMID 28646033, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2016.269274, abstract). WHO ISR 13/146-derived materials were not commutable on over half of six immunoassays; frozen human serum pools were (PMID 37141618, DOI 10.1515/cclm-2023-0215, abstract; Chinese study, recent assays).
- Insulin: inter-laboratory LC-MS/MS imprecision 22.2% (single) and 15.3% (averaged); C-peptide 13.4% and 10.8% (PMID 38549041, DOI 10.1093/clinchem/hvae034, abstract; full text read in the project). Chinese 94-laboratory data: recalibration lowered C-peptide inter-system CV from 24.5% to 5.7% but insulin only 22.4% to 17.2% (PMID 35177563, DOI 10.3343/alm.2022.42.4.428, via analysis/clinical-chemist.md, abstract level). Older-generation and non-US data; they may not describe current US platforms.
- Pre-analytics: in plain serum tubes left uncentrifuged at room temperature C-peptide fell to 51% and insulin to 62% of baseline within 24 h; K2EDTA tubes stayed above 90% (PMID 40587435, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0312065, abstract; 8 healthy adults). Insulin and C-peptide are among analytes affected by delay, tube and temperature (PMID 22285385, DOI 10.1016/j.clinbiochem.2012.01.012, abstract).
- Sulfonylurea screen: qualitative LC-MS/MS assays exist, one used in 19 emergency-department hypoglycaemia cases (PMID 26790752, DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2016.01.007, abstract; PMID 22767115, DOI 10.1007/978-1-61779-934-1_13, abstract). A send-out/specialised test in most settings is my assumption.

## Reasoning per row
- **31 (score 3).** One untimed C-peptide with glucose: a standard test. Method disagreement is largest at higher concentrations (PMID 18420730), and the decision-relevant range is low. Medium confidence because no US-platform data were retrieved.
- **7 (2).** The evidence base is post-meal UCPCR: urine collected in boric acid 120 min after the largest meal, cutoff >=0.2 nmol/mmol (PMID 21270186, DOI 10.2337/dc10-1293; paediatric >=0.7, PMID 23289766, DOI 10.1111/pedi.12008; >=0.22, PMID 25792383, DOI 10.1159/000375410; >=0.28, PMID 32052124, DOI 10.1007/s00431-020-03606-7; all abstracts). Cutoffs differ by study; no standardized urine assay. Blood C-peptide alone would be a 3.
- **3 (2).** Insulin and C-peptide are routine but timed to hypoglycaemia; the insulin method is the least standardized; the discriminating drug screen is specialised.
- **32 (2).** Fasting C-peptide is routine; the scheme in PMID 17130187 (DOI 10.2337/dc06-0749, abstract) defines function by peak C-peptide after glucagon >=1.5 ng/mL, a timed test, assessed at 12 months or later.
- **47 (3).** Fasting C-peptide <0.6 ng/mL (PMID 38184802, DOI 10.1111/jdi.14121, abstract). No named assay; transfer to US methods unverified.
- **49 (2, low).** OGTT with C-peptide at several time points; derived indices are cohort-specific and not reportable laboratory results (project file, row 49: Ismail 2022 PMID 35998266, Galderisi 2025 PMID 39992257; not re-read here).
- **4 (2).** Critical-sample timing, small volume, high-sensitivity insulin needed, guideline notes insulin may be undetectable in hyperinsulinism (PMID 37454648, DOI 10.1159/000531766, abstract; criteria from project full-text read).
- **10 (2).** Igls criteria: C-peptide relative to baseline, basal and stimulated, with sensitive assays for low values (PMID 29528967, DOI 10.1097/tp.0000000000002158, abstract; numeric statements from the project's skeptic full-text read of the 2018 report).
