## Row 6

D3 = 3. Read: ADA/EASD consensus 2021 (PMID 34590174, DOI 10.1007/s00125-021-05568-3, PMC8481000) and ADA Standards of Care 2026 section 2 (PMID 41358893, DOI 10.2337/dc26-S002, PMC12690183), both by passage search of the C-peptide text (full text, passages). Both say C-peptide testing is indicated only in insulin-treated people, a random sample with concurrent glucose within 5 hours of eating can replace a stimulation test, 600 pmol/L or more needs no repeat, below 80 pmol/L needs no repeat, and not within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency. The ADA 2026 text reproduces the Holt algorithm (Figure 2.1), so it is a specific-population recommendation inside a consensus algorithm. Not 4: autoantibodies are the first test and nothing makes C-peptide required or label-mandated.

D7 = 4, low confidence. Source figures: CDC National Diabetes Statistics Report (web page dated 2026-01-21): 28.8 million diagnosed adults, 1.8 million adults with type 1. MMWR 2016 NHIS (PMID 29596402, DOI 10.15585/mmwr.mm6712a2, abstract via search excerpt): 5.8 percent type 1, 90.9 percent type 2, 3.3 percent other. The number of insulin-treated adults with uncertain type is not published in the records I screened. Estimate: insulin-treated adults of several million, 2 to 10 percent of uncertain type, about a third needing a test in a given year, giving about 120,000 (30,000 to 400,000). The point sits at the 100,000 boundary and the range spans bins 3 and 4. The wider reading (all adults newly diagnosed each year, about a million, bin 5) was rejected because Holt restricts C-peptide to insulin-treated people more than 3 years from diagnosis.

## Row 5

D3 = 2. ADA Standards 2026 section 8 (PMID 41358882, DOI 10.2337/dc26-S008, PMC12690172; full text, passage search): rec 8.26 says that when post-metabolic-surgery hypoglycaemia is suspected, clinical evaluation should exclude other disorders contributing to hypoglycaemia, and management should include education, medical nutrition therapy and medication; CGM is advised for safety. No insulin or C-peptide test is named. The same passage search for insulin level, C-peptide and mixed-meal found nothing in section 8. PES 2015 (PMID 25957977, DOI 10.1016/j.jpeds.2015.03.057, PMC11891912; full text, passage search; paediatric) states that postprandial hypoglycaemia after fundoplication or gastric bypass is often preceded by exaggerated insulin responses detectable on oral glucose or mixed-meal tests: a description, not a recommendation. Endocrine Society 2009 (PMID 19088155, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-1410; abstract only) covers adult hypoglycaemia work-up in general. No post-bariatric-specific guideline naming an insulin test was found in the screened records. Score 2 because insulin at documented hypoglycaemia sits inside accepted general work-up; 1 was the alternative.

D7 = 3, low confidence. ASMBS estimate for 2023 (web page 2025-04-14): sleeve 157,254, gastric bypass 63,132, total 270,089. The systematic review "Prevalence of post-bariatric hypoglycemia in the United States" (PMID 41966914, DOI 10.1016/j.soard.2026.03.013; abstract only, truncated in retrieval) reports from its Level A studies that 12 percent of gastric bypass and 5 percent of sleeve patients need medical attention, and models about 160,000 patients needing medical management and over 30,000 receiving critical care (a prevalent pool, not an annual count). New cases a year: 0.05 x 157,000 + 0.12 x 63,000 is about 15,500. Adding a share of the prevalent pool needing work-up again gives a point of about 30,000, range 8,000 to 160,000, spanning bins 3 and 4. The abstract also says published incidence ranges from 0.1 to 75 percent.

## Row 13

D3 = 2. ADA Standards 2026 section 3 (DOI 10.2337/dc26-S003, Crossref-verified; PMID not retrieved here; PMC12690170; full text web page, passage search): rec 3.13 "pioglitazone may be considered" in people with history of stroke and evidence of insulin resistance and prediabetes (grade C per the retrieved text); the text describes IRIS entry as HOMA-IR 3.0 or more. AACE 2022 (PMID 35963508, DOI 10.1016/j.eprac.2022.08.002, PMC10200071; full text XML, passage search): rec 9.4 says pioglitazone "should be considered" in persons with insulin resistance, prediabetes or T2D and prior TIA or stroke; a search for HOMA in the guideline text found only an unrelated match. IRIS (PMID 26886418, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1506930, PMC4887756; full text): entry HOMA-IR above 3.0, sample at least 14 days after the event, patients with diabetes and certain heart failure excluded. Neither guideline recommends measuring insulin; the drug is recommended in a group defined partly by insulin resistance. Score for the measurement: 2.

D7 = 4, medium confidence. CDC Stroke Facts (web page 2025-07-10): more than 795,000 strokes a year, about 610,000 new, 87 percent ischaemic (about 690,000). TIA at least 240,000 a year (Yale Medicine news page 2025-04-08 citing an AHA/ASA statement; secondary source). About 930,000 events; assuming 70 percent without diabetes gives about 650,000; assuming 25 to 50 percent with HOMA-IR above 3.0 and applying exclusions gives about 100,000 to 300,000. Point 200,000, range 100,000 to 400,000. Even the widest reading (every non-diabetic stroke or TIA patient, about 650,000) is in bin 4, so the bin is stable though the number is not.
