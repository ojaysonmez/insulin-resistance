# V10 report: D4 analytical feasibility (rows 1, 2, 24, 29, 22, 23, 33)

Role: clinical chemist. Read level: abstracts for new papers (title only for PMID 39086570); project files for guidelines. Tool calls: 16 before this report's final handback.

## Row 1: D4 = 2 (confidence medium, origin both)
Insulin and C-peptide are routine immunoassays in US labs, but they are not standardized across platforms: insulin has no common reference system and analogue/proinsulin cross-reactivity differs; the antibody-free LC-MS/MS comparison across three labs still gave 13% (C-peptide) and 22% (insulin) median imprecision. Proinsulin and BOHB are send-out or less common, and the ENETS thresholds are tied to assays of unstated generation. Measure type: fasting/at-hypoglycaemia insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin. Protocol burden left to D5. Alternative 3 if only insulin and C-peptide are counted.
Sources: PMID 26742109 DOI 10.1159/000443171 (ENETS 2016, read via project files, full text per usecases-verified.md); PMID 38549041 DOI 10.1093/clinchem/hvae034 abstract; PMID 19088155 DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-1410 abstract (via project files)

## Row 2: D4 = 2 (confidence medium, origin both)
The pattern needs an insulin assay that detects the analogue in use; Atellica recovered 92-121% of five analogues while LC-MS/MS detected only glargine at 10 ng/mL, and one infant case went undetected until the local assay changed. Reviews advise two assays with different cross-reactivity, so the test is available but needs assay-specific knowledge or send-out. Evidence is from non-US-specific platforms and spiked samples (CV and recovery, not clinical sensitivity); confidence capped at medium. Measure type: insulin plus C-peptide at hypoglycaemia.
Sources: PMID 39030378 DOI 10.1007/s12020-024-03970-6 abstract; PMID 36965853 DOI 10.1016/j.ando.2023.03.015 abstract; PMID 36733550 DOI 10.7759/cureus.33224 abstract; PMID 25957977 DOI 10.1016/j.jpeds.2015.03.057 full text (via project files); PMID 39086570 DOI 10.4103/ijem.ijem_56_24 title only

## Row 29: D4 = 2 (confidence medium, origin both)
Stimulated C-peptide is a common lab assay and the FDA label sets 0.2 pmol/mL peak after an MMTT, with an alternative method allowed. C-peptide immunoassays are not harmonized (LC-MS/MS interlab 10.8-13.4% imprecision; the threshold sits at the low range where immunoassay lower limits matter), and the MMTT is a timed protocol not in routine care. Assay generation behind the PROTECT cut-off was not checked. Measure type: post-load/mixed-meal C-peptide.
Sources: FDA Tzield label (supplement 2026-06-15) s2.1, 14.2 via project files (usecases-verified.md row 29); PMID 38549041 DOI 10.1093/clinchem/hvae034 abstract

## Row 22: D4 = 1 (confidence low, origin both)
Insulin is measured by ordinary immunoassay, but the test as a whole (arterial calcium stimulation with timed hepatic venous samples, rapid turnaround) is done at few expert centres and published series are small (13 and 42 patients); gradient ratios are within-patient so assay bias largely cancels. Scored 1 as specialised; alternative 2 if only the assay is scored. Measure type: stimulated regional insulin (not fasting).
Sources: PMID 26742109 DOI 10.1159/000443171 (ENETS 2016, read via project files, full text per usecases-verified.md); PMID 26577133 DOI 10.1007/s40618-015-0406-4 abstract; PMID 25873534 DOI 10.1016/j.surg.2015.03.004 abstract

## Row 23: D4 = 2 (confidence low, origin both)
Insulin and glucose by standard assays during a prolonged OGTT; no validated insulin cut-off, assay not specified in the guideline passage, and standardization as in row 1. Single-centre data only. Measure type: post-load insulin. Not found in the screened records: a dedicated analytical study.
Sources: PMID 26742109 DOI 10.1159/000443171 (ENETS 2016, read via project files, full text per usecases-verified.md)

## Row 33: D4 = 1 (confidence low, origin both)
UCPCR is a specialised test: the cohorts are UK/European or single-centre, the home urine sample with preservative was not confirmed as a US-orderable assay in the screened records, and units/cut-offs (0.2 to 0.7 nmol/mmol) differ across studies. Serum fasting or post-meal C-peptide would score 2. Not found in the screened records: US availability or stability data for UCPCR. Measure type: urinary C-peptide (post-meal) or serum C-peptide.
Sources: PMID 23289766 DOI 10.1111/pedi.12008 abstract; PMID 25792383 DOI 10.1159/000375410 abstract
