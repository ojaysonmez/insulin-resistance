## D3: guideline and regulatory status

Read levels refer to this session. "Keyword windows" means the text was searched with `tools/pmc_text.py --grep` and the matching passages were read, not every page.

### Row 31 (D3 = 4, high)
- ADA Standards of Care 2026 section 2 (PMID 41358893, DOI 10.2337/dc26-s002, PMC12690183): full text, web-page version, keyword windows. Figure 2.1 note 4: a C-peptide test is only indicated in people receiving insulin; a random sample with concurrent glucose within 5 hours of eating can replace a stimulation test; at 600 pmol/L (1.8 ng/mL) or above the circumstances do not matter; below that with glucose under 4 mmol/L repeat; below 80 pmol/L no repeat needed; where a person is insulin treated, C-peptide must be measured before insulin discontinuation; do not test within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency.
- Reasoning: the row's primary decision is "stop or avoid insulin"; the guideline says the test must be done first (anchors 7 and 13: 4). The reclassification part alone is "indicated" (3).
- Measure type: random non-fasting C-peptide. Not evaluated here: Holt 2021 and Jones 2013, which the project file records as read; I did not re-read them.

### Row 7 (D3 = 2, medium)
- ADA 2026 (same record): rec 2.29b recommends genetic testing for MODY in children and young adults without typical type 1 or type 2 features and with a multigenerational family history. It does not name C-peptide. Figure 2.1 names monogenic features (A1C below 7.5% at diagnosis, a parent with diabetes, specific syndromic features, a calculator probability above 5%) and C-peptide only for insulin-treated people.
- ISPAD 2022 monogenic diabetes (PMID 36537518, DOI 10.1111/pedi.13426, PMC10107883): full text, keyword windows; the PMC XML has stripped lists. Preserved beta-cell function with detectable C-peptide (blood or urine) over an extended remission phase of at least 5 years is listed as one reason to doubt a type 1 label.
- Monogenic diabetes precision diagnostics, systematic review and expert opinion (PMID 37794142, DOI 10.1038/s43856-023-00369-8, PMC10550998): full text, keyword windows; yields of testing in antibody-negative, C-peptide-positive groups (4% in a UK group under 30; 24% yield in a UK screened pathway). This is expert opinion, not a guideline.
- ANZ consensus 2025 (PMID 40640995, DOI 10.5694/mja2.52717): not re-read; non-US national (anchor 6: 1 for the US unless adopted).
- Reasoning: C-peptide is an option or one suspicion feature, not a recommended step: 2. The existing status "established as a triage component" in `usecases-verified.md` was not used to set the score.

### Row 3 (D3 = 3, medium)
- Endocrine Society adult hypoglycaemia guideline 2009 (PMID 19088155, DOI 10.1210/jc.2008-1410): abstract only; no open full text found (no PMC record). Abstract: in adults suspected of endogenous hyperinsulinism, measure plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate and circulating oral hypoglycaemic agents during an episode of hypoglycaemia.
- Reasoning: the recommendation names insulin and C-peptide with the oral-agent screen in one panel, so it covers the sulfonylurea case in a specific population (3). The 2023 Endocrine Society diabetes-hypoglycaemia guideline (PMID 36477488) is about prevention in people with diabetes; I saw only its abstract and it does not address this test.
- Project-file note kept: insulin and C-peptide show the hyperinsulinaemic mechanism but not the cause; the drug screen discriminates.

### Row 32 (D3 = 2, medium)
- ADA 2026 (same record): names ketosis-prone diabetes, says the insulin requirement is generally transient, advises no C-peptide within 2 weeks of a hyperglycaemic emergency, and says C-peptide is indicated in insulin-treated people. It does not recommend C-peptide for insulin withdrawal in ketosis-prone diabetes specifically.
- Reasoning: a recommendation for a broader insulin-treated group that includes this one scores 2 (anchor 14).

### Row 47 (D3 = 1, high)
- Japan Diabetes Society SPIDDM criteria 2023 (PMID 38184802, DOI 10.1111/jdi.14121, PMC10804889): full text, the whole article (about 9,800 characters). Criterion 3: fasting serum C-peptide below 0.6 ng/mL at the last observed time point; the discussion says this defines progression to an insulin-dependent state. Diabetol Int copy (PMID 38264233) not read.
- ADA 2026: slowly progressive adult-onset autoimmune diabetes is classed under type 1 and the term LADA is acceptable; no C-peptide criterion is given for confirming insulin dependence.
- Reasoning: national criteria from outside the US count as 1 for the US unless adopted (anchor 6). In Japan the same document would score 4.

### Row 49 (D3 = 1, medium)
- ADA 2026: recs 2.6 and 2.7 (autoantibody screening), 2.8b (people with multiple confirmed islet autoantibodies are referred to a specialised centre for metabolic staging, education and consideration of prevention trials or approved treatments such as teplizumab). Stage definitions use autoantibodies and glycaemia; C-peptide is not named for individual progression risk.
- FDA label for TZIELD (teplizumab-mzwv), BLA 761183, approved 2022-11-17 (indications section as returned by Amass RegulatoryCore; the label itself was not opened): delay onset of Stage 3 in people aged 1 year and older with Stage 2 T1D; second indication, delay decline in endogenous insulin production in children aged 8-17 recently diagnosed with Stage 3, approved under accelerated approval on reduced C-peptide decline. The first indication does not require C-peptide. The second uses C-peptide as a trial surrogate, which is a different use from this row.
- Measure type: post-load OGTT glucose plus C-peptide, which the project did not evaluate as a predictor.

### Row 4 (D3 = 3, high)
- International guidelines for the diagnosis and management of hyperinsulinism 2023 (PMID 37454648, DOI 10.1159/000531766, PMC11124746): full text (XML, headings and keyword windows). Grade 1 recommendation: make a specific diagnosis from measurements at the time of hypoglycaemia of beta-hydroxybutyrate, free fatty acids and hormones (insulin, growth hormone, cortisol) and the glycaemic response to glucagon; insulin should be measured with a high-sensitivity assay; the glucagon response may be diagnostic when insulin is low; neonates tested before 72 hours should be retested after 72 hours.
- PES 2015 (PMID 25957977, DOI 10.1016/j.jpeds.2015.03.057, PMC11891912): excerpts only this session; project file records full text read.
- Reasoning: recommended in this population, but insulin is one of several markers and the diagnosis can rest on the others (anchor 7: 3).

### Row 10 (D3 = 3, medium)
- Igls consensus report (IPITA/EPITA), PMID 29528967 DOI 10.1097/tp.0000000000002158 (Transplantation) and PMID 29453879 DOI 10.1111/tri.13138 (Transpl Int): the same paper in two journals. Full text of the Transpl Int copy (PMC5867272) read via keyword windows. Optimal and good graft function are defined partly by C-peptide relative to the pretransplant value; the text says levels of C-peptide should be measured before transplantation in order to determine posttransplant graft function.
- Igls symposium report 2021 (PMID 34061967, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgab386): abstract. Igls 2.0 not found.
- Reasoning: international consensus recommending the measurement in this population: 3. See anchor issue 2.

## D7: United States patients per year

Common US sources used:
- CDC National Diabetes Statistics Report (updated 2026-09-16) and earlier editions: 40.1 million with diabetes (2023); 1.8 million adults with type 1 diabetes using insulin; 3.8 million adults started insulin within a year of diagnosis; 1.2 million new adult cases in 2021; SEARCH youth incidence 2017-2018: 18,169 type 1 and 5,293 type 2. Web excerpts only.
- SEARCH trends 2002-2018: PMID 36868256, DOI 10.1016/s2213-8587(23)00025-6, excerpt/abstract only.

### Row 31 (D7 = 3, low; bin 3 or 4)
Chain: pool 3.8 million (insulin started within a year of diagnosis; a lower bound for insulin-treated adults) x 1-5% (point 2%, assumption) = 76,000 (38,000-190,000). The bin-deciding share is an assumption and the range crosses 100,000. Cross-checks: about 37,000 incident adult type 1 cases per year (PMID 29115947, DOI 10.1186/s12916-017-0958-6; excerpt only); up to 40% of adults with new type 1 diabetes misdiagnosed (ADA 2026; PMID 37273894, DOI 10.1016/j.lanepe.2023.100661; excerpt only). Narrowest population applied (anchor 2); the broader reading, all insulin-treated adults, is several million and is a note only. Searches: PubMed 'misclassification ...' returned 0 and 1 hits; no US count of C-peptide testing for classification was found in the screened records.

### Row 7 (D7 = 3, low; bin 2 or 3)
Chain: SEARCH youth 23,462 new per year; of these, antibody-negative type 1 phenotype (5-10% of type 1) plus non-obese or family-history type 2 give about 1,000-2,500; adults aged 20-34 assumed 40% of the CDC 2012 count of 371,000 new cases aged 20-44 (about 150,000) x 5-10% non-obese and antibody-negative (assumption) = 7,500-15,000. Point about 12,000 (3,000-40,000). ProDiGY (PMID 34362814, DOI 10.2337/dc21-0491; title and excerpt only) found 2.8% MODY among youth with presumed type 2 diabetes, so confirmed MODY is far fewer than the number in whom the question arises. The adult share and the antibody-negative non-obese share have no direct source.

### Row 3 (D7 = 2, low; bin 2 or 3)
Chain: 5-10 million sulfonylurea users (assumption; no source retrieved) x 5.75 hypoglycaemia ED visits or admissions per 1,000 person-years on sulfonylurea-only regimens (PMID 31922562, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.19099; abstract/excerpt) = 29,000-58,000 events; x 5-20% worked up for an unclear mechanism (assumption) = 1,500-12,000, point about 4,000. Sentinel (PMID 28682851, DOI 10.1097/ede.0000000000000709) reports 19-22 serious hypoglycaemia events per 1,000 person-years for new glyburide or glipizide users, which would raise the event count. Geller 2014 (PMID 24615164, DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.136): 97,648 insulin-related hypoglycaemia ED visits per year; context only, not sulfonylurea. No national sulfonylurea-specific count was found in the screened records. The situation is everyone worked up for an unclear hypoglycaemia mechanism (anchor 4), not confirmed sulfonylurea cases.

### Row 32 (D7 = 3, medium)
2014 adult DKA events: 184,255 (168,185 admissions plus 16,070 ED visits), 70.6% in type 1 diabetes, so about 54,000 events not coded type 1 (PMID 32161050, DOI 10.2337/dc19-2449; excerpt only). Ketosis-prone type 2 diabetes was 35% of adults with DKA or ketosis at onset (PMID 37888894, DOI 10.1002/dmrr.3743; abstract). Estimate about 40,000 (13,000 if only new-onset events x 35%; 80,000 allowing for rising DKA rates since 2014). All within bin 3. The counts are events; the type split is coded type. MMWR trend paper: PMID 29596400, DOI 10.15585/mmwr.mm6712a3 (excerpt only).

### Row 47 (D7 = 3, low; bin 2 or 3)
1.2 million new adult cases per year x LADA 2-12% (PMID 32847960, DOI 10.2337/dbi20-0017, excerpt; one older report gives 10.2% in African-Americans, PMID 16306343, DOI 10.2337/diabetes.54.suppl_2.s68; no US-wide figure found) = 24,000-144,000 arising; x 10-25% recognised by antibody testing (assumption; US testing is low-volume per provider and rising: 28,206 non-diabetic people tested in one dataset in one year, PMID 42138126, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgag200, abstract) gives 2,400-36,000 identified per year; the pool followed for several years raises the number in whom the progression question arises. Point about 20,000 (5,000-70,000).

### Row 49 (D7 = 2, low; bin 2 or 3)
Identified stage 1 or 2 people followed in a year, not the unidentified pool. TrialNet: more than 250,000 relatives screened, about 2.5% with multiple autoantibodies (PMID 35316839, DOI 10.2337/dbi20-0054; excerpt), 135,914 children and 99,795 adults in a later analysis (PMID 40694568, DOI 10.2337/dc25-0192; abstract: multiple autoantibodies in 2.8% of children and 0.83% of adults). Modelled prevalence if all were screened: about 350,000 stage 1 and 70,000 stage 2 in children (PMID 40555704, DOI 10.1111/dom.16522; abstract and web excerpt). Point about 5,000 (1,500-20,000) from assumed 1,000-5,000 new identified per year plus 1,500-10,000 under monitoring. If general-population screening is adopted this changes by orders of magnitude (note only). I found no count of patients treated with teplizumab.

### Row 4 (D7 = 2, low)
Congenital hyperinsulinism is about 1 in 25,000-50,000 births (NORD; ScienceDirect topic page gives 80-120 new US cases per year), but the situation is persistent hypoglycaemia in which a critical sample is taken (anchor 4). Manitoba: 58 infants evaluated for hypoglycaemia lasting beyond 72 hours over 5 years, 52 with hyperinsulinism (PMID 31239816, DOI 10.1093/pch/pxy166; abstract and excerpt); with about 16,500 births per year assumed, about 7 per 10,000 births; US births 3,628,934 in 2024 (NCHS Data Brief) gives about 2,500 per year. Older children add some. Range 1,000-10,000.

### Row 10 (D7 = 2, low)
New pancreas transplants: 915 in 2023 (PMID 39947803, DOI 10.1016/j.ajt.2025.01.021; abstract) and 821 adult transplants in 2024 (OPTN/SRTR 2024 report, PMID 42276696, DOI 10.1016/j.ajt.2026.05.720; excerpt only). Islet transplant and TPIAT counts were not found. Prevalent functioning grafts assumed 8,000-10,000 (not sourced). Share with a C-peptide-based graft classification in a year assumed 50% of new recipients and 10-30% of the pool: point about 2,000 (600-5,000). Routine pancreas follow-up may use glucose and HbA1c only, which would lower the number.
