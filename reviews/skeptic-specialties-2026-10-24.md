### 1.8 Pioglitazone-depression (PMID 26602230) and TRIO-BD (PMID 35120288)
Pioglitazone: full text read (PMC4978223, PubMed full-text tool; tables not in the output). Confirmed: aim 2 was a priori ("In an a prior manner ... 2) ... comparing treatment outcomes ... between IR and insulin sensitive (IS) participants"); overall HDRS decline 24% versus 23%, t(29) = -1.22, P = 0.23; "The active IR group declined in HDRS-21 scores an average of 7.15 points more than that of the placebo IR group"; IR defined by any of FPG ≥100 mg/dL, 2-h OGTT ≥140 mg/dL, FPI ≥15 mIU/L, or HOMA-IR >3.8 (MIX). Additional caveats the psychiatry report missed, all from the text:
- "No significant changes were found in FPI, FPG, OGTT or HOMA for either treatment group from baseline to final assessment", so pioglitazone did not measurably change the insulin or glucose measures; the insulin-sensitisation mechanism is not supported within the trial.
- Cell degrees of freedom (t(8), t(8), t(5), t(5)) imply about 9, 9, 6 and 6 participants, about 30 of the 37 completers; the remainder is unexplained in the text.
- The paper set a multiplicity-adjusted threshold of P = 0.006; no P value or interaction test for the 7.15-point contrast appears in the text.
- Baseline HDRS and OGTT differed between arms; metabolic data were multiply imputed.

TRIO-BD: abstract (PubMed) and registry record NCT02519543 (API, excerpts). Confirmed: published primary is converters versus non-converters (10 of 20 on metformin, 1 of 25 on placebo); registered primary: "We will assess whether the effect of metformin on improvement in MADRS scores at week 14 is mediated by conversion of IR to insulin sensitivity (determined using ... HOMA-IR)"; entry "HOMA-IR ≥ 1.8". Additional: the registry aimed to enrol 110 and records 50 actual enrolment (45 randomised in the paper), so the trial was well short of its planned size. The outcome-reporting concern is fair but rests on the current registry version; the version history was not checked by anyone.

### 1.9 Insulin clearance caveats in cirrhosis and CKD
Read level: abstracts of 8077319, 20489042, 27150853 (liver) and 26823551, 24476695 (kidney), re-read by me.
- **Holland-Fischer 2010 (20489042) is misreported in hepatology S3.** The abstract says "Insulin clearance, growth hormone, cortisol, and glucagon levels were unchanged" after TIPS. Fasting insulin did rise (123 to 193 pmol/L), the C-peptide-to-insulin ratio fell 21%, and clamp sensitivity was unchanged; the authors attribute the change to "shunt-related decreased insulin exposure to the liver cells". So "reduced hepatic insulin clearance raises fasting insulin" is the authors' interpretation of the shunt, while the measured insulin clearance did not change. The sentence must say that.
- Letiexhe 1993 (8077319): cirrhosis had 40% lower insulin clearance (correlated with shunt size, r = 0.75) **and** markedly lower minimal-model insulin sensitivity (SI 2.82 versus 5.86), 60% higher secretion; n = 9 per group. So cirrhotic hyperinsulinaemia is partly true insulin resistance. Hepatology's "fasting insulin and HOMA-IR ... are not interpretable in cirrhosis" overstates; "mix reduced clearance with reduced sensitivity" is supported.
- Miyaaki 2016 (27150853): NAFLD n = 37; an OGTT-derived hepatic insulin clearance index was lower with severe fibrosis (P = 0.013) and HOMA-β higher; HOMA-IR is not reported in the abstract. Supports a direction only.
- de Boer 2016 (26823551): n = 59 nondiabetic CKD versus 39 controls; sensitivity 3.9 versus 5.0 and clearance 876 versus 998 mL/min, r = 0.72 between them; adjustment for lifestyle and body composition "partially attenuated" both; "Among participants with CKD, eGFR did not significantly correlate with insulin sensitivity or clearance". Nephrology S-3 omits the attenuation and the within-CKD null, which weaken a dose-response reading.
- Jia 2014 (24476695): 495 men with eGFR under 60; "All ISIs provided satisfactory (weighted κ over 0.6)"; OGTT-derived better than fasting; "Neither HEGC nor ISIs independently predicted mortality". Confirmed.

The hepatology and nephrology reports propose two separate sentences at the same insertion point. They should be merged into one sentence scoped to FASTING and CPEP (M6).
