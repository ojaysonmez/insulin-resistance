### Per-dossier summary

- **Row 1 (insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, BOHB at hypoglycaemia; secretion measure).** Quotes and numbers I could check hold (Mayo 237 cases, 90% / 70%, Olmsted 4 per million, 7.6% MEN1, 5.8% malignant). Measure-type scoping is correct. Pathway: Step A (re-analysis of stored critical-sample and fast aliquots on current assays) is realistic and small. Step B (6-10 US centres, 40-70 insulinomas, 4-5 years) is feasible but has no sponsor and gains only +0.03 to +0.06. "Established" here means guideline-endorsed on expert evidence, not demonstrated to change outcomes; the table label should say so.
- **Row 6 (random C-peptide with concurrent glucose, insulin-treated adults over 3 years from diagnosis).** Identifiers verified. Evidence is one UK uncontrolled cohort plus consensus. Pathway: Step A (cross-platform check at 80, 200, 600 pmol/L against ID-LC-MS/MS) is realistic and cheap; Step B (cluster stepped-wedge, 1,500-2,500) is plausible but expensive; a single-arm cohort with matched EHR controls is the realistic version. Weakest point: D4 3 rests on Chinese 94-laboratory data (CV 24.5%) and no US platform data; I would not raise it.
- **Row 7 (serum C-peptide or post-meal UCPCR before MODY gene panel).** Not verified by me beyond Shepherd and Holt. UCPCR cannot separate MODY from T2D (AUC 0.64 and 0.57, as the dossier reports) and US UCPCR availability is unverified, so the pathway is the least realistic of the four: it needs a CLIA UCPCR, a 1,000-1,500 person study with full gene-panel reference, and it competes with falling direct-sequencing cost (dossier's own 40% no-added-yield estimate).
- **Row 31 (C-peptide-guided insulin withdrawal).** Identifiers verified per dossier; I re-verified 34590174 and 41358893. Stage A (3-5 site prospective cohort, step-down at 600 pmol/L or more, DKA endpoint, about 150 withdrawn patients; rule of three gives 2%) is arithmetically sound. Stage B randomised trial is not worth taking forward. Searches (a) and (b) were not screened, so "no prospective withdrawal study" is "not found".

## Part C. Confidence proposal (`reviews/confidence-rederivation-2026-10-23.md`)

**Bottom line 1, keep 80-85%: justified, but not for the stated reasons.**
- Weak support in the proposal: "nothing found contradicts it" is an absence argument at abstract level, and the tier-C placement of rows 13, 14, 35 and 45 is partly circular (the project's own scoring penalises IR rows on D2 by construction; row 13 is 0.492, only 0.008 below tier B).
- Better support: Endocrine Society 2017 Rec 1.7 against measuring insulin in paediatric obesity (grade 1; project row 16), ADA and AACE wording that names no assay, and the narrowing of the claim to fasting and surrogate measures (a narrower claim is easier to hold than the old general one). Main residual risk is "established" as a definitional word; "routine" excludes the post-stroke pioglitazone subgroup.
- Verdict: keep 80-85%. No evidence in today's dossiers bears on it, since all four are secretion or classification uses.

**Bottom line 2, lowering from 45-50% to 35-45%: direction justified, magnitude and form not.**
- Justified: the existing text already says DIETFITS (INS-30, weight-loss outcome) is post-load, so the scoped claim loses its only prespecified interaction test. What remains is small or narrow nulls (WHI, Hu, NHANES) and conflicting TyG results. The proposal's own sub-figures (fasting insulin or HOMA-IR 40-45%, TyG 25-35%) pool to roughly 33-40%, so the proposed upper bound 45% is above what its own parts support.
- Problems: (a) a pooled number over a "mixed set" is hard to interpret; report two figures in the deliverable (fasting insulin or HOMA-IR about 40%, range 35-45; TyG about 30%, range 25-35) and drop the pooled figure. (b) A 5-10 point change in a judgement figure is within noise (the steelman skeptic said so for the earlier move); the change is warranted only because of the scope change, so say that. (c) The proposal's own caveat (absence of a positive is not a shown null) means the figure depends on the reading of "shown"; define it in the text.
- If adopted, the same figure appears in `README.md` line 14, `summary-detailed.md` lines 23 and 116, `merged-conclusion.md` Bottom line 2 and its scope note, `DECISIONS.md` D-08 and `HANDOFF.md` line 46; update all together and log in `CHANGELOG.md`.

## Claims that survived scrutiny

- Row 1: Whipple's triad gate and the analyte panel at documented hypoglycaemia are recommended ("we recommend ... measure plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, beta-hydroxybutyrate, and circulating oral hypoglycemic agents during an episode", PMID 19088155 abstract); sensitivity above 90% and specificity above 70% in operated insulinoma cases (PMID 19141587, case-series caveat stands); 4 per million person-years (PMID 1677058, 8 local cases).
- Row 6/31: a random C-peptide with concurrent glucose within 5 h of eating, beyond 3 years from diagnosis, is recommended where type is uncertain; "always" before insulin discontinuation (Holt text, verified). Reclassification 6.8% with 1.5% off insulin, one UK centre, uncontrolled (PMID 33131101, abstract verified).
- No controlled study of measurement-guided management exists in any of the four rows (consistent across dossiers; row 31 search was incomplete).
- Arithmetic of the four composites and the sample-size and rule-of-three statements.

## Final verdict on the overall case for clinical utility

- For fasting insulin, HOMA-IR and TyG as routine measures: no established utility; the case is weak and the verdict is stable (80-85% holds).
- For the secretion and classification uses: the case is real but rests on consensus and expert-opinion evidence plus mechanism, not on outcome demonstration. None of the four has a controlled study showing that measurement-guided care beats usual care. Row 1 is the strongest (graded guideline, no alternative test); rows 6 and 31 are strong on consensus and cheap to improve; row 7 is the weakest.

## Pathways worth taking forward (ranked)

1. **Rows 6 and 31 combined: random C-peptide in insulin-treated adults of uncertain type.** Step 1: US cross-platform harmonisation at 80, 200 and 600 pmol/L against an ID-LC-MS/MS reference (small, 9-12 months). Step 2: a prospective multi-site cohort with a pre-specified step-down rule and DKA and HbA1c endpoints, with matched untested controls from the EHR. Why first: consensus text already requires the test before stopping insulin, yield data exist, the test is cheap and routine, and the missing evidence (safety of withdrawal, assay agreement) is concrete. Before anything else, fix the D1/D3/S inconsistencies in problem 1, because tier A or B for row 31 depends on them.
2. **Row 1: insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, BOHB at hypoglycaemia.** Step A only (re-analysis of stored aliquots on current US assays, linked to final diagnosis) plus a request to the Endocrine Society for assay-specific cut-offs and an analogue and mass-spectrometry caveat. Already viable; the gain is robustness (+0.03 to +0.06), not viability. Step B (multicentre prospective accuracy study) only if funded.
3. **Row 7: C-peptide or UCPCR triage before a MODY panel, conditional.** First trace or drop the "PPV 20%", add the Holt "C-peptide above 200 pmol/L" gate to the evidence, then run a head-to-head of antibodies plus age and BMI against the same plus C-peptide or UCPCR in a US multiethnic cohort with gene panels in everyone. Only worth it if US UCPCR availability is confirmed or the study is restricted to serum C-peptide, and if direct sequencing costs do not make triage moot.

Row 31 Stage B (randomised comparison) and row 1 Step B as a standalone project are not recommended.
