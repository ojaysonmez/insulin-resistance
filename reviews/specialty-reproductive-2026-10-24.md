Reviewer: specialty subagent (reproductive and obstetric medicine). Scope: claims in `merged-conclusion.md` that touch PCOS, fertility, pregnancy, gestational diabetes (GDM) and postpartum follow-up, plus rows 15, 35, 38 and 45 of `usecases-verified.md` and the reproductive items in `reviews/scope-audit.md`. ## 3. Missed evidence (sourced)

1. **PCOS guideline: practice points are "evidence not sought"** (PMID 37580314, Table 1, full text). See C2. It changes how much weight the "don't test" practice points can carry: they record a judgement about assays, not an evidence review of testing.

2. **PCOS guideline: metformin and preconception decisions are made without insulin** (PMID 37580314, full text). Metformin 4.3.1 (EBR, conditional, very low certainty) by BMI 25 or more "for anthropometric and metabolic outcomes including insulin resistance, glucose, and lipid profiles" (insulin resistance appears as an outcome, not a selector); 4.4.4 metabolic-risk groups; 1.9.11 and 4.10.7 OGTT when planning pregnancy or seeking fertility treatment, at the first antenatal visit if not done before, and at 24-28 weeks; 5.7.5.1 adjunct metformin in IVF with GnRH agonist long protocol to reduce OHSS, with no insulin criterion. This closes the most likely clinical uses of an IR test in PCOS: metformin choice, preconception risk, IVF adjuncts.

3. **ADA 2026 s15 on metformin prevention in insulin-resistant pregnancy** (PMID 41358885, summariser excerpt, not verbatim-checked): "A meta-analysis of 11 RCTs demonstrated that metformin treatment in pregnancy does not reduce the risk of GDM in high-risk individuals with obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome, or preexisting insulin resistance (143)." The cited reference 143 was not identified. If correct, it removes the obvious action that an early-pregnancy IR result would trigger. Against it: Yu 2023 (PMID 37248332, abstract; 15 RCTs plus 5 cohorts, 3911 women, Chinese databases included) reports RR 0.59 (0.43-0.80), with larger effects in Asian and PCOS subgroups. The two syntheses disagree; neither stratifies by a measured IR value in the abstract. Separately, PCOS guideline 4.11.1 (EBR) says metformin in pregnant women with PCOS "has not been shown to prevent" a list of outcomes (the list was stripped in the PMC text).

4. **Early treatment of glucose-defined GDM is itself uncertain** (TOBOGM, PMID 37144983, abstract; ADA 2026 s15 excerpts). Immediate treatment of GDM diagnosed by OGTT before 20 weeks reduced a composite neonatal outcome (24.9% vs 30.5%, adjusted risk difference -5.6 points, -10.1 to -1.2) with no difference in pregnancy hypertension or neonatal lean mass. ADA 2026 says benefits "are not universal", notes about one third of controls regressed by 24-28 weeks, and notes possible excess small-for-gestational-age infants in treated women with lower OGTT values. If treating early glucose-defined GDM is still debated, a fasting HOMA-IR or TyG prediction of later GDM has no established intervention to trigger. This is an argument from the decision pathway, not a test of IR measurement.

5. **GDM physiological subtypes (post-load indices) give conflicting prognostic results** (POSTLOAD). Powe 2016 (PMID 27208340, abstract; n = 809, OGTT indices at 24-30 weeks): GDM with a predominant insulin-sensitivity defect (51% of GDM) had larger infants (birthweight z 0.57 vs 0.03) and more adverse outcomes (57.6% vs 28.2%) than normal glucose tolerance, independent of BMI; the secretion-defect subtype did not. Wang 2020 (PMID 32935481, abstract; 299 GDM, Chinese women): the secretion-defect and mixed subtypes, not the resistance subtype, were associated with LGA after BMI adjustment. Neither study allocates treatment by subtype; I found no trial that does (search 5, top 15 screened; not exhaustive). This bears on L110 ("IR phenotypes carry decision-relevant prognostic information" not supported) and on the steelman's pregnancy item; it is outside the bottom-line scope because the indices are post-load.

6. **Fasting HOMA-IR predicts insulin need in GDM poorly** (PMID 41018129, abstract; retrospective, single centre, n = 383, Japan). HOMA-IR AUC 0.618 for GDM discrimination with poor model fit; a composite propensity-adjusted Matsuda index reached AUC 0.785 for predicting insulin therapy. Single-source, in-sample, and the composite includes glucose, so it does not show an insulin-specific gain. It is evidence against a FASTING use for treatment selection in GDM, and at most a research lead for POSTLOAD.

7. **Pregnancy changes the meaning of any IR cutoff.** ADA 2026 s15 (excerpts): insulin sensitivity may be enhanced in early pregnancy, insulin resistance rises from about 16 weeks, and resistance falls rapidly after delivery. The review PMID 38185282 (abstract) states that pregnancy-specific reference ranges are needed and that results depend on gestational age, ethnicity and assay. So non-pregnant HOMA-IR thresholds (IRIS 3.0, MAFLD 2.5) should not be applied in pregnancy or the early postpartum period. This adds a pregnancy-specific reason to the project's assay and within-person variability argument (L39-57).

8. **Registered IR-enriched reproductive trials exist; none found where an insulin value allocates treatment** (ClinicalTrials.gov, search 19: 144 interventional records, top 20 titles screened). Examples: NCT03898037 (lifestyle and/or metformin in overweight or obese non-PCOS infertile women "with insulin resistance", completed, n = 80, pilot); NCT01915550 (adding metformin in "insulin-resistant diabetic pregnant women", completed, n = 90); NCT02647827 (acupuncture or metformin for IR in PCOS, recruiting); NCT05524259 (MYPP, myo-inositol in PCOS pregnancy, status unknown). How IR was defined was not checked (titles only). These are IR-enriched or IR-as-outcome designs, like PRISM-I, not tests of measurement-guided care.

9. **PPCOS I uses HOMA-IR only as a covariate** (PMID 38154770, abstract): in the main US trial of clomiphene, metformin and both, AMH, not HOMA-IR, was the reported predictor of ovulation; HOMA-IR was a confounder in the model. Single item, not a systematic look at PPCOS predictors.

Searched and found nothing usable: postpartum HOMA-IR or fasting insulin added to OGTT-based prediction of type 2 diabetes after GDM (search 20, zero hits from an over-specified string; this is not tested, not absent).

## 4. Overreach risks

- **Do not cite the PCOS practice points as evidence that testing was evaluated and rejected.** They are "evidence not sought" items (C2). The correct reading is "the guideline group judged current insulin assays unsuitable for routine care".
- **Do not cite the IPD interaction (RR 1.03) as support for insulin-guided metformin in PCOS infertility.** It is a post hoc interaction from an abstract with no unit or sampling time stated, in a comparison (clomiphene plus metformin vs clomiphene) that the 2023 guideline ranks below letrozole, and the guideline did not adopt it.
- **Do not let early-pregnancy HOMA-IR or TyG associations read as a screening use.** Association with GDM and a moderate in-sample AUC do not show added value over fasting glucose and BMI, and there is no agreed intervention to trigger (missed evidence 3 and 4).
- **Do not transfer non-pregnant HOMA-IR thresholds into pregnancy or the first postpartum weeks** (missed evidence 7).
- **Do not merge the reproductive post-load findings (GDM subtypes, Matsuda) into the fasting-based bottom lines.** They are POSTLOAD, observational and conflicting.
- **The ADA s15 "metformin does not reduce GDM in pre-existing insulin resistance" sentence is summariser-level** and conflicts with Yu 2023; cite it only with that caveat until the text and reference 143 are read.
- **ACOG statements are unverified.** Nothing in this review rests on ACOG wording.
