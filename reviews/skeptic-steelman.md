## Ranked problems

### 1. HIGH. PERSON is mislabelled in both files: the "matched" arm is the opposite of the pre-stated match
- **Claim.** Steelman §1 item 7 and §2 row 13, and merged table row: "phenotype-matched vs mismatched diet … insulin sensitivity, glucose homeostasis, triglycerides and CRP improved more in the matched arm".
- **What the paper says (FT).** The authors hypothesised that muscle-IR (MIR) people would do best on the high-MUFA diet and liver-IR (LIR) people on the low-fat/high-protein/high-fibre (LFHP) diet; that pairing was PhenoDiet group A. The arm that improved more was group B (MIR on LFHP, LIR on HMUFA): "Although not in concert with the initial hypothesis" and "conflicting findings" relative to CORDIOPREV-DIAB. So the arm that did better was the **opposite** of the hypothesised match. "Matched" is wrong unless one redefines the match after seeing the result.
- **Other facts the team omitted.**
  - Primary outcome (disposition index): group x time P = 0.109; no change over time in either group (P = 0.640). The abstract and both files do say the primary was null, so that part is carried through.
  - By the authors' own limitations paragraph, group A was "by chance somewhat more insulin sensitive at baseline" (Matsuda 4.8 vs 4.2). Baseline imbalance in the very outcomes that "improved more" is a plausible alternative explanation, adjusted for statistically only.
  - No multiplicity control is described (Methods: two-tailed P < 0.05); the secondary improvements are many outcomes at P < 0.05.
  - Phenotype assignment used a 7-point OGTT with insulin and tertile cut-offs of MISI/HIRI (the median HIRI of the study population was used for one cut-off); 76% of participants had normal glucose tolerance. This is not a routine single-sample measurement.
  - Both arms were phenotype-informed. There is no arm that ignores IR phenotype, so the trial cannot say that measuring IR beats not measuring it. It tests a diet x phenotype interaction, and the observed one is opposite to the hypothesis.
  - 199 of 242 completed the primary outcome measures; 22 had a reduced COVID-adjusted protocol; ITT mixed model assumes missing at random.
- **Fix.** Describe as: "Group B (MIR on LFHP, LIR on HMUFA) vs group A (the hypothesised pairing). Primary outcome null (P = 0.109); several secondary outcomes better in B, unadjusted for multiplicity, with a chance baseline imbalance favouring A on insulin sensitivity; opposite to the hypothesis; no phenotype-blind arm." Remove "matched/mismatched". The headline value "direct randomized evidence that an IR-defined phenotype can be matched to treatment" (steelman §1 item 7) should be dropped or rewritten as "randomized, hypothesis-contradicting".

### 2. HIGH. Presurgical metformin row: the second P value is misdescribed, and the subgroup finding is weaker and less IR-specific than stated
- **Claim.** Steelman §2 row 1 and merged table: "Ki-67 fell only in women with HOMA above 2.8; P(interaction) 0.045, 0.07 at follow-up; overall null."
- **Check.** 0.045 is right (HOMA > 2.8; abstract of 22564993; the HOMA cutoff and ER status were protocol-predefined per the follow-up methods). **The 0.07 is not an interaction replicating 0.045.** In 25253174 it is the interaction between HOMA and the *association of serum metformin level with Ki-67 change*, in a 41-woman subset of the metformin arm with a blood draw within 24 h of dosing. It does not test the treatment x HOMA effect again. The merged table should not list it as a "follow-up" P(interaction) for the same question.
- **Additional facts (FT/ABS).**
  - In the primary report the HOMA > 2.8 subgroup decrease was "nonsignificant" (-10.5%, 95% CI -26.1% to 8.4%) and the HOMA ≤ 2.8 increase was also nonsignificant (+11.1%, -0.6% to 24.2%). "Fell only in women with HOMA above 2.8" overstates the within-subgroup result; the follow-up abstract does say "significantly decreased" vs placebo in that subgroup (a different analysis; I did not reconcile the two).
  - In the follow-up, fasting insulin, glucose, BMI, weight, adiponectin and total IGF-I did **not** modify the effect, and the C-peptide interaction was P = 0.3. The signal attaches to HOMA (insulin x glucose), IGFBP-1/IGFBP-3, free IGF-I and hs-CRP, not to insulin. This is an unflagged point against "insulin measurement" specifically.
  - The follow-up applied no multiplicity adjustment ("exploratory … hypothesis generating"); thresholds other than HOMA and ER were set empirically from the STEPP plots; approx. 25% of women were "insulin resistant"; 4-week surrogate endpoint with a Ki-67 rise in the placebo arm that the authors attribute to biopsy/surgery wound effects.
- **Contradiction the files do not draw.** The MA.32 authors state their findings "contrast with" this trial: DeCensi saw benefit at high HOMA; MA.32 saw no benefit at high HOMA and possible harm at low (see 3). The two oncology items are discordant, not convergent. Presenting them together as "opens oncology as a setting" overstates.
- **Fix.** State "P(interaction) 0.045 for HOMA > 2.8; separate 0.07 for a drug-level x HOMA association in 41 women; insulin itself and C-peptide not effect modifiers; direction opposite to MA.32".

### 3. HIGH. MA.32 interaction analysis: steelman overstates, merged under-reports multiplicity
- **Steelman wording** (§1 item 2): "a baseline insulin assay changed the sign of a hard-outcome effect … still falsifies 'no baseline-IR interaction on hard outcomes anywhere'"; §2 row 2: "multiplicity unclear".
- **What the paper says (FT).**
  - Multiplicity is not unclear: "We examined **72 interactions** … up to **4** statistically significant interactions could have occurred because of the play of chance"; no adjustment was made. By my count of Table 3, six interaction P values are ≤ .05 (leptin DRFS .02 and OS .05; insulin DRFS .04; HOMA DRFS .04 and OS .05; triple-negative glucose DRFS .02), i.e. about 8% of 72 where 5% is expected by chance. The overlap between outcomes and between correlated biomarkers makes these non-independent.
  - Invasive disease-free survival (the trial's primary outcome) interactions were all non-significant (luminal insulin P = .20, HOMA .14). Overall survival with insulin P = .09. HOMA was available for 1,486 of 2,104 luminal patients (same-day glucose and insulin).
  - "Sign change" is a point estimate crossing 1.0 within overlapping confidence intervals. Luminal DRFS HR for metformin vs placebo: insulin 40 pmol/L 1.23 (0.93-1.64), 60 pmol/L 1.07 (0.85-1.35), 94 pmol/L 0.91 (0.71-1.18); only the HOMA 1.3 point has a CI excluding 1 (1.41, 1.02-1.95). There is **no benefit at high insulin/HOMA** at any point; the pattern is a possible harm at low values.
  - Authors' conclusion: "failed to predict metformin effect", "may reflect the play of chance", "do not support an important indirect effect of metformin", and "little evidence that BMI or obesity-associated variables identified subgroups that might benefit". The abstract line quoted by the steelman ("did not identify subgroups…") is accurate but the steelman then uses the paper as positive support.
  - It is protocol-specified, with DRFS as the prespecified primary outcome of these analyses (this is in the paper's favour and neither file says it).
- **Net.** The paper shows that a baseline-IR interaction analysis in a large RCT exists and that it is compatible with chance. It does not show that baseline insulin changes the effect of metformin. The steelman's own §3 claim 4 ("Overturned for MA.32") is true only for the narrow gap statement "no analysis exists"; it overturns nothing about utility. Merged table row wording ("exploratory interactions … authors say they did not identify subgroups") is acceptable but should add "72 unadjusted tests, ≤ 4 expected by chance; IDFS interactions null; discordant with the presurgical trial".
- **MA.32 main trial (ABS).** Primary analysis in 2,533 ER/PgR+ patients: HR 1.01 (0.84-1.21), P = .93; "null" is correct.

### 4. MEDIUM. ATTICA: the numbers are exact and the correction stands, but "external validation" and "optimism" need qualifying, and the base score is applied outside its validated range
**Verified exactly (FT).** Derivation (ATTICA, n = 1,677, 269 events, median follow-up 10 y): TyG ≥ 8.46 adds NRI 0.668 (events 33.1%, non-events 33.7%) and delta C 0.054 (0.020-0.088), P = 0.002. "Validation" (pooled Athens Cardiometabolic + Hippokration Hypertension cohorts, n = 1,237, 63 events, median follow-up 5 y): NRI 0.469 (0.215-0.722), delta C 0.044 (0.002-0.085), P = 0.038. Abstract and merged file figures match Table 2. **The cutoff 8.46 was derived in ATTICA** (inflection point of the spline, then time-dependent ROC), which is the same sample in which the 0.054 and NRI 0.668 are computed, so these are in-sample as the merged file says. The merged file's "I have not seen the full text" is now out of date.
**Points the merged bullet and steelman miss.**
- *What "external" is.* The validation set is two hospital clinic populations (a dyslipidaemia and atherosclerosis unit, and a hypertension clinic) from the same city and the same research network, pooled, with different follow-up (median 4 y and 12.5 y), not a general-population or geographically separate cohort. Only 63 events (31 CV deaths, 22 MIs, 10 revascularisations; ATTICA did not break out component events). The authors themselves call these "primary prevention patients … at least one cardiometabolic risk factor". "Externally validated" is the authors' label and is defensible in the narrow sense of an independent cohort; "the largest reported gain over a current European score" (steelman §2 row 3) is not a robust statement. The validation delta C has a lower bound of 0.002.
- *Singular "an external validation cohort"* in the merged bullet should read "a pooled set of two Athens clinic cohorts".
- *Base score.* SCORE2 is applied in ATTICA (mean age 43.9, SD 13.6; first tertile mean 37.2; range extends below 40). SCORE2 is validated for ages 40-69 (my recall of the ESC 2021 scope; the paper applies SCORE2 for < 70 and SCORE2-OP for ≥ 70 without discussing under-40s). A weak or mis-calibrated base score inflates delta C. This is inference from the baseline table, not something the paper says.
- *Binary marker at roughly the median.* 8.46 sits near the cohort medians (8.39 derivation, 8.45 validation) and flags 44% and 49% of people. Event rates in the validation set were 7.4% vs 2.9% above/below. No calibration or decision-curve analysis is reported.
- *NRI type.* The main text does not say whether categories or category-free; NRI values of 0.67 and 0.47 with ~33% of events moved up are typical of category-free NRI, which is known to be inflated. The text also describes 563 "more appropriately reclassified" people by summing the two net components, which is not what NRI means. I did not read the supporting information.
- *Construct.* TyG uses triglycerides and glucose, not insulin. SCORE2 uses non-HDL cholesterol and HDL, not triglycerides or glucose, so the gain may be triglyceride/glucose information, not "IR". No analysis separates these. The authors' own cutoff (8.46) is not an IR threshold (their cited clamp-based threshold is 4.68).
- *Comparison with PMID 39117485 (ABS).* delta C 0.005 (P = 0.042) with a *continuous* TyG over SCORE risk factors (components, not the SCORE2 score), 1,970 healthy adults, 15.4 y. It also reports association "evident only in subjects aged 41 or 51 years" subgroup. Pooled Liu 2026 is against Framingham. The merged line "results over a current score range from 0.0004 to 0.054 and conflict" compares different designs, scores and codings (continuous vs dichotomised at a derived cutoff); it is fair as a statement that results disagree, not as a spread of one quantity. In bottom line 2, "gains over a current score" fits ATTICA (SCORE2) but 39117485 used SCORE risk factors, not the current score.
**Verdict on the bullet.** The correction (ATTICA does not fit "small or no gain") is justified. The surrounding text should say the validation is a small, hospital-based, pooled set and that the delta C interval nearly touches zero.

### 5. MEDIUM. "Far from the cutoff" is wrongly applied to IRIS-type entry (steelman §1 item 3; merged "Added by the steelman run")
The merged file says severe/syndromic IR, lipodystrophy work-up and "IRIS-type entry sit in the range where misclassification is rare". IRIS entry was defined by HOMA-IR > 3.0, i.e. the decision *is* a threshold decision; people with true HOMA between 3 and about 4.5 are close to it, and by the merged file's own arithmetic (20% above reads below ≈ 24%; 1.5x ≈ 6%) a meaningful share would be misclassified at that threshold. Only values well above the cutoff are insulated, and the steelman's "top quartile" statement is unsourced (I could not find it in the steelman's cited material). Also the merged file already notes the lipodystrophy insulin claim is unsourced. Fix: restrict the "far from cutoff" argument to severe/syndromic IR, and remove IRIS-type entry.

### 6. MEDIUM. TriMaster is used on both sides and neither use is clean
- ABS facts match: 309 adults, three-way crossover; cluster approach (routine features + HOMA) and routine-features model both associated with differential response (P < 0.0001); clusters showed benefit for only one cluster-drug pair (severe insulin-resistant diabetes, SGLT2i vs TZD, P = 0.005); routine-features allocation gave 0.27% (0.18-0.35) vs 0.16% (0.07-0.25) HbA1c reduction. The abstract does not test the difference between those two intervals; the overlap should be noted before "beat".
- *Steelman on utility side* (§1 item 8): a single cluster-drug pair at P = 0.005 within a family of cluster x drug comparisons (the number of comparisons is not in the abstract) is not strong evidence that HOMA-based clustering identifies a responder group.
- *Steelman and merged on the no-utility side* ("supports IR is not needed for glycaemic choice"): the contrast is cluster method vs routine-features model, and the clusters themselves were built from routine features *plus* HOMA. It does not test HOMA's increment over routine features. The conclusion that routine features suffice is what the paper says; the team's gloss ("IR measurement does not beat clinical features") is a stronger statement than the abstract supports. HOMA in the cluster scheme: the original clusters used HOMA2 from C-peptide, which I recall but did not verify.

### 7. MEDIUM. Search log: strings that cannot support a "survived" claim
- **P1 / "no RCT of IR-guided care vs usual care".** The log string uses `"insulin resistance"[majr]` while §4 prints `"Insulin Resistance"[Mesh]`; the two are different restrictions, so the table does not describe what the log ran. Words after AND (`stratified OR selected OR guided`) are unfielded and expanded by PubMed's automatic term mapping. Only the top 5 of 305 (P1) and the top 8 of 286 (P25) were screened, sorted by relevance. "Not in the top 5/8" cannot be read as "no such trial". The convergent support for this negative is the original searcher's work (merged line 47, "many convergent searches"), not these two strings.
- **Merged open gap 1** still says the MeSH-based search "nobody ran", while the steelman says it ran one. Reconcile: "run once by the steelman, top 5-8 of 286-305 screened".
- **Zero-hit strings** (P5, P8, P12, P19, P20, P28): zero hits for a loose topic means a broken query, not an absence. The steelman admits "my STAR strings were poor" but still lists the STAR absence under "Claims of absence that survived". It should be under "not tested". P28 (pregnancy) and P45/P44 (dementia/HFpEF, 2 hits each) are correctly marked not tested in the table, but the HFpEF and dementia rows' "Not found" wording is a six-term conjunction (the same flaw the merged file attributes to the searcher).
- **P27** (PCOS, 12 records): "Survives" rests on the top hits being about hyperandrogenism and LH/HDL; no record is cited as comparing IR with clinical features. A 12-record string could be fully screened; it was not described as such.
- **P11** (TriMaster, 3 hits) is a lookup, not a search for a negative; see 6.
- **P2** (`"insulin resistance" AND "decision curve"`, 145) counts records containing both phrases, including DCAs of other things where IR is a covariate. Existence of DCAs for an IR index is supported at title level for 36093151 (TyG added to GRACE in NSTE-ACS PCI patients, a secondary-prevention population) and for eGDR papers 40241176 and 40223076 (eGDR is not an insulin assay). "About 145 PubMed records on IR and decision curves" in the merged file is accurate as a record count, but "decision-curve papers on IR indices exist" is supported by about three titles read, not 145.

### 8. LOW-MEDIUM. The "single study that would most change the conclusion" (steelman §5)
- **Sound as a design for one niche question**: a post-stroke/TIA pioglitazone trial across all HOMA strata with a second randomisation of "measure HOMA, treat if > 3.0" against "treat by glycaemic category" does separate the IR value from the glycaemic category, which Spence 2019 suggests may be the true selector.
- **Overstated as "most change the conclusion".** Bottom line 1 is about routine measurement in primary care, prediabetes, T2D, PCOS, MASLD and obesity. A positive result in post-stroke patients would establish one niche, which the merged file already carries as the single IR-threshold treatment-selection niche (bottom line 3). It would change "shown not to add value" for that niche and leave the routine verdict unchanged.
- **Feasibility.** An interaction or two-strategy comparison needs several times IRIS's size (my rough arithmetic; IRIS enrolled 3,876); pioglitazone is generic with no obvious sponsor; the proposed "assay traceable to an isotope-dilution reference" cannot currently be delivered because the merged file itself records that no certified reference system with a deployed traceability chain exists. The steelman does not discuss any of this.
- **Alternative that would change the routine verdict more cheaply**: the prespecified incremental-value study already listed as merged open gap 2 (delta C, category-based and category-free NRI, calibration and decision curve, HOMA-IR/TyG over PCE/SCORE2, external diverse cohort), plus any trial of measurement-guided care. I would present that and a niche trial as two different studies answering two different questions.

### 9. LOW. Smaller items
- **MAFLD (FT table).** HOMA-IR ≥ 2.5 is one of seven optional metabolic risk criteria (waist, BP, triglycerides, HDL, prediabetes, HOMA-IR, hs-CRP) and any two suffice; a lean person can meet MAFLD without an insulin assay. Steelman and merged ("definitional, not a management use") are correct, and the "second-hand" qualifier can be dropped now (primary criterion confirmed). Add "one of seven; not required".
- **MR 32398352 (ABS).** Numbers correct (CAD OR 1.79, 1.57-2.04; small-artery stroke 1.80, 1.30-2.49; overall ischaemic stroke 1.21, 1.05-1.40). The instruments are SNPs for fasting insulin, HDL-C and triglycerides; HDL-C and triglycerides are themselves lipid risk factors with their own pathways, so "no evidence of pleiotropy" (abstract) does not separate IR from lipid biology. Steelman §2 row 15 ("Strengthens plausibility for treating IR") is stronger than the merged file's careful wording. Keep the merged wording.
- **TRIO-BD (ABS + REG).** Steelman and merged are accurate on the converter analysis: the published primary is converters vs non-converters (a post-randomisation split: 10 of 11 converters were on metformin), n = 45 randomised. The registry description frames the primary as "effect of metformin on MADRS … mediated by conversion of IR to insulin sensitivity", target 110, actual enrolment listed as 50 (the paper says 45 randomised). The randomised metformin vs placebo contrast on MADRS is not reported in the abstract. I could not retrieve the full text, so I cannot say whether it was reported; this is a point to check, not a finding.
- **Pioglitazone in depression, 26602230 (ABS).** The abstract reports that improvement in depression was associated with improvement in glucose metabolism "only in patients with insulin resistance", n = 37, 12 weeks. That is a within-treatment association with a post-randomisation change, not a baseline-IR x treatment interaction. The steelman table ("Same" as an IR-defined entry) does not apply: entry was across the insulin-sensitivity spectrum.
- **NAFLD cohort 34674510 (ABS).** Numbers match (n = 15,728; 86% men; OR 2.25 NFS and 2.55 APRI for HOMA-IR rise > 1; no effect in women and BMI < 25). The exposure (change in HOMA-IR, which contains glucose) and the outcome (NAFLD fibrosis score, which contains BMI, impaired fasting glucose/diabetes, AST/ALT, platelets and albumin) share components, so some association is mechanical (my inference from the standard definition of NFS). The steelman says "supports monitoring value, not tested". The data support an association between two changes only; "supports monitoring value" should be dropped.
- **C-peptide scores 22963817 / 32873586 (ABS).** The Diabetes Surgery Score was derived in 63 and validated in 176 patients (1-year remission) and includes C-peptide with BMI, duration and age; no incremental C-peptide analysis is reported. The SOS paper shows 2-year AUROCs of 0.79-0.88 for five remission scores and 0.84 for **diabetes duration alone**, 0.70-0.76 vs 0.73 at 10 years. So at 2 years the duration-only AUC sits inside the score range, not only at 10 years; "no better than duration alone" is a fair reading at both horizons. Steelman §1 item 5 lists the 0.79-0.88 as support for utility; it should not be, since not all of the five scores use C-peptide (my recall of the score definitions: the DiaRem-type scores use age, HbA1c, insulin use and number of drugs; not verified here) and the stronger comparator is a variable that costs nothing.
- **Stale/internal wording in merged.** Line 3 says the steelman run is "not yet reviewed by a skeptic" (now reviewed here). ATTICA "I have not seen the full text" (now read). Open gap 6 ("have a skeptic review ir-steelman.md and read ATTICA, PERSON and MA.32 in full") is now done for those three (and for the follow-up metformin paper), not for TriMaster, MR, TRIO-BD, the NAFLD cohort or the C-peptide papers.

## Is the lowering from 50-55% to 45-50% justified?

- **Direction: defensible.** One real error of fact in the old text (ATTICA filed as "small or no gain") is corrected and it does show that results over a current score disagree; the decision-curve negative was properly softened (carried through correctly: "titles screened only", "none was shown to compare against a standard risk score or to test the decision to measure"); and there is still no adequately powered outcome test of measuring IR.
- **Reasons given in bottom line 2: partly not valid.** It lists "exploratory RCT interactions with baseline insulin/HOMA exist in oncology" as evidence against "shown not to add value". After reading the papers, those two trials point in opposite directions, MA.32 was chance-compatible by its authors' own multiplicity statement, and the presurgical trial used a Ki-67 surrogate with insulin and C-peptide not modifying the effect. They do not move the probability that incremental value has been shown absent. PERSON, as shown in problem 1, cannot count. TriMaster, if anything, leans toward routine features. What does move the number is ATTICA (with the qualifiers above) and the unscreened decision-curve literature.
- **Magnitude.** A 5-point change in a judgement figure whose own wording says "a judgement, not a computation" is within noise. I would carry the existing 45-50% only if the reasons are rewritten as above; otherwise the range is unsupported by the stated evidence. The number is also ambiguous about what it is a probability of (that the claim "shown not to add value" is true, or that it would survive further checking?); the file should say which.
- My own figure for the claim "shown not to add value" is about 45-55%, so I do not object to 45-50%, but I would not call it "fell after the steelman run" without the caveats.

## Are abstract-level caveats carried through?

Mostly yes. Merged consistently tags rows as abstract-level and surrogate (Ki-67), and the "Steelman limits" bullet is honest. Gaps: (a) the multiplicity statement for MA.32 is absent (72 unadjusted tests); (b) PERSON's baseline imbalance, absent multiplicity control, and reversed hypothesis are absent; (c) ATTICA's validation set composition and event count are absent; (d) several items now upgraded to full-text (ATTICA, PERSON, MA.32 exploratory, follow-up metformin, MAFLD table) are still labelled abstract-level or "second-hand" in the merged file.

## What survived scrutiny

- All identifiers resolve; no title/PMID mismatches in the 15 targets or the 18 others checked; no retractions or corrections (Crossref).
- ATTICA figures (NRI 0.668/0.469, delta C 0.054/0.044, cutoff 8.46, n = 1,677/1,237) exact; cutoff derived in ATTICA; the correction of the "small or no gain" filing is right.
- Blicher 2024: delta C 0.005, P = 0.042, n = 1,970, 15.4 y.
- PERSON: n = 242, primary outcome null (P = 0.109), secondary improvements exist in group B.
- MA.32 main trial null; exploratory interaction results exist with the stated direction; authors' own conclusion is non-supportive.
- Presurgical metformin: n = 200, overall null, HOMA > 2.8 interaction P = 0.045.
- MAFLD: HOMA-IR ≥ 2.5 is a listed metabolic criterion; definitional only.
- MR numbers; TRIO-BD converter analysis; NAFLD cohort numbers; SOS and Diabetes Surgery Score numbers.
- The steelman's own honest bottom line (routine measurement has no established utility; items 1, 2 and 7 weakest despite being called best) and its caveat that none shows better outcomes from measuring.

## Verdict

**Are the changes to merged-conclusion.md justified?** Partly.
- *Justified:* the ATTICA correction (with qualifiers); softened decision-curve wording; the "not tested" list (pregnancy, dementia, HFpEF, MA.32 low-insulin sign) as long as the search strings are described as weak; the 45-50% direction.
- *Needs correction before they stand:* PERSON row (inverted "matched"); presurgical row (0.07 is not an interaction replication; insulin and C-peptide not modifiers; discordant with MA.32); MA.32 row (add 72 unadjusted tests, IDFS null, chance statement); "IRIS-type entry sits far from the cutoff"; bottom-line-2 reasoning; stale "not yet reviewed" and "full text not seen" notes; open gap 1 vs steelman's MeSH run.
- *Overall strength of the case for clinical utility after this review:* unchanged at the level the merged file states for routine measurement: no established utility. Confidence in that bottom line 1 verdict about 85%. Confidence that the steelman-derived edits are right in direction but need the corrections above about 80%.
