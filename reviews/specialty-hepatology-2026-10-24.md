## 3. Missed evidence (hepatology)

1. **EASL "possible MASLD" sentence (FASTING + POSTLOAD; full text).** EASL 2024 says people with steatosis, no cardiometabolic risk factor and no other cause "may have 'possible MASLD' – requiring further tests of insulin-resistance – or cryptogenic SLD". This is the one place where a hepatology guideline attaches an IR test to a classification decision (MASLD versus cryptogenic SLD). It changes a label, and through the label the work-up for other causes; EASL does not tie it to a treatment change. The project lists the "may be considered" item but not this one.
2. **EASL Table 7 menu at diagnosis and follow-up (FASTING, CPEPTIDE, POSTLOAD; full text).** It is attached to a 100%-agreement recommendation that comorbidity tests "are recommended" at diagnosis and at follow-up. Table 7 includes fasting insulin and/or C-peptide, HOMA-IR, and OGTT or mixed-meal IR indices. Read literally, this could be cited as support for measuring IR repeatedly in MASLD. It is the strongest risk of misreading in hepatology (see section 4).
3. **C-peptide-based HOMA and Adipo-IR (FASTING; full text).** EASL names HOMA-IR "from fasting insulin or C-peptide" and Adipo-IR (fasting insulin with non-esterified fatty acids), which it says "even predicts the severity of hepatic fibrosis". The citation behind that claim was not traced. Adipo-IR is a common research end point in MASH trials: in the TOSCA.IT NAFLD subgroup (PMID 34311022, abstract), pioglitazone lowered HOMA-IR and Adipo-IR versus sulphonylurea, as an outcome, not a selector. In Cusi 2016 (27322798, abstract), adipose, hepatic and muscle insulin sensitivity were secondary outcomes. These are research uses, and the project's measure taxonomy does not cover Adipo-IR.
4. **Hepatic insulin clearance distorts insulin-based IR indices in liver disease (FASTING; abstracts).** This is a liver-specific analytical-validity caveat that the clinical-chemistry section lacks:
   - Letiexhe 1993 (8077319): compensated cirrhosis, n=9 versus 9 controls; insulin clearance 40% lower, correlated with portosystemic shunt size (r=0.75), together with 60% higher secretion.
   - Holland-Fischer 2010 (20489042): TIPS raised fasting insulin (123 to 193 pmol/L) and lowered the C-peptide-to-insulin ratio by 21%. Clamp-measured insulin sensitivity was unchanged.
   - Miyaaki 2016 (27150853): NAFLD with severe fibrosis had lower OGTT-derived hepatic insulin clearance and higher HOMA-β.
   - Lamprinou 2021 (33862045, MR): did not support a causal link from liver fat to hepatic insulin clearance. Steatosis alone may therefore matter less than fibrosis or shunting (my inference from the two abstracts).
   - Implication (inference): fasting insulin and HOMA-IR overstate IR as fibrosis advances and in cirrhosis. HOMA-IR-fibrosis associations are partly a clearance effect. C-peptide-based HOMA, which EASL mentions, should be less affected by clearance; this is untested here.
5. **HOMA-IR associations with fibrosis, without incremental testing (FASTING; abstracts).**
   - Fujii 2019 (30600551): retrospective, cross-sectional, multicentre Japanese study; n=361 biopsy-proven non-diabetic NAFLD, 48 with advanced fibrosis. HOMA-IR of 2.90 or more was an independent predictor in both the estimation and the validation split. There was no comparison with FIB-4 or elastography, and the authors' conclusion is about treatment goals, not testing.
   - Smaller single-centre studies: Ballestri 2016 (26785389, n=118) and Cetin 2020 (33364879, n=85).
   - The CIR score (36536019; CAP plus HOMA, n=155, AUROC 0.93 derivation) targets NASH, not fibrosis, and is single-centre.
   - A search for HOMA-IR increment over FIB-4 returned 0 records (log item 9).
   - These support "association real, incremental value untested" for liver outcomes, and item 4 weakens even the association.
6. **TyG for steatosis (SURROGATE; abstracts).** Wang 2022 meta-analysis (36500999): 20 studies; TyG diagnosed NAFLD/MAFLD with pooled sensitivity 0.73, specificity 0.67 and AUC 0.75, with threshold effects and heterogeneity. The umbrella review (39180024) reports an association (OR 2.36), and the TyG-WC meta-analysis (40312323) reports a mean difference only. These figures are for detecting steatosis, which ultrasound, CAP or MRI-PDFF already do. None is about fibrosis, and no hepatology guideline read uses TyG.
7. **No IR-selected or IR-stratified MASH trial (all; abstracts and PIVENS full text).** MAESTRO-NASH and ESSENCE selected by histology. PIVENS excluded diabetes and found no baseline predictor of response. Cusi 2016 selected by glucose category (prediabetes or T2D). The AGA lean update allows pioglitazone without an IR test. Hepatology therefore has no counterpart to IRIS (bottom line 3), and the "IR-threshold treatment selection" niche stays cardiology/stroke-only.

## 4. Overreach risks

- **Risk of overstating utility:** citing EASL Table 7 or the "requiring further tests of insulin-resistance" sentence as "EASL recommends HOMA-IR in MASLD". The graded item is weak ("may be considered", 92%). Table 7 is a menu "according to clinical evaluation and a priori probability", and neither passage changes fibrosis staging or therapy.
- **Risk of overstating futility:** "no guideline requires an IR measurement" read as "IR measurement has no role in hepatology". EASL does attach IR testing to the "possible MASLD" versus cryptogenic SLD distinction in people without cardiometabolic risk factors (a classification niche, specialist, not routine).
- **Measure-type blur:** EASL bundles fasting (HOMA-IR, fasting insulin or C-peptide, Adipo-IR) with post-load (OGTT and mixed-meal indices). Project text should not reduce it to "HOMA-IR".
- **Population blur:** none of the insulin-based measures is interpretable in people on insulin (EASL says so for HOMA-IR). In cirrhosis or after TIPS, reduced hepatic clearance raises fasting insulin independent of sensitivity. Claims about HOMA-IR in "MASLD" should exclude cirrhosis or state the caveat.
- **TyG "diagnostic accuracy" for MASLD:** an AUC of 0.75 for steatosis is not an argument for routine use. Steatosis is detected by imaging, and fibrosis is what drives management.
