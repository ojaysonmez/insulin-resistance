# Endocrinologist assessment: clinical utility of insulin resistance (IR) measurement

Scope: specialist-referred adults (T2D, prediabetes, PCOS, MASLD, obesity-related disease, lipodystrophy and hyperinsulinemic states). Coverage and reimbursement are out of scope.
Date: 2026-10-18. Author: ir-endocrinologist session.

## Citation and verification policy (read first)
- Every PMID below was checked against PubMed this session: I fetched the record and read the title (marked **[V]**). Two PMIDs I first recalled from memory were wrong (the record was an unrelated article), so I discarded them and re-resolved them by journal/volume/page.
- I verified bibliographic identity (title, journal), not every numeric result. Where I state a quantitative or content result from a paper, it comes from my own knowledge and is marked **[recall]**; treat it as lower-confidence until checked against the abstract.
- Statements with no citation are marked **[opinion]** (my clinical reasoning) and are not evidence.
- Evidence grades (high/moderate/low) refer to the claim "measuring IR changes a clinical decision or outcome", not to the physiology.

## 1. Cross-cutting: what the tests are and where they fail
**Reference standard.** The hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp is the gold standard for whole-body insulin sensitivity (PMID 382871) but is a research tool: long, invasive, not used in routine specialist care **[opinion]**.
**Practical surrogates.** HOMA-IR (PMID 3899825), QUICKI (10902785), OGTT-derived Matsuda index (10480510), and lipid-based indices such as TyG (19067533). A 2022 review describes diagnosing IR as challenging, with no universally accepted threshold for HOMA-IR or TyG (PMID 35939943).
**Limits in my patients**
1. *Assay variability.* Eleven commercial insulin assays gave materially different results on the same samples (Manley, PMID 17363420), and cross-reactivity with proinsulin and analogues differs by assay **[recall]**. Consequence: a cutoff from one assay or cohort is not transferable **[inference]**.
2. *No agreed cutoffs.* No guideline-endorsed HOMA-IR threshold (PMID 35939943). ADA Standards 2025 diagnose diabetes/prediabetes by glucose and HbA1c criteria, not IR (PMID 39651986). Cutoffs depend on population and ethnicity **[opinion; biostatistician to quantify]**.
3. *Beta-cell failure.* HOMA assumes intact insulin secretion. In longer-standing T2D, falling insulin output lowers fasting insulin and makes HOMA-IR understate true insulin resistance **[opinion based on HOMA model, PMID 3899825]**.
4. *Exogenous insulin.* In any insulin-treated patient, fasting insulin and HOMA-IR are uninterpretable; assay cross-reactivity with analogues adds to this (PMID 17363420). C-peptide is the usable endogenous marker (PMID 23413806).
5. *Fasting and acute illness.* Needs a proper overnight fast and metabolic stability; glucotoxicity and acute illness distort results **[opinion]**.
6. *Biology.* Whole-body IR is a composite of muscle, liver and adipose IR with different clinical meanings **[opinion; ir-scientist's domain]**.

**Bottom line:** IR measurement is not part of any guideline diagnostic algorithm cited here for T2D, prediabetes, PCOS or MASLD. Its use is research, risk phenotyping and a few niche diagnostic questions.

## 2. Prediabetes
- **Use case:** who progresses, which intervention.
- **What IR adds:** Pathophysiology-based subphenotyping of at-risk people (insulin resistance and secretion phenotypes) identified groups with differing risk of progression and complications (PMID 33398163), i.e. phenotypes carry prognostic information beyond glycaemia. Trial evidence of benefit, however, is for interventions applied by glycaemic category: lifestyle and metformin in DPP (PMID 11832527) and pioglitazone in IGT (ACT NOW, PMID 21428766; large reduction in conversion, HR about 0.28 **[recall]**). None used IR measurement to select treatment.
- **Decision it would change:** none established. I would intensify lifestyle/pharmacotherapy by glycaemia, age, BMI, gestational diabetes history and CV risk, not HOMA-IR **[opinion]**. Choosing a TZD in a high-IR phenotype is plausible but untested.
- **Evidence strength:** *low* for "IR measurement changes prediabetes management"; *moderate* for IR phenotype carrying prognostic information (one high-quality cohort analysis, not tested in treatment-by-phenotype RCTs).
- **Status:** research / expert opinion.

## 3. Type 2 diabetes
- **Use case:** drug selection, phenotyping, separating T1D/LADA/MODY/lipodystrophy from typical T2D.
- **What IR adds:** Data-driven subgroups, including a severe insulin-resistant cluster (SIRD) defined from HOMA2-IR with BMI, age, HbA1c, HOMA2-B and GAD antibodies, differ in complications, notably kidney disease **[recall]** (PMID 29503172). Follow-up work compared subgroup-based models with models using simple clinical features for progression and treatment response (PMID 31047901). My reading is that simple features (BMI, HbA1c, age, sex) did most of the work, so clusters add little for treatment choice **[recall of conclusion; verify]**.
- **Guidelines:** drug choice follows comorbidities (ASCVD, HF, CKD), weight, hypoglycaemia risk and cost, not an IR assay. The GRADE trial compared four add-ons to metformin on glycaemic outcomes without IR-based selection (PMID 36129996). I did not retrieve the ADA pharmacologic section, so "no guideline instructs IR-guided drug choice" is **[opinion]**.
- **Where a related measurement changes management:** C-peptide with glucose to judge endogenous insulin secretion when classifying diabetes and judging insulin need (PMID 23413806). This measures secretion, not IR, and is read with autoantibodies and clinical context.
- **Pioglitazone:** IRIS enrolled non-diabetic patients with recent stroke/TIA *and insulin resistance* (HOMA-IR above 3.0 **[recall]**) and reported fewer stroke/MI events with pioglitazone (PMID 26886418). It is the largest outcome trial I know in which IR defined enrolment, but it had no non-IR comparison group, so it shows benefit in an IR population, not that measuring IR is required to target the drug. Weight gain, oedema and fracture risk limit use **[recall]**.
- **Evidence strength:** *low* that routine IR testing changes T2D drug choice; *high* that C-peptide is useful for classification.
- **Status:** IR phenotyping is research; C-peptide is established practice for selected patients.

## 4. PCOS
- **Use case:** the 2023 international guideline bases diagnosis on Rotterdam-type criteria and positions metformin as an adjunct for metabolic features (PMID 37580314). I recall that it does not recommend routine insulin or IR measurement for diagnosis; verify the exact wording **[recall]**.
- **What IR adds:** IR is mechanistically central and common even in lean women (PMIDs 23065822, 27459230). IR indices diverge across PCOS phenotypes, so a single index may misclassify (PMID 23476073).
- **Decision it would change:** I would screen glycaemia (OGTT or HbA1c) and cardiometabolic risk in all PCOS patients regardless of fasting insulin; a high HOMA-IR would not change that **[opinion]**. Whether high HOMA-IR should prompt earlier metformin is plausible, but I found no verified RCT showing IR-stratified response; rated unproven.
- **Evidence strength:** *low* for IR measurement guiding management; *moderate* for IR being biologically relevant.
- **Status:** expert opinion / research. Gap: ethnicity-specific cutoffs.

## 5. MASLD (formerly NAFLD)
- **Nomenclature:** MASLD requires steatosis plus at least one cardiometabolic risk criterion (PMID 37363821); the criteria use BMI/waist, glycaemia, BP and lipids rather than an IR index **[recall]**.
- **Guidelines:** AASLD (PMID 36727674) and EASL-EASD-EASO (PMID 38851997) centre assessment on fibrosis risk (FIB-4, elastography) and cardiometabolic comorbidity; to my knowledge neither requires an IR measurement to stage or treat **[recall; verify]**.
- **Treatment trials:** pioglitazone improved NASH histology in non-diabetic adults (PIVENS, PMID 20427778) and in patients with prediabetes/T2D (PMID 27322798); resmetirom (PMID 38324483) and semaglutide (PMID 40305708) have phase 3 histology results. Eligibility rests on histology/fibrosis stage, not measured IR **[recall]**.
- **Decision it would change:** none established; fibrosis staging dominates. IR is mechanistic background (hepatic IR/lipolysis; ir-scientist) rather than a decision tool **[opinion]**.
- **Evidence strength:** *low* for IR testing.
- **Status:** research.

## 6. Obesity-related metabolic disease and cardiovascular risk
- HOMA-IR associated with incident CVD in the Insulin Resistance Atherosclerosis Study (PMID 12087016); I recall attenuation after adjustment for other metabolic syndrome components **[recall; biostatistician to check]**. Association is not incremental prediction, and not evidence of benefit from acting on the number.
- **Decision it would change:** none. Obesity treatment (lifestyle, incretin therapy, surgery) is selected by BMI and complications; IR improves as a consequence **[opinion]**.
- **Evidence strength:** *low*.

## 7. Settings where an insulin measurement does change specialist management (not "IR screening")
1. **Hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia / insulinoma:** insulin and C-peptide drawn during documented hypoglycaemia are part of the Endocrine Society work-up (PMID 19088155). This detects insulin excess, not IR. Established practice.
2. **Factitious/exogenous insulin:** high insulin with suppressed C-peptide (PMIDs 19088155, 23413806).
3. **Lipodystrophy and extreme/syndromic IR:** the multi-society guideline (PMID 27710244) supports metabolic assessment (diabetes, triglycerides, hepatic fat). Severe hyperinsulinaemia supports the diagnosis in context and metreleptin is targeted therapy in generalized lipodystrophy **[recall]**. Evidence is consensus and case series, *moderate to low*.
4. **Diabetes classification:** C-peptide (PMID 23413806).

## 9. Open questions
1. Does IR-stratified selection of pioglitazone (or metformin in PCOS) improve outcomes versus selection by glycaemia alone? IRIS cannot answer this.
2. Do any IR indices add incremental discrimination or reclassification beyond BMI, waist, glucose, HbA1c, lipids and liver fibrosis scores? Needs the biostatistician (C-statistic, NRI, calibration).
3. Can insulin assays be harmonized so one HOMA-IR cutoff is portable (PMID 17363420 shows they are not)?
4. Ethnicity- and age-specific reference ranges: none guideline-endorsed.
5. Role of IR in predicting response to GLP-1/GIP agonists: not searched.
6. Search gaps: no ClinicalTrials.gov registrations retrieved; ADA pharmacologic section, AACE statements and the full PCOS recommendation text not read. All [recall] items need confirmation.

## 10. Key conclusions
- Measuring IR (HOMA-IR, fasting insulin, TyG) is not guideline-supported as a diagnostic or treatment-selection tool in prediabetes, T2D, PCOS or MASLD; evidence that it changes management is low.
- Insulin and C-peptide measurement is clearly useful for a narrow set of questions (hypoglycaemia work-up, diabetes classification, exogenous insulin, lipodystrophy); these should not be conflated with general IR testing.
- Assay variability, absent cutoffs, beta-cell failure and exogenous insulin each limit interpretation.
- Prognostic information from IR phenotypes (Wagner, Ahlqvist) is interesting but not shown to improve outcomes when used to guide therapy.
