# IR measures as clinical tools: biostatistics/epidemiology appraisal

Role: ir-biostatistician. Scope: incremental prediction, cutoffs, assay variability, bias. Coverage and reimbursement are out of scope.
Date of run: 2026-10-18. Independent run; no files in ir-team/ other than this one were read.

Method and limits (read first):
- Sources: PubMed (via PubMed MCP) and Consensus. Amass was not used. All numbers below come from abstracts returned by the tools. I did not read full texts, so covariate sets, calibration and risk-of-bias details are not verified.
- Every citation below was returned by a tool in this session. Items I know exist but could not retrieve and verify (for example the IRIS primary NEJM report, Framingham/ARIC/MESA HOMA-IR reclassification analyses) are listed under "Not retrieved", with no numbers quoted.
- PubMed search through this connector is strict AND matching, so recall is limited. Treat the evidence base below as a purposive sample, not a systematic search.

## 1. Bottom line

1. Association evidence is consistent: higher HOMA-IR is associated with incident T2DM, non-fatal MACE and hypertension (meta-analyses PMID 36181637, 23300589).
2. Prediction evidence is thin and mostly negative where tested: in the best-designed incremental-value analysis I found, HOMA-IR added nothing material beyond glucose/HbA1c/BMI for diabetes prediction (PMID 38191206). For the TyG index (a surrogate), pooled C-index gain over Framingham was about 0.0004 to 0.005, with very low GRADE certainty (PMID 42569452).
3. Decision-impact evidence is almost absent. No decision-curve or net-benefit analysis of HOMA-IR was found. The only randomized evidence that uses an IR threshold (IRIS, HOMA-IR > 3.0 as an entry criterion) tests pioglitazone in a selected stroke/TIA population. It does not test whether measuring IR improves outcomes (PMID 25458644; secondary analysis PMID 30734043).
4. Cutoffs are not transferable. Reference limits are assay-, population-, age- and BMI-specific, derived data-adaptively, rarely externally validated. Inter-laboratory CV of HOMA-IR is about 25 percent (PMID 28660493, 34380182, 26070771, 17272483).
5. Measurement error is large: within-person CV of HOMA-IR about 30 percent, ICC 0.70 over about 6 weeks (PMID 29618016). The original HOMA paper itself reported estimate CV of about 31 percent and said this limits use (PMID 3899825).

## 3. Incremental-value findings (summary)

- HOMA-IR for incident diabetes: no material gain in AUC or reclassification once FPG, HbA1c and BMI are in the model (PMID 38191206). Fasting glucose alone moved AUC 0.79 to 0.84 and continuous NRI to 0.61, so most of the "IR signal" for diabetes is already in glycemic variables. This is expected because HOMA-IR is computed from fasting glucose and insulin.
- HOMA-IR for CVD: association evidence (RR per SD about 1.46 for CHD in non-diabetic adults; HR about 1.46 for non-fatal MACE) but I did not retrieve any analysis reporting delta-C, NRI with clinically meaningful cut points, calibration, or decision curves for HOMA-IR over pooled-cohort or Framingham models. Gast and colleagues only infer that adding HOMA-IR "may be useful" from RR (PMID 23300589).
- TyG as surrogate: delta C-index about 0 to 0.005 over Framingham, very low GRADE (PMID 42569452). In NHANES older non-diabetic adults, no significant discrimination gain for TyG or HOMA-IR over traditional factors (PMID 42702103).
- Positive incremental-value claims for TyG appear in small, single-center, high-risk cohorts (for example C-statistic 0.63 to 0.66 with 73 events in 546 patients, reported by Consensus for a Cardiovasc Diabetol 2025 paper whose PMID I did not retrieve; not relied on).
- Pattern: C-index gain near zero with some significant NRI/IDI is the usual signature of over-optimism in reclassification metrics. NRI is sensitive to model miscalibration, category definitions, and events-vs-non-events weighting (PMID 24592497, 25042215, 21204120, 25176621, 25724473).

## 4. Cutoff derivation and validation

- Derivation methods in the evidence: population percentiles (95th/97.5th of a "healthy" subset), ROC-optimal thresholds against a proxy outcome (NAFLD, cardiometabolic risk factor, clamp-defined IR), and unsupervised clustering. These yield cutoffs of about 1.6 to 3.4 in adults and 1.7 to 3.4 in children, depending on population, assay and criterion (PMID 28660493, 34380182, 26070771, 35888595, 34888081).
- Overfitting: the NAFLD cutoff (1.9; sens 87 percent, spec 79 percent) was derived and reported in the same 368 participants (PMID 28660493); ROC-optimal cutoffs in small clamp samples (n=102) carry very wide intervals (PMID 35888595). I found no external validation of any HOMA-IR cutoff in the retrieved sources.
- Variation by group: percentiles vary by age, sex and BMI category in children (PMID 26070771); the reference-limit moves with how stringent the "healthy" definition is (97.5th percentile 2.35 in the strictest German reference group, PMID 34380182). I did not retrieve data-driven comparisons across Black, Hispanic, South Asian, East Asian adults; this is a gap, not evidence of equivalence.
- IRIS used HOMA-IR > 3.0 as an enrollment rule (PMID 25458644). That is a trial eligibility threshold and does not establish a clinically validated treatment threshold; note it is higher than most population upper limits above (1.9 to 2.35).
- Cutoff-based classification inherits measurement error: if within-person CV is about 30 percent (PMID 29618016) and inter-lab CV is about 25 percent (PMID 28660493), a person near a cutoff can cross it on repeat testing or at another lab.

## 5. Assay and measurement variability

- Inter-assay: among-assay CV of insulin immunoassays 12 to 66 percent (median 24 percent) and a common reference preparation did not fix it (PMID 17272483). Contemporary inter-laboratory CV of HOMA-IR 25 percent, attributable to insulin not glucose (PMID 28660493). Three assays in one cohort gave systematically different HOMA-IR (PMID 34380182).
- Within-person: ICC 0.70 and CV about 30 percent for HOMA-IR over a median 46 days; MDC95 2.3 units (PMID 29618016). For TG/HDL-C, ICC 0.80 and CV 23 percent (same source); the cheaper lipid-based index is more repeatable than HOMA-IR in this study.
- Index validity: HOMA vs clamp correlations r about 0.8 to 0.88 (PMID 3899825, 10857969) but kappa for categorization 0.63 (PMID 10857969), so agreement at a threshold is only moderate. In T2DM with BMI <35, the correlation of HOMA-IR with clamp glucose disposal was -0.324 (PMID 35888595).
- Consequences for risk estimates: random measurement error in the exposure attenuates hazard ratios (regression dilution) and biases cutoff-based classification; with ICC about 0.7 the observed log-HR is expected to be diluted by roughly 30 percent for a single measurement (my inference from ICC; not directly reported by these studies). Single-measurement studies therefore understate the biological association but also reflect what a single clinical test would deliver.
- Mechanistic confounders of HOMA-IR in specific settings: SGLT2 inhibitor treatment shifted the HOMA-IR to insulin-sensitivity relationship (HOMA-IR in controls about 2.45 times SGLT2i-treated for the same clamp ISI, in a very small matched sample, 17 pairs; found via Consensus, PMID not retrieved, so cited descriptively only).

## 6. Design and bias assessment

- Confounding by adiposity: HOMA-IR is strongly correlated with BMI and waist; many studies adjust for BMI, but adjusted estimates in the meta-analyses depend on study-level adjustment, and residual confounding is likely (PMID 36181637, 23300589). Adiposity may be the common cause of IR and outcome; HOMA-IR then adds little over BMI/waist in prediction.
- Mathematical coupling: HOMA-IR = (glucose x insulin)/22.5, so it is partly a function of fasting glucose, a stronger and already-standard predictor for diabetes. Incremental-value tests must adjust for glucose, as done in PMID 38191206.
- Reverse causation and survivorship: in older adults, low HOMA-IR can reflect frailty or insulin deficiency; J-shaped and inverse associations with mortality appear (PMID 42702103). Follow-up exclusions and competing risks matter.
- Selection and spectrum: single-sex, occupational or hospital cohorts (PMID 38191206, 35888595); healthy-volunteer reference samples (PMID 28660493, 34380182); mixtures of diabetic and non-diabetic participants change the cutoffs.
- Imperfect reference standards: HOMA-IR is often used as the criterion for evaluating TyG (PMID 32256572), which inflates or distorts accuracy; the clamp is the only true reference but samples are small.
- Meta-analytic heterogeneity: the HOMA-IR/CVD meta-analysis showed I2 0 percent for HOMA-IR vs 62 percent for glucose (PMID 23300589), but low I2 with few studies has low power; neither review's abstract provides prediction intervals, publication-bias tests or GRADE for HOMA-IR (PMID 36181637). The TyG incremental meta-analysis was GRADE very low and sensitive to a single cohort (PMID 42569452).
- Multiple testing and outcome selection: many analyses test several outcomes, indices and subgroups (for example the IRIS adherence and prediabetes subgroups, PMID 30734043) with no pre-specified correction in the abstracts.
- Publication bias: small single-center positive "TyG improves prediction" studies are common in this literature; the 2026 meta-analysis shows aggregate gain close to zero (PMID 42569452). I did not find funnel-plot or selective-reporting assessments for HOMA-IR.
- Evidence type separation: Gast and the 2022 meta-analysis support association (PMID 23300589, 36181637). Hu 2024 and Liu 2026 address prediction (PMID 38191206, 42569452). IRIS is the only decision-impact style evidence, and it is a treatment trial in a selected population, not a test-and-treat trial (PMID 25458644, 30734043).

## 7. Missing analyses (what would change the conclusion)

1. Prospective, pre-registered incremental-value study of HOMA-IR and TyG over contemporary risk equations (PCE/PREVENT for ASCVD; QDiabetes/FINDRISC/ADA-type for T2DM) reporting delta-C with CIs, calibration slope and in-the-large, category-free and clinically-anchored NRI with event/non-event components, IDI, and external validation in at least two ethnically diverse cohorts.
2. Decision-curve/net-benefit analysis at clinically meaningful thresholds (for example 5, 7.5 and 20 percent 10-year ASCVD risk; 20-30 percent 5-year diabetes risk) with a defined action (statin, lifestyle program, metformin) and a test-tradeoff statement. None found for HOMA-IR.
3. Test-and-treat evidence: randomized or well-emulated target-trial comparison of IR-guided vs usual care on patient outcomes. IRIS tests a drug in an IR-selected group, not the test.
4. Measurement-error-corrected risk estimates (regression calibration with repeat measures; multiple-lab calibration) and cutoff reliability (proportion reclassified on repeat testing around 1.9, 2.5, 3.0).
5. External validation of cutoffs by ethnicity, age and BMI with assay-harmonized insulin; reference intervals in non-European adult populations.
6. Head-to-head comparison against cheap alternatives (waist, TG/HDL-C, HbA1c, fasting glucose, TyG) in the same cohort with the same outcome, because the added value of an insulin assay should be benchmarked against zero-cost data already on file.
7. Individual-participant-data meta-analysis of HOMA-IR cohorts with harmonized adjustment (adiposity, glycemia) and explicit prediction intervals, publication-bias testing and risk-of-bias grading (for example PROBAST, QUIPS).
8. Cost-effectiveness is out of scope here; it is noted only because NRI alone cannot support clinical use without decision analysis (PMID 24592497).
