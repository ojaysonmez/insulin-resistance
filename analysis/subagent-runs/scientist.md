# Insulin resistance physiology and what each IR measure captures (independent run, ir-scientist)

Scope: physiology and measurement meaning only. Coverage and reimbursement are out of scope.
Method: every PMID/DOI below was retrieved from PubMed in this session and its title and abstract checked. Claims I could not tie to a retrieved paper are labelled **[uncited]**. They are general physiology I believe to be correct but did not verify here, so treat them as lower confidence. Where the cited paper supports only part of a statement, I say so. No preprints were used.

## 1. Physiology of insulin resistance (IR)

**Definition.** IR is a reduced tissue response to a given insulin concentration. It is not one lesion. It is tissue-specific, pathway-specific and state-specific (fasting vs. insulin-stimulated). Integrated mechanistic review: Petersen & Shulman, Physiol Rev 2018, PMID 30067154, doi:10.1152/physrev.00063.2017.

**Tissues.**
- *Skeletal muscle.* It is the main site of insulin-stimulated glucose disposal. The euglycemic clamp glucose infusion rate (GIR) reflects mostly this tissue. The clamp "primarily reflects muscle" insulin sensitivity (PMID 19487637, doi:10.2337/dc09-0684, states this wording for whole-body sensitivity at its clamp dose).
- *Liver.* Insulin suppresses gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, and drives lipogenesis. Hepatic IR means fasting hepatic glucose production is inadequately restrained for the prevailing insulin. Fasting measures weight hepatic IR more heavily than they weight muscle IR. The fasting hepatic IR index is basal hepatic glucose production x fasting insulin (PMID 19046218, doi:10.1111/j.1464-5491.2008.02597.x).
- *Adipose tissue.* Insulin suppresses lipolysis. Failure to do so raises free fatty acid (FFA) flux to liver and muscle, drives hepatic gluconeogenesis and ectopic lipid deposition, and raises triglyceride (TG) output. Supporting data:
  - Generalised impaired insulin suppression of lipolysis in T2D vs. equally obese non-diabetic people (PMID 11350782, doi:10.1152/ajpendo.2001.280.6.E1000).
  - In obese and T2D adolescents, poor NEFA suppression during the clamp tracked higher fasting TG and ALT (PMID 23861170, doi:10.1111/j.2047-6310.2013.00189.x).
  - Petersen & Shulman (PMID 30067154) argue that adipose lipolysis to hepatic gluconeogenesis crosstalk is under-appreciated.

**Tissues are correlated but not identical.** In 182 non-diabetic Mexican Americans, hepatic and muscle IR were strongly correlated (r=0.68), yet 23% were discordant (PMID 19046218). Isolated IFG subjects show relatively hepatic IR with preserved muscle sensitivity (PMID 21553243, doi:10.1007/s00592-011-0285-x, background statement in its abstract). So "IR" from one test may not describe the tissue that drives a given phenotype.

**Selective hepatic IR.** In T2D models, insulin fails to suppress gluconeogenesis but continues to drive lipogenesis, producing hyperglycemia plus hypertriglyceridemia (Brown & Goldstein, PMID 18249166, doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2007.12.009; animal commentary). Petersen & Shulman offer an alternative explanation for the paradox (PMID 30067154). **Implication:** TG-based surrogates (TyG, TG/HDL) probably reflect hepatic lipogenic and adipose-lipolytic output, not muscle glucose disposal. That is a mechanistic inference I make, not something shown by those papers.

**Lipid mediators and inflammation.** Petersen & Shulman (PMID 30067154) review diacylglycerol, ceramide, acylcarnitine, ER and mitochondrial stress, inflammation, branched-chain amino acids, adipokines and hepatokines, and describe which are well supported vs. contested. Visceral/mesenteric fat lipolysis links to hepatic steatosis and IR in mice, with a human correlative element (PMID 26384383, doi:10.2337/db15-0941). I treat this as plausible, mostly preclinical.

**Compensatory hyperinsulinemia and beta-cell failure.**
- Normal glucose tolerance is maintained while beta cells increase output. Fasting glucose rises only when compensation fails. DeFronzo's Banting Lecture frames T2D as multi-organ failure, including muscle, liver, adipose and beta-cell dysfunction (PMID 19336687, doi:10.2337/db09-9028).
- Early beta-cell defects precede frank hyperglycemia, e.g. impaired early-phase secretion already in IFG (PMID 21553243) and disordered secretory oscillations in IGT (PMID 8325993, doi:10.1172/JCI116560).
- Because compensation is a hyperbolic relationship between sensitivity and secretion, insulin level alone is ambiguous: a normal insulin in a person with IR may mean good compensation or early failure. The disposition index is the standard way to express this. It has a pitfall: it uses plasma insulin, which depends on both secretion and clearance (PMID 24937535, doi:10.1210/jc.2014-1515, a pioglitazone trial in IGT where insulin-based and C-peptide-based indices diverged).

**Insulin clearance.** Hepatic extraction removes a large fraction of secreted insulin (a constant 56% in normal monkeys by direct portal and jugular sampling; PMID 16353355, doi:10.1002/j.1550-8528.1993.tb00619.x). Extraction falls with obesity (PMID 16353355, monkeys; PMID 1879308, doi:10.1016/0168-8227(91)90086-s, women with upper-body obesity, inferred from C-peptide/insulin ratio). It also differs by ancestry (PMID 9005969, see section 4). Consequence: peripheral hyperinsulinemia = increased secretion + reduced clearance in unknown proportions. C-peptide is not extracted by the liver to a meaningful degree, so it separates secretion from clearance. **[uncited]** for the statement that C-peptide has negligible hepatic extraction; the papers above use the C-peptide/insulin ratio on that assumption but do not test it.

**Pulsatility.** Insulin is secreted in rapid pulses (~10 min) and slower ultradian oscillations (80-170 min) (PMID 8473402, doi:10.1210/jcem.76.4.8473402). Oscillation control by glucose is disrupted in IGT and T2D (PMID 8325993), and oscillation amplitude falls with age (PMID 8739916, doi:10.1007/BF00403303; PMID 8572190, doi:10.1152/ajpendo.1995.269.6.E1006). Pulsatility also changes with obesity and weight loss (PMID 8964829, doi:10.1210/jcem.81.6.8964829).

## 2. What each measure captures

| Measure | Inputs | State | Dominant tissue/physiology reflected | Where it diverges from clamp | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hyperinsulinemic euglycemic clamp** (reference) | Exogenous insulin infusion, GIR (ideally with tracer) | Steady-state, supraphysiologic insulin; endogenous secretion and hepatic glucose output suppressed | Whole-body glucose disposal, mostly skeletal muscle; with glucose tracer also hepatic glucose production suppression; NEFA suppression gives adipose readout | It is the reference. Limits: dose-dependent (low dose probes liver/adipose, high dose muscle), non-physiologic, does not assess beta-cell function | PMID 382871, doi:10.1152/ajpendo.1979.237.3.E214; PMID 19487637 |
| **HOMA-IR** | Fasting glucose x fasting insulin / 22.5 | Fasting, basal steady state | Feedback loop of basal glucose and insulin; mostly hepatic insulin sensitivity plus basal secretion **[uncited for "mostly hepatic"; see PMID 19046218 for the hepatic index logic]** | Correlation with clamp Rs=0.88 in the original; but model CV 31% (IR) and 32% (beta-cell); no correlation with receptor binding. Insulin assay dependent. Poor when beta-cell secretion is failing (low insulin, high glucose) | PMID 3899825, doi:10.1007/BF00280883 |
| **QUICKI** | 1/[log I0 + log G0] | Fasting | Same information as HOMA (a log transformation of the same inputs) | r=0.78 with clamp vs. 0.57 for minimal model in the derivation cohort (n=56). Same fasting limits. Log form behaves better in obese/diabetic than raw reciprocal (derivation claim) | PMID 10902785, doi:10.1210/jcem.85.7.6661 |
| **Matsuda ISI** | Glucose and insulin at 0, 30, 60, 90, 120 min of 75 g OGTT | Fasting plus post-load | Composite: fasting term weights hepatic, post-load terms weight muscle | r=0.73 with clamp glucose disposal (n=153). A simple mean glucose/insulin ratio had no correlation (r=-0.02). Depends on insulin delivery, which includes clearance | PMID 10480510, doi:10.2337/diacare.22.9.1462 |
| **OGTT insulin curve (shape, 1-h/2-h, peak)** | Serial insulin | Stimulated | Mix of insulin sensitivity, secretion and incretin/gut handling, and hepatic extraction | Not a sensitivity measure on its own; the same curve can mean good compensation or failing secretion. Secretion is better read via C-peptide deconvolution | PMID 21553243; PMID 24937535 |
| **C-peptide** | Fasting or stimulated; ratio to insulin | Fasting or stimulated | Beta-cell secretion (equimolar to insulin, not extracted by liver). C-peptide/insulin molar ratio is a proxy for insulin clearance. Not itself a measure of IR | Not a direct IR test. Rises with IR through compensation; falls in beta-cell failure. Used to correct insulin-based indices for clearance. Confounded by renal function **[uncited]** | PMID 24937535; PMID 21553243; PMID 16353355; PMID 9005969 |
| **TyG** | ln[TG x fasting glucose / 2] | Fasting | Hepatic and adipose lipid-glucose handling; fasting glucose captures hepatic output and TG captures VLDL secretion. No insulin input | r=-0.68 with clamp M, AUC 0.86 in a Mexican cohort (n=99; clamp reference) | PMID 20484475, doi:10.1210/jc.2010-0288; PMID 19067533, doi:10.1089/met.2008.0034; PMID 21665314, doi:10.1016/j.diabres.2011.05.030 |
| **TG/HDL-C** | Fasting lipids only | Fasting | Lipoprotein consequence of hepatic and adipose IR (high TG, low HDL). No glucose or insulin input | In overweight people, sensitivity 64% and specificity 68% at cut-point 3.0 (US units) vs. insulin suppression test. Does not track IR in African Americans (AUC 0.56; sensitivity 17%) | PMID 14623617, doi:10.7326/0003-4819-139-10-200311180-00007; PMID 15983289, doi:10.1001/archinte.165.12.1395 |

Notes on specific cells:
- *Clamp tissue weighting.* The tracer-clamp and OGTT-derived tissue-specific indices (hepatic IR index = basal HGP x fasting insulin; muscle IR = clamp disposal / steady-state insulin) were used in PMID 19046218. OGTT-derived "hepatic" and "muscle" IR indices (Abdul-Ghani formulas) are used in PMID 27931981 (doi:10.1016/j.ando.2016.10.003), where liver-IR classified women had more visceral fat, liver fat and higher TG than muscle-IR women. I did not retrieve the original derivation paper, so I do not cite it.
- *TyG vs. HOMA.* The original TyG paper (PMID 19067533) validated against HOMA-IR, not a clamp. Its optimal cut-off gave sensitivity 84% but specificity 45%, so it is partly circular (HOMA is itself imperfect). The clamp-based validation is PMID 20484475. The Brazilian study (PMID 21665314) used a hyperglycemic clamp and reported only "moderate agreement" in its abstract; the "performs better than HOMA" in the title is the authors' claim.
- *Matsuda includes fasting data from the same sampled insulin*, so it inherits assay and clearance problems.

## 3. Mechanistic implications for clinical meaning

1. **No single test measures "IR".** The clamp measures mostly muscle glucose disposal at a supraphysiologic insulin dose. HOMA/QUICKI measure fasting loop behavior, which is more hepatic and depends on basal secretion. Matsuda blends both. TyG and TG/HDL are lipid-metabolic consequences, closer to hepatic and adipose IR. Since muscle and liver IR are correlated but 23% discordant (PMID 19046218), different tests can legitimately disagree.
2. **Fasting insulin-based indices cannot distinguish secretion from clearance.** Reduced hepatic extraction raises peripheral insulin (PMID 16353355, PMID 1879308, PMID 9005969), which makes HOMA, QUICKI and Matsuda read "more resistant" without any change in tissue sensitivity. Conversely a drug that increases clearance can lower insulin while sensitivity improves (PMID 24937535).
3. **A high insulin can be adaptive or part of the disease.** Whether hyperinsulinemia is a consequence of IR, or contributes to it, is a debated causal question. I did not retrieve a paper that settles it, so I do not claim either direction. **[uncited]**
4. **Beta-cell failure flips the sign.** In people with failing secretion, fasting insulin falls while glucose rises, so HOMA-IR can understate IR. The original HOMA paper treats this as a two-variable model (IR and beta-cell function) (PMID 3899825). That means HOMA-IR is least interpretable at the stage of disease where it may matter most.
5. **Lipid-based surrogates are mechanistically indirect.** TG responds to adipose lipolysis, hepatic lipogenesis, VLDL clearance, diet, alcohol, genetics and drugs. These confounders are not tested in the cited cohorts, but their existence is basic lipid physiology **[uncited]**. Ethnic differences are documented for TG/HDL (PMID 15983289).
6. **Clinical meaning is probabilistic, not diagnostic.** Best cited discrimination figures: TyG vs. clamp AUC 0.86 (PMID 20484475); TG/HDL vs. insulin suppression test sensitivity 64%, specificity 68% (PMID 14623617). These are cross-sectional, single-center, and neither study tests whether acting on the result improves outcomes. I did not find or retrieve outcome evidence in this run, so I make no claim about it.

## 4. Physiological limits on clinical use

| Limit | Evidence and mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Pulsatile secretion** | Insulin pulses every ~10 min plus ultradian oscillations (PMID 8473402). A single fasting sample is a point on an oscillating signal. Pulse amplitude falls with age and in IGT/T2D (PMID 8739916, PMID 8325993), so the variability itself changes with disease. I have no retrieved paper quantifying within-person CV of HOMA-IR from repeat draws **[uncited]**; the original HOMA paper reports model CV ~31% (PMID 3899825). |
| **Hepatic extraction** | ~56% in normal monkeys; falls with obesity (PMID 16353355); lower in people of West African ancestry (25-52% lower basal and postprandial extraction and clearance) with higher insulin at similar C-peptide (PMID 9005969). Peripheral insulin is therefore a poor proxy for secretion or sensitivity across groups. |
| **Assay problems** | The ADA task force found identical samples gave widely disparate results across laboratories, and that cross-reactivity with proinsulin and conversion intermediates varied between assays (PMID 8549870, doi:10.2337/diab.45.2.242). Modern immunometric assays show little proinsulin cross-reaction (PMID 25172526, doi:10.1177/0004563214551613 abstract; PMID 30027696, doi:10.3343/alm.2018.38.6.530 for one platform), but insulin analogues cross-react with some platforms (PMID 25172526). A radioimmunoassay blind to proinsulin gave elevated insulin in IGT and new T2D, contradicting the claim that cross-reactivity explains hyperinsulinemia (PMID 7476300, doi:10.1016/0026-0495(95)90045-4, one Minnesota population, European ancestry). HOMA/QUICKI/Matsuda cutoffs are therefore assay and population specific; I found no retrieved evidence of a universal standardization. |
| **Ethnicity and ancestry** | Hyperinsulinemia with lower extraction in West African ancestry (PMID 9005969). TG/HDL fails to track IR in African Americans (PMID 15983289). Cutoffs derived in Mexican (PMID 20484475, PMID 19067533), US (PMID 14623617) and Brazilian (PMID 21665314) cohorts are not interchangeable. A general cross-ethnic meta-analysis of insulin sensitivity vs. response was not retrieved. |
| **Age** | Oscillation amplitude and secretory responsiveness decline in older adults (PMID 8739916, PMID 8572190), so a given insulin level means different things at different ages. |
| **Pregnancy and other states** | Late pregnancy raises C-peptide response and lowers C-peptide/insulin ratio, i.e. both secretion and clearance change (PMID 1802920, doi:10.1007/BF03347933). Hypertension showed lower C-peptide/insulin ratio with normal C-peptide (PMID 8320421, doi:10.1007/BF03348843). |
| **Tissue-specific blind spots** | A fasting or whole-body index will not say whether liver, muscle or adipose is the driver (PMID 19046218, PMID 21553243). Adipose IR (NEFA suppression) is measured only in research settings (PMID 11350782, PMID 23861170). |
| **Fasting conditions** | Prior diet, sleep, exercise and circadian timing alter glucose regulation (sleep and circadian modulation of glucose tolerance and insulin secretion, PMID 8572190). Standardised fasting draws are assumed; the cited papers do not quantify the effect on HOMA. |
| **Clamp itself** | Resource intensive, so not usable in routine care; supraphysiologic insulin; GIR confounded by glucose effectiveness if no tracer **[uncited]**. |

## 5. Plausible mechanisms not shown to matter clinically

- Selective hepatic IR as the unifying explanation for the TG/HDL and TyG signal: strong in rodents (PMID 18249166), contested in mechanism (PMID 30067154), not shown to make the human surrogates better predictors than simpler measures.
- Mesenteric fat lipolysis driving liver IR (PMID 26384383): mouse data plus a modest human correlation (r about 0.3).
- Hepatic extraction as a modifiable driver of hyperinsulinemia: supported by C-peptide/insulin ratios but those are inferences from peripheral sampling; direct portal sampling was in monkeys only (PMID 16353355).
- That a particular IR phenotype (hepatic vs. muscle) should direct treatment: I found no retrieved clinical evidence either way.

## 6. Key uncertainties

1. No retrieved evidence here on how well any single IR number predicts outcomes or treatment response compared with glucose, HbA1c, waist, or TG. Out of scope for a physiology memo but it limits clinical interpretation.
2. Within-person day-to-day variability of fasting insulin and HOMA-IR was not quantified from a retrieved paper.
3. Insulin assay harmonization status today, and exact cutoffs per platform, not established from retrieved sources.
4. Cross-ethnic and cross-age normative values were not established beyond the individual studies cited.
5. Direction of causality between hyperinsulinemia and IR is unresolved.
6. Several "textbook" physiology points are marked [uncited] above (negligible hepatic C-peptide extraction, renal C-peptide clearance, assay-independent biology of TG confounders). A reviewer should verify them before they are used in a formal document.
7. Citations checked via PubMed abstracts only; I did not read full texts. Cohort sizes and effect figures are those stated in the abstracts.
