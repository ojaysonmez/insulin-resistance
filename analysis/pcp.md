# Primary care view: is measuring insulin resistance (IR) useful?

Role: PCP teammate. Scope: undifferentiated primary care, short visits. Coverage and reimbursement are out of scope.
Date: 2026-10-18.

## 0. Bottom line and evidence limits

**Routine IR measurement (fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, or similar) is not useful in primary care today.** It does not beat BMI/waist, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids, ALT and BP for deciding who gets counseling, metformin, a GLP-1 RA, a screening interval or a referral. I found no trial in which IR-guided care improved outcomes versus usual care.

How far to trust this report:
- Every citation below was checked against PubMed (PMID and DOI returned by the tool), except item 10, which is flagged. Where a claim rests on my clinical reasoning and not on a verified source, it is labeled **[PCP judgment, uncited]**.
- My search was a targeted scan, not a systematic review. Several searches returned nothing usable (DPP secondary analyses of insulin sensitivity, insulin assay harmonization, TyG validation, ADA Standards of Care). I did not fill those gaps from memory. Section 6 lists them as open items.
- The Consensus tool returned papers without PMIDs/DOIs. I cite only those I could confirm in PubMed.

## 1. Verified evidence base

| # | Source | What it supports | PMID / DOI |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Matthews et al., Diabetologia 1985 (original HOMA) | HOMA-IR correlates with the euglycemic clamp (Rs 0.88) but has low precision (CV about 31% for IR); the authors say this limits its use | PMID 3899825; DOI 10.1007/BF00280883 |
| E2 | Hu et al., BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care 2024 (5578 Japanese workers, 94.7% male, 943 incident diabetes) | Adding FPG to a model with BMI and HbA1c raised AUC 0.79 to 0.84 (cNRI 0.61). Adding HOMA-IR and HOMA-beta "did not further materially improve" performance | PMID 38191206; DOI 10.1136/bmjdrc-2023-003680 |
| E3 | Gast et al., PLoS One 2012 (meta-analysis, 65 studies, 516,325 adults without diabetes) | Pooled CHD relative risk per SD: 1.46 for HOMA-IR, 1.21 for glucose, 1.04 for insulin. Association only; the authors say HOMA-IR "may be useful" in risk models, which this study did not test | PMID 23300589; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0052036 |
| E4 | Bonora et al., Diabetes Care 2002 (Verona, 1326 people with T2D) | HOMA-IR independently predicts incident CVD in diagnosed T2D (OR 1.56 per log unit). Not a primary-care screening population | PMID 12087010; DOI 10.2337/diacare.25.7.1135 |
| E5 | Knowler et al., NEJM 2002 (Diabetes Prevention Program, 3234 adults with elevated fasting and post-load glucose) | Lifestyle cut diabetes incidence 58%, metformin 31%. Eligibility was by glucose and overweight, not by IR | PMID 11832527; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa012512 |
| E6 | USPSTF 2021 | Screen adults 35-70 with overweight or obesity for prediabetes/T2D (grade B); offer or refer those with prediabetes to preventive interventions. Selection is by BMI and age, not IR | PMID 34427594; DOI 10.1001/jama.2021.12531 |
| E7 | Teede et al., 2023 International PCOS Guideline (JCEM version) | Evidence is generally low to moderate quality. Recognizes metabolic risk, CVD and sleep apnea as part of PCOS. The abstract does not state a position on IR testing | PMID 37580314; DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgad463 |
| E8 | Peña et al., BMC Med 2025 (adolescent PCOS recommendations from the 2023 guideline) | Adolescent diagnosis rests on irregular cycles plus clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism, not IR. Metformin "could be considered" for metabolic features and cycle regulation. Evidence is low to moderate certainty | PMID 40069730; DOI 10.1186/s12916-025-03901-w |
| E9 | Rinella et al., AASLD Practice Guidance on NAFLD, Hepatology 2023 | Existence of the guidance only. I did not read it, so I make no claim about its IR content | PMID 36727674; DOI 10.1097/HEP.0000000000000323 |
| E10 | Jiao et al., Front Endocrinol 2026 (perspective, found via Consensus; no PMID/DOI retrieved) | Argues routine fasting insulin or HOMA-IR should not be a diagnostic requirement for PCOS, and that IR is "neither universal" nor the sole cause. This is an opinion piece; I could not verify the primary guideline wording | Not verified in PubMed |

Not verified and therefore not relied on: assay-harmonization data, TyG-vs-clamp validation, ADA 2025 Standards text, DPP IR-subgroup analyses.

## 2. Scenario verdicts

"Adds beyond" asks: does the result change what I do, compared with what I already have?

| Scenario | Verdict | Action the IR result would trigger | Adds beyond BMI/glucose/A1c/lipids? | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Adult 35-70 with overweight/obesity (general screening)** | **Don't test IR.** Test glucose or HbA1c | None. Management follows glycemic status (E6) and DPP-type lifestyle programs (E5) | No. In the one reclassification study I found, HOMA-IR added nothing once FPG, HbA1c and BMI were in the model (E2) | Moderate that glucose/A1c-based screening suffices; low for IR specifically (single study, mostly male, Japanese) |
| **B. Prediabetes (by FPG/A1c)** | **Don't test.** Prediabetes already triggers lifestyle referral and consideration of metformin | None beyond that. DPP enrolled by glucose, not IR (E5) | No verified evidence that IR picks who responds better to lifestyle or metformin | Low |
| **C. Normal glucose and A1c, family history of T2D** | **Don't test** | Counsel on weight and activity; repeat glucose/A1c at an interval set by risk. A normal HOMA-IR risks false reassurance; an elevated one has no validated action **[PCP judgment, uncited]** | Unproven | Low |
| **D. PCOS symptoms** | **Don't test IR for diagnosis.** Screen glycemia, lipids, BP, weight, OSA symptoms. Whether IR adds anything for management is unclear | Diagnosis runs on cycles and androgens, not IR (E8). Metformin is an option for metabolic features in adolescents (E8) | An opinion source says no (E10). I did not verify the primary guideline's wording on IR | Low (guideline evidence itself low to moderate, E7, E8) |
| **E. Fatty liver on imaging or raised ALT** | **Don't test IR.** Use glycemic status, lipids and fibrosis risk stratification | Risk-stratify fibrosis and treat cardiometabolic risk. I did not verify what AASLD (E9) says about IR | No verified evidence **[PCP judgment, uncited]** | Low |
| **F. Hypertriglyceridemia / low HDL** | **Don't test IR** | Lipids are already the cardiometabolic signal. Check glucose/A1c, TSH, alcohol, medications **[PCP judgment, uncited]** | Fasting insulin would add little | Low |
| **G. CVD risk estimation (primary prevention)** | **Don't test** | Use standard risk calculators and risk enhancers | HOMA-IR is associated with CHD (E3, E4). I found no study showing better discrimination or reclassification than standard calculators, and no outcome trial of acting on it | Low to moderate for association; low for decision value |
| **H. Established T2D** | **Don't test in primary care** | Treat per glycemia, CV and renal risk | HOMA-IR predicts CVD in T2D (E4), but I found no verified evidence it changes drug choice | Low |
| **I. Lean patient, normal labs, wellness testing** | **Don't test** | None. Overdiagnosis and anxiety risk **[PCP judgment, uncited]** | No | Low |

Net: I have no scenario where I would order IR routinely. The "unclear" cases are specialist-referred ones (PCOS with a discordant picture, suspected lipodystrophy or hyperinsulinism). Those belong to the endocrinologist's report, not primary care.

## 3. Practical considerations (PCP judgment unless cited)

- **Precision:** the original HOMA paper reported a CV of about 31% for the IR estimate (E1). A single value that noisy is a weak basis for a diagnostic label.
- **Fasting and logistics:** needs a fasting draw and an insulin assay, which adds a morning visit. Assays differ across labs. I could not retrieve harmonization data, so treat cutoffs as lab-specific **[uncited]**.
- **No agreed cutoff:** I found no verified, population-validated HOMA-IR threshold for primary care. Any threshold I used would be arbitrary.
- **Counseling value:** "you have insulin resistance" may motivate some patients, but weight, activity and glucose are what I can act on. No verified trial shows the label itself changes outcomes.
- **Harms:** false reassurance when glucose is normal, overdiagnosis, supplement and fad-diet exposure, follow-up burden.

## 4. Does IR change drug initiation, interval or referral?

- **Metformin / GLP-1 RA initiation:** indications rest on glycemic status, BMI and comorbidity (E5, E6, E8). I found no verified evidence that IR level should select the drug or dose.
- **Screening interval:** set by glucose/A1c result and risk factors. No verified IR-based interval.
- **Referral:** a discordant story (severe acanthosis, symptoms of marked hyperinsulinism, atypical PCOS) justifies specialist work-up regardless of a HOMA value **[PCP judgment, uncited]**.

## 5. Evidence needed before routine use

1. Prospective primary-care cohorts showing incremental discrimination and net reclassification for T2D and CVD over a model with BMI, FPG, HbA1c, lipids and BP. E2 is one negative example; E3 shows association only. I found no positive reclassification study.
2. A standardized insulin assay with reference intervals by sex, age and ancestry.
3. A randomized trial where IR-guided care (lifestyle intensity, metformin or GLP-1 RA choice) beats usual glucose/BMI-guided care on diabetes incidence or cardiovascular events.
4. Evidence that identifying IR in normoglycemic people changes behavior or outcomes without harm.

None of these exists, to the best of my verified search.

## 6. Open items for other teammates

- Biostatistician: assay CV, cutoff derivation, and a systematic look for positive incremental-prediction studies (my search found only E2).
- Endocrinologist: what IR testing adds in referred PCOS or MASLD patients; the actual PCOS guideline text on IR.
- Scientist: tissue-specific IR and what fasting HOMA-IR reflects (hepatic vs muscle).
- Skeptic: please check E2's generalizability (94.7% male, Japanese workers) and that E10 is a perspective, not the guideline.
