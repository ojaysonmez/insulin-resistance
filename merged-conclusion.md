# Merged conclusion: clinical utility of insulin resistance (IR) measurement (fasting-based and surrogate measures; see the scope note)

Sources: Set A (session reports), Set B (subagent reports), comparison.md, six skeptic reviews (ir-skeptic.md, subagents/ir-skeptic-session-review.md, subagents/ir-skeptic.md, ir-skeptic-new-roles.md, subagents/ir-skeptic-new-roles.md, ir-skeptic-merged.md), and one steelman run (ir-steelman.md) with its skeptic review (ir-skeptic-steelman.md), whose corrections are applied below. The searcher and chemist roles were each run twice (ir-team/ and ir-team/subagents/). Coverage and reimbursement are out of scope.
Provenance: every figure and PMID comes from those files. The skeptics checked well over 100 PMIDs against PubMed and found no fabricated citations; Crossref retraction checks found none (two corrections on the EASL guideline). Neither report set is a systematic review, and the sets share tools and role files, so their agreement is not fully independent.

I (the main session) have still not read those four reports myself. A later use-case review (usecases-verified.md, with its skeptic review ir-skeptic-usecases.md) read several guideline passages verbatim and resolved some items previously marked [single-source]; those updates are applied below and marked "use-case review". ## Bottom line
*Scope: the bottom lines are about fasting insulin and fasting-based indices (HOMA-IR) and the non-insulin surrogates TyG and TG/HDL-C. Post-load measures (OGTT insulin, Matsuda, OGIS, mixed-meal) were evaluated only through a few trials and use cases, and C-peptide only for niche uses; neither was weighed as a predictor here (the two post-load prediction results in the files are both nulls). The confidence figures below were set before this rescoping and have not been re-derived. See `reviews/scope-audit.md`.*

1. **No established clinical utility today for routine fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG/TG-HDL-C measurement** in primary care, prediabetes, T2D, PCOS, MASLD or obesity. Post-load insulin, OGTT-derived indices and mixed-meal measures were examined in only a few studies and not weighed separately for routine use (the two post-load prediction results in the files are nulls); C-peptide and insulin are evaluated only for the uses in point 3. Confidence about 80-85%. This is a judgement of how likely the verdict is to survive further checking, not a measurement. The upper end comes from the two Set B skeptics (about 85%); the first-round skeptic gave about 80%. It is scoped to *routine* measurement and predates the guideline items listed below, which refine it and do not reverse it.
2. **"Shown not to add value" is a weaker claim: about 45-50%** (the steelman skeptic's own figure is 45-55%). It is a judgement of how likely the claim is to be true, not a computation. It covers a mixed set of measures. For fasting insulin/HOMA-IR there are several small or narrow incremental-value nulls (WHI, Hu, NHANES); for TyG the results conflict (ATTICA against Liu 2026, NHANES and Barzegar); and the one prespecified interaction test (DIETFITS, n=609, P = 0.47) used 30-minute post-glucose insulin and a weight-loss outcome, so it speaks to post-load insulin, not to fasting measures. What lowers it is the conflicting incremental-value evidence (ATTICA, with the qualifiers below, and one other cohort report gains over a current score) and decision-curve papers on IR indices that exist but were not screened and do not test the decision to measure. The oncology interaction trials, PERSON and TriMaster do **not** lower it: the two oncology trials point in opposite directions and are chance-compatible by their own authors' statements, PERSON's result contradicted its hypothesis, and TriMaster leans toward routine features. There is no adequately powered outcome-based test of measuring fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG, and the searches that would find one were incomplete.
3. **Insulin (drawn during hypoglycaemia) and C-peptide (random, fasting or mixed-meal stimulated) have clinical roles, mostly not as IR screening:**
   - Hypoglycaemia/hyperinsulinism work-up (a hypersecretion question, not IR) and diabetes classification (C-peptide). The use-case review lists 49 uses in total: 11 established, 16 limited, 9 research-only, 8 unsupported and 5 not evaluated (most of the unsupported rows mean no use was found in the sources read, not that a use was tested and rejected), and all 11 established are secretion, hypersecretion or classification uses. Two notable ones not listed above: C-peptide of at least 0.2 pmol/mL on a mixed-meal test is an FDA label eligibility criterion for teplizumab in recently diagnosed stage 3 type 1 diabetes (ages 8-17, within 8 weeks, accelerated approval on a C-peptide surrogate), and C-peptide enters the Igls consensus classification of islet and pancreas graft function. The skeptic also found Japanese national C-peptide criteria for fulminant and slowly progressive type 1 diabetes (limited).
   - Severe or genetic IR syndromes: the Japan Diabetes Society 2022 criteria use fasting insulin above 30 µU/mL as a major sign (PMID 35463863; table text read verbatim in the use-case review, which notes the value "may depend on the course of treatment for diabetes", so it is not simply invalid on exogenous insulin; single-nation expert criteria, no accuracy data). The lipodystrophy guideline cited by the endocrinologist (PMID 27710244) uses no insulin assay for diagnosis or metreleptin eligibility; it diagnoses by clinical phenotype plus genetics and uses leptin, HbA1c and triglycerides, with a high insulin dose requirement (about 200 U/day or more) only as a clinical clue, so the insulin claim is resolved as unsupported and evidence there is low.
   - IR-threshold treatment selection: the IRIS entry criterion (HOMA-IR above 3.0) is already embedded in guideline wording. ADA Standards 2026 Rec 3.13 says pioglitazone "may be considered" in people with stroke history and evidence of IR and prediabetes, and AACE 2022 R 9.4 (Grade A, BEL 1) says it "should be considered" in people with IR, prediabetes or T2D and prior TIA/stroke (both read verbatim by the use-case skeptic; the ADA text describes IRIS HOMA-IR of 3.0 or more). Neither names an assay or threshold, so no guideline requires a fasting insulin or HOMA-IR *measurement*.

## What is well supported
- **No guideline read requires a fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or OGTT-derived estimate, or ties one to a change in treatment.** The nearest ties are EASL's weak "may be considered" recommendation (below) and the JDS fasting-insulin criterion for severe IR syndromes, which triggers genetic and antibody testing; neither changes treatment or work-up as documented. C-peptide is covered in point 3 and the use-case list.
  - PCOS: the 2023 guideline (practice points 1.9.12 and 3.1.10, full text read by skeptics) says insulin assays are of limited clinical relevance and not recommended in routine care. These are consensus practice points, and the stated reason is assay inaccuracy, not proof of no benefit. The guideline does not specify fasting or post-load insulin, or an index.
  - MASLD: the EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 full text contains a weak, consensus-based (level of evidence 3, 92% agreement) recommendation that HOMA-IR or OGTT-derived estimates "may be considered to clarify metabolic dysfunction" in suspected MASLD without T2D. It is not linked to a change in fibrosis work-up or therapy, which still runs FIB-4 then elastography. Abstract-only readings missed this.
  - Paediatric obesity: the Endocrine Society 2017 guideline (PMID 28359099) Rec 1.7 recommends against measuring insulin concentrations in children and adolescents evaluated for obesity (Rec 1.7, strong, moderate quality), calling insulin "a research tool with no clinical value for evaluation of obesity" and saying fasting insulin to diagnose IR in general practice "should be abandoned" (the rationale names fasting insulin; OGTT insulin is not addressed in the text read). Read verbatim from the raw text in the use-case review, so no longer single-source; it is still one 2017 guideline, and the AAP 2023 guideline could not be retrieved.
  - Paediatric MASLD: the Italian paediatric endocrinology society 2024 position paper (PMID 39380079) says HOMA-IR is "clinically valuable" in suspected MASLD that meets none of the five criteria. It traces to the multisociety Delphi nomenclature consensus (PMID 37364790), which only says metabolic dysfunction "may be early MASLD and prompt additional testing (eg, HOMA-IR and OGTT)" at clinical-team discretion and excludes HOMA-IR from the criteria for cost and between-laboratory variability. The Italian paper overstates its source; both are weak and permissive and neither ties a measurement to a management change.
  - ADA/EASD 2023 precision-medicine consensus (PMID 37794253) is quoted by the Set A searcher as giving TyG "moderate evidence" for CVD prediction. This is about prediction, not reclassification, and should be read next to the Liu 2026 and ATTICA nulls below.
  - Limits of "no recommendation found": Set A regex-scanned the full text of 16 documents (AASLD 2023, ADA 2026 sections 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 14, ADA 2025 section 2, others). Set B scanned only the first 100k characters of ADA 2026 sections 2, 3 and 10 (of 159k, 275k and 373k). The two sets used different ADA years. A negative is therefore regex- or window-limited, not read. Documents not read in full: ESC 2021, AHA 2023, AACE 2026, ADA/EASD 2026 consensus, the 2018 cholesterol guideline (Set A), and NICE, KDIGO and ISPAD (Set B).
- **Incremental prediction is thin where tested, for fasting insulin/HOMA-IR; results for TyG conflict; post-load insulin and C-peptide were not weighed as predictors in this conclusion.** The files hold two post-load prediction results not weighed here: Health ABC (PMID 19700136; abstract level; heart-failure outcome), where an OGTT measure (not specified in the files), fasting insulin and HOMA-IR added to fasting glucose did not improve prediction, so it is also a fasting null; and the Gutt index in Framingham (PMID 17533210; the biostatistician reports no gain, but the evidence searcher notes it was not verified whether insulin was included). Both are nulls.
  - Hu 2024 (PMID 38191206; 5,578 workers, 94.7% male, one Japanese cohort): the base model had BMI, HbA1c and standard covariates; adding HOMA-IR and HOMA-β did not materially improve it, while adding fasting glucose raised AUC 0.79 to 0.84 (cNRI 0.61). The Set A full-text scan reports a small but statistically significant NRI/IDI gain for one HOMA model (NRI 0.13, IDI 0.04; not re-verified), so the wording is "much less than glucose, and nothing beyond it", not zero.
  - Liu 2026 (PMID 42569452): TyG added to Framingham gave a pooled C-index gain of 0.0004 (-0.0092 to 0.0100), very low GRADE, 0.0051 (0.0007-0.0094) without one dominant cohort; the abstract also reports IDI/NRI gains for TyG quartiles.
  - Barzegar 2020 (age under 60 only) fits "small or no gain". **ATTICA does not** (PMID 41178693, abstract; corrected after the steelman run): TyG at or above 8.46 added to SCORE2 gave delta C 0.054 in the derivation cohort and 0.044 in an external validation cohort, NRI 0.67 and 0.47, composite CV events, n=1,677 and 1,237. The numbers were verified against the full text (skeptic). Qualifiers: the cutoff 8.46 was derived in the first cohort, so the 0.054 and NRI 0.668 are in-sample; the "validation" set is two pooled Athens hospital clinic cohorts (dyslipidaemia and hypertension units) with only 63 events and a delta C interval whose lower bound is 0.002; the marker is binary near the cohort median; SCORE2 was applied to a cohort with mean age 43.9 and a range below its validated 40-69; the NRI type is unstated (values typical of an inflated category-free NRI); no calibration or decision-curve analysis; and TyG uses triglycerides and glucose, not insulin, so the gain may not be IR. A second cohort (PMID 39117485, n=1,970, 15-year MACE, continuous TyG over SCORE risk factors, not the SCORE2 score) gave delta C 0.005 (P = 0.042). Pooled Liu 2026 was against Framingham. These are different designs, scores and codings, so read them as "results disagree", not as one spread of a single quantity, and not as "the largest reported gain".
  - NHANES (PMID 42702103; non-diabetic adults 45 or older): outcome is cardiovascular and cerebrovascular **mortality**, not incident disease. Neither TyG nor HOMA-IR improved the C-statistic, and under competing risks higher HOMA-IR had a *lower* subdistribution hazard with a J-shaped pattern, consistent with reverse causation.
  - WHI women (PMID 25944628): HOMA-IR and fasting insulin lost association after HDL-C adjustment. HDL-C is part of the IR phenotype, so read this as "redundant given HDL-C", not "unrelated to CVD".
  - Set A biostatistician also cites Framingham Offspring (PMID 17533210, AROC unchanged when HOMA-IR or insulin added) and Wang 2022 (PMID 35700159, per-SD HR about 1.04 in normal glucose tolerance with a CI including 1). Skeptic files disagree on some Wang figures, and the Set A searcher lists 17533210 as "not verified whether insulin was included", so check both before citing.
- **Association is real but modest.** HOMA-IR is linked to incident T2D (HR 1.87), non-fatal MACE (HR 1.46) and hypertension (HR 1.35) in a 38-cohort meta-analysis (PMID 36181637), with *no* association with fatal MACE. It survives adjustment for waist and lipids in at least one cohort (San Antonio, PMID 12087016: OR 2.52 to 1.94). For TyG, the Liu 2022 meta-analysis rated certainty moderate only for CAD, very low for other CVD outcomes, with no association with CV mortality (HR 1.10, 0.82-1.47) or all-cause mortality (HR 1.08, 0.92-1.27). In UK Biobank the highest-quartile TyG HR was 1.19, mostly mediated by dyslipidaemia, diabetes and hypertension.
- **IRIS (PMID 26886418)** shows pioglitazone helps post-stroke patients selected by HOMA-IR above 3.0 (stroke/MI 9.0% vs 11.8% over 4.8 years, HR 0.76; absolute reduction 2.8 points, NNT about 36, against more weight gain, oedema and fractures). It had no non-IR comparator, so it cannot show testing is needed. The trial had 13 prespecified subgroups with no significant heterogeneity (multiplicity-adjusted P = 0.94 per the figure legend; whether HOMA-IR is one of them is unconfirmed). Spence 2019 (PMID 30734043) is titled as a post hoc analysis of IRIS; its glucose, HbA1c and adherence subgroups were prespecified in the analysis plan, and prediabetes ITT HR was 0.70 (0.56-0.88), so glycaemic category may be a substitute selector.
- **Surrogates are modest.** TyG versus clamp r about -0.68 (in-sample cutoff, n=99; AUCs across studies 0.59-0.88). TG/HDL-C fails in African Americans (PMID 15983289). HOMA-IR versus clamp: r about -0.82 and category agreement kappa 0.63, which the authors called good. OGTT-derived indices are in the files but not weighed here (for example Matsuda versus clamp r 0.73, PMID 10480510).

## Added by the evidence-searcher and clinical-chemist runs

**Measurement (clinical chemist, checked by three skeptics)**
- Within-person CV of insulin and HOMA-IR is about 25-30% (EuBIVAS 26.7%, ARIC 30.4%, minimum detectable change 2.3). QUICKI's 4.1% reflects its log transform and does not make it more informative. HOMA-IR, QUICKI and FIRI are monotone transforms of one another (PMID 29940866).
- Insulin assays disagree despite WHO 66/304 traceability claims: twelve immunoassays differed from an isotope-dilution MS reference by about -298 to +303 pmol/L (PMID 40802520). HOMA-IR ranges across 11 assays in Manley 2008 (PMID 18535197): 0.8-2.0 in normoglycaemia, 1.9-3.2 in prediabetes, 1.5-2.9 in T2D. Cutoffs are assay-specific: one assay's 97.5th percentile was 2.35, with systematic differences across three assays (Gutenberg, PMID 34380182).
- Inter-laboratory and inter-assay disagreement for insulin is large but is not one number; it depends on the kit set, the concentration and the study design (corrected after the round 2 skeptic review, with full texts read for Zhou and Rosli):
  - Zhou 2022 (PMID 35177563, full text): 94 Chinese laboratories, five pooled sera. Mean inter-laboratory CV 21.8% and inter-system CV 22.4%, but by pool the ranges were 8.4-64.3% and 10.7-58.3%: the lowest pool (below 60 pmol/L, the fasting range) was at about 58-64% and the other four averaged about 11-13%.
  - Marcovina 2007 (PMID 17272483, abstract): among-assay CV 12-66%, median 24%, for 10 assays (older generation; a common reference preparation did not lower it).
  - Tohidi 2017 (PMID 28150502, abstract): 8 assays, lowest and highest median differing by a factor of 1.8. It reports no CV.
  - Rosli 2022 (PMID 35622611, full text): seven kits on one pooled serum. The 1.7-23.2% is the RSD within each kit (replicate precision), not between-kit disagreement. Between kits the pool read from about 18 to 150 pmol/L (8.3-fold); the ratio of inter-run means in Table 1 is 5.8-fold (28.38 vs 4.88 uIU/mL). The three automated CLIA kits (Siemens, Beckman, Roche; hospital platforms) span about 1.5-fold; the large spread comes from two ELISAs traceable to WHO IS 83/500 rather than 66/304. Manufacturers' unit conversion factors differed (6.0, 6.945, 7.0).
  - Read these as differently designed studies, not as one 20-25% value, and do not cite a single universal inter-laboratory CV. Scope: this disagreement matters for cutoff transport and for pooling studies; for a patient followed at one laboratory and assay, within-person variation (EuBIVAS 26.7%) plus that laboratory's own imprecision is what matters.
  - For HOMA-IR specifically, only one small study (Isokuortti, PMID 28660493, DOI not recorded in the files; 25%, ten samples, seven labs) gives a figure.
- C-peptide recalibration cut inter-system CV from 24.5% to 5.7% (Zhou), but its own within-person CV is also about 24% (n=12, young, older RIA-era assay). Insulin therapy and analogue cross-reactivity make insulin- and C-peptide-based indices uninterpretable; LAMP excluded insulin users for this reason.
- Reference change value (RCV) must be asymmetric for a log-normal index. Skeptic recomputation (Z = 1.96, within-person CV 26.7% from EuBIVAS, PMID 38987271): about +107% / -52% with no analytical CV, about +110% / -52% with an assumed analytical CV of 5%, and about +86% / -46% one-sided (Z = 1.645). For QUICKI (within-person CV 4.1%) about +12% / -11% if analytical CV is ignored or propagated through the log transform; applying a 5% analytical CV directly gives about +20% / -16%. Assumptions: log-normality, stationarity over the interval, and the same laboratory and assay. The empirical type 2 diabetes figure (+90% / -47%; Jayagopal 2002, PMID 12401750, DOI 10.2337/diacare.25.11.2022; abstract) comes from 12 diet-controlled women, so its similarity to the computed value is a coincidence of populations, not confirmation, and Jayagopal's own non-diabetic controls were far less variable. One chemist run (Set B) applied a symmetric 70-80% figure; the other wrote the asymmetric form. These are derived numbers, not published ones.
- Single-sample misclassification depends on distance from the cutoff (analytic error ignored): a true value 20% above the cutoff reads below it about 24% of the time, 1.5x above about 6%, 2x above under 1%. Classification is unreliable near a cutoff and acceptable far from it. "A fixed cutoff cannot be stable" overstates it, and no published study quantifies cutoff crossing.
- Not established: "no reference measurement procedure for insulin exists", and "LC-MS/MS is no better than immunoassay" (different metrics on different samples). Wording after the peer exchange and the round 2 skeptic review (not yet re-reviewed):
  - Isotope-dilution MS is called "a reference measurement procedure for insulin" in Staten 2010 (PMID 20040676, a short commentary, full text) and was the comparator in Miller 2009 (PMID 19325009, abstract).
  - Against that, the 2024 LC-MS/MS paper (PMID 38549041, full text) says there is no reference measurement procedure available; Rohlfing 2025 (PMID 40802520, full text) says there is "still no JCTLM-listed reference method for insulin" and no listed certified reference materials, and calls its own comparator "not an established reference method"; Liu 2025 (PMID 40353876, abstract) presents a candidate procedure; Zhou 2022 independently states the JCTLM position. These are author statements. The JCTLM database itself was not queried by anyone, and "no commercial assay is standardised to a reference procedure" is our inference from them. For C-peptide, a University of Missouri procedure is JCTLM-listed (2024 paper, full text).
  - The same 2024 paper reports a median inter-laboratory insulin CV of 22.2% for an LC-MS/MS protocol (three laboratories, leftover clinical samples, single-point calibration), so standardising to MS would not by itself remove variability.
  - Current-platform data, Rohlfing 2025 (full text; 12 immunoassays on 38 analysed sera spanning 2-1364 pmol/L; anonymised, not independent): differences from the MS comparator ranged from about -298 to +303 pmol/L. The +303 came from the lowest samples (below 10 pmol/L, n=3) and the -298 from high concentrations. "1 of 12 in full agreement" is a Passing-Bablok criterion on 38 samples; four more assays agreed on intercept only.
- Pre-analytical: haemolysis lowers insulin; delayed centrifugation loses insulin and C-peptide (small studies). Not covered: TyG repeatability (none found), current-platform proinsulin cross-reactivity, variation in adults with obesity and in PCOS, and Matsuda reproducibility in adults. Post-load (OGTT-derived, not fasting-based): WBISI, which has the Matsuda index formula by naming convention (the paper's own formula was not seen), was more reproducible than HOMA-IR and QUICKI in 257 children and adolescents aged 8-17 without known diabetes (PMID 39726936, DOI 10.1155/2024/2136173, full text; Spearman comparison, ICC table not read; not better than FGIR or 1/fasting insulin in the 71 normal-weight participants). In 89 adults, as reported by that paper's authors, fasting indices had smaller coefficients of variation than OGTT-derived indices (Hudak, PMID 34679116, not read for this point). Test-retest data for other post-load indices exist in the files and are not weighed here: OGIS CV about 6.5-7.8% (PMIDs 34679116, 21945562), Stumvoll ISI 14.4% (21945562) and 2-hour insulin (8721775).
- Net: a single fasting insulin or HOMA-IR cannot reliably classify an individual near a cutoff or detect individual change. This is the strongest measurement conclusion in the project; it is established for fasting measures. For post-load indices, reproducibility has been measured and is mixed (OGIS good in adults; WBISI better than fasting indices in children, with adult data pointing the other way; Stumvoll moderate, 2-hour insulin worse than fasting insulin in Mooy: 95% of test-retest differences below 125% of the median versus 61%), but classification at a cutoff and detection of individual change were not tested.

**Trials and interaction (evidence searcher, checked by three skeptics; most rows abstract-level)**
- **No randomized trial comparing IR-measurement-guided care with usual care was found.** This negative is the best supported (many convergent searches, using HOMA-IR, fasting insulin and IR-guided terms). The evidence searcher also lists a registered randomized crossover comparing mixed-meal, fasting, OGTT and clamp measures (ACTRN12624000306516, n=255); it is a diagnostic-comparison design, not an outcome trial, and was not independently checked. Closest items:
  - ISRCTN11215387 ("ITT-IR", Wakayama Rosai Hospital): cluster-randomized open trial of an "intention to treat insulin resistance" policy (self-monitoring, visceral-fat follow-up, diet, non-insulinotropic drugs) versus conventional diabetes care, 2016-2021, no publication found; the record shows an unexplained three-month recruitment window with 757 final enrolment, and allocation is not triggered by a fasting insulin or HOMA-IR laboratory measurement (the measure is not stated in the record).
  - PLIS (PMID 34531293): a multi-component phenotype (secretion, sensitivity, liver fat), so it tests different contrasts and is not evidence of care guided by a fasting insulin or HOMA-IR measurement.
- **Registered trials (retrieved by the searcher; independently confirmed by a skeptic only for NCT07710274 and NCT06856291):**
  - CTRI/2026/09/117733: HOMA-IR above 3.8 (men) or 3.46 (women) decides metformin versus voglibose; the only registered trial where a HOMA-IR value decides treatment; not yet recruiting.
  - IRCT20170812035642N11: personalised diet by metabolic profile; recruiting.
  - NCT07710274 (PRISM-I): IR-enriched, HOMA-IR is the primary outcome; recruiting.
  - ISRCTN74144960 (MET-HFpEF): insulin resistance by FIRI (fasting-based, 2.7 or more) is one of three entry routes; recruiting.
  - NCT06856291: observational, completed. NCT07412028 (AP-HP): severe genetic IR within classic PCOS.
- **Randomized or trial-based interaction evidence (abstract-level, mostly weight-loss or glycaemic outcomes, not hard outcomes).** Measure used, per the files: DIETFITS (30-minute post-glucose insulin), Gardner 2016 (insulin AUC median split) and PERSON (OGTT-derived muscle versus liver IR) are post-load; Cornier 2005 used fasting-insulin strata (below 10 versus above 15 uIU/mL); the 2025 Obesity RCT used IVGTT-derived insulin sensitivity and acute insulin response; IRIS, LAMP, the presurgical metformin trial, LIRA-ADD2, the MA.32 analysis and TriMaster's cluster approach used HOMA-IR or fasting insulin; the GRADE row's P = 0.009 heterogeneity is for HOMA2-%S, with a separate C-peptide response analysis; for DPP k-means, the PCOS IPD meta-analysis ("baseline serum insulin", sampling time not stated), ODYSSEY and the semaglutide versus dapagliflozin SIRD/SIDD row the measure is not determinable from the files:

- **LAMP (PMID 42145088):** interaction P = 0.02 at HOMA-IR 2.5 (continuous spline P = 0.07, nonlinear P = 0.02), post hoc, no multiplicity adjustment, 3-month follow-up with early termination for loss of funding, local unstandardised assays. Analysis was 510 of 636: 114 excluded for insulin use in the preceding week and 12 for missing glucose or insulin. The overall effect was already positive (HR 0.48); in the non-IR group the estimate favoured control (11.5% vs 9.5%, HR 1.22, 0.47-3.16); control event rates were 18.1% in IR vs 9.5% in non-IR, so the interaction is partly a prognostic imbalance. Do not quote its crude absolute risk reduction.
- **eGDR-based analyses** (FIDELITY, FINEARTS-HF) do not use an insulin assay. **ODYSSEY OUTCOMES** (PMID 42291078, 11,837 patients): the abstract says sdLDL-C and LDL-C relationships with MACE were similar in patients with greater or lesser insulin resistance, and the treatment effect did not vary by LDL-C or sdLDL-C. It is not a treatment-by-IR test and, if anything, leans toward no IR modification. It was found by a skeptic after the searcher's own over-narrow string missed it.

**How far the searcher's negatives can be trusted.** The skeptics put the searcher's "not found" list at about 55-60%. Its negative on trials of fasting-insulin or HOMA-IR-guided care is reasonably supported. Its claims of no decision-curve analysis (the steelman found about 145 PubMed records on IR and decision curves, mostly prediction-model papers from Chinese cohorts and NHANES, titles screened only; none was shown to compare against a standard risk score or to test the decision to measure), no delta-C study over PCE/SCORE2 (ATTICA is one; see above), and no baseline HOMA-IR interaction in the CV outcome trials rest on result sets of hundreds of records of which only the top ~10 were read, plus strings that were too narrow or broken (a six-way conjunction that could not return anything; a 246-record reclassification string never screened). Read them as "not found in the screened records".

## Added by the steelman run (reviewed by a skeptic; PMIDs checked, no retractions or corrections in Crossref)
The steelman searched for the strongest case that measuring insulin resistance (fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, surrogates and post-load indices) is useful. It confirmed that routine measurement of the fasting and surrogate measures still has no established utility and found one error of fact in this file (ATTICA, corrected above). Its skeptic then found several overstatements in the steelman's own additions, which are corrected here. Read levels: ATTICA, PERSON, the MA.32 insulin/HOMA analysis, the presurgical metformin follow-up and the MAFLD criteria table were read in full text by the skeptic; everything else below is abstract-level.
- **Oncology.** The two breast-cancer metformin RCTs are in the trials table with their caveats. They are discordant (benefit at high HOMA in one; none at high HOMA and possible harm at low in the other), exploratory, chance-compatible and surrogate- or multiplicity-limited. They show that such analyses exist, not that insulin or HOMA changes a treatment effect. Do not read them together as "oncology is an opening".
- **Psychiatry.** TRIO-BD (PMID 35120288, n=45): the published primary is a post-randomisation split of metformin-arm "converters" vs non-converters; the randomised metformin-vs-placebo MADRS contrast is not in the abstract and not checked. Pioglitazone in depression (PMID 26602230, n=37, entry across the insulin-sensitivity range): the finding is a within-treatment association with post-randomisation change in glucose metabolism, not a baseline-IR x treatment interaction. Hypothesis-generating at most.
- **MAFLD definition.** The 2020 international consensus (PMID 32278004; criteria table read in full) lists HOMA-IR of 2.5 or more as one of seven optional metabolic risk criteria (waist, BP, triglycerides, HDL, prediabetes, HOMA-IR, hs-CRP); any two suffice in lean adults, so an insulin assay is never required. Definitional, not a management use; Korean authors criticise it as rarely used in practice.
- **Mendelian randomization** (PMID 32398352; numbers verified): genetically predicted IR phenotypes (instruments are fasting insulin, HDL-C and triglycerides) carry OR 1.79 for CAD and 1.80 for small-artery stroke. The lipid instruments have their own pathways, so this does not separate IR from lipid biology, and causal plausibility does not imply that measuring fasting insulin or HOMA-IR is useful.
- **Far from the cutoff, the variability argument weakens, but only for severe or syndromic IR and lipodystrophy work-up.** It does *not* hold for IRIS-type entry: that is a threshold decision at HOMA-IR 3.0, so people with true values from 3.0 up to about 4.5 are close to it and a meaningful share would be misclassified. The steelman's statement that HOMA-IR above 3.0 marks the top quartile in people without diabetes is sourced: the IRIS authors write that the threshold "was chosen because it identifies the highest quartile among populations without diabetes", citing two references (use-case review).
- **Other settings, mostly constructs other than an insulin assay:**
  - Bariatric remission scores (PMIDs 22963817, 32873586): the Diabetes Surgery Score includes C-peptide with BMI, duration and age, with no incremental C-peptide analysis; diabetes duration alone had AUROC 0.84 at 2 years against 0.79-0.88 for the five scores and was no worse at 10 years. Not support for C-peptide utility.
  - eGDR in type 1 diabetes (PMID 40841970, observational).
  - Longitudinal HOMA-IR change tracking NAFLD fibrosis score (PMID 34674510, n=15,728, 86% men): exposure and outcome share components (glucose; BMI, glucose, AST/ALT), so some association is mechanical. It supports an association between two changes only, not monitoring value.
  - ICU and neonatal model-based insulin sensitivity (STAR) and a commercial fasting test (Quantose IR): titles only, leads.
- **Points that go the team's way:** TODAY (insulin sensitivity did not differ by race and ethnicity), LIRA-ADD2SGLT2i, TriMaster's routine-features model, and PCOS predictor studies all favour clinical features over IR.
- **Not tested by anyone** (searches were too weak to count as absence): early-pregnancy IR measures (fasting insulin or HOMA-IR; the sources do not fix the measure) for predicting gestational diabetes; IR-selected trials in dementia and HFpEF; STAR-type or closed-loop RCTs of insulin-sensitivity measurement; PCOS (a 12-record string not fully screened as described). Several steelman "survived" claims rested on zero-hit or top-5/8 screens of 286-305 records and belong here, not under surviving absences.
- **Steelman limits:** top 5-10 titles per search were screened; ICTRP, CTIS, Embase, Cochrane and Chinese databases were not searched; the ADA/EASD 2026 consensus, AACE 2026 algorithm and AHA 2023 CKM advisory could not be retrieved; it did not try to find more evidence against utility. Not read in full by anyone: TriMaster, MR, TRIO-BD, pioglitazone-depression, the NAFLD cohort, the two C-peptide papers.
- **Two different studies would help, answering two different questions.** (1) Routine verdict: the pre-specified incremental-value study already listed as open gap 2 (delta C, category-based and category-free NRI, calibration, decision curve, over PCE/SCORE2, external diverse cohort), plus any trial of measurement-guided care. (2) One niche: the steelman's proposed post-stroke/TIA pioglitazone trial enrolling all HOMA-IR strata with a second randomization of "measure and treat if HOMA-IR is above 3.0" against "treat by glycaemic category". The niche trial would answer whether the IR value or the glycaemic category identifies responders (Spence 2019 suggests the latter may), but it would leave the routine verdict unchanged, needs a sample several times IRIS's size with no obvious sponsor, and its requirement of an assay traceable to an isotope-dilution reference is hard to meet (no JCTLM-listed insulin reference method exists, though an isotope-dilution MS comparator has been used in research).

## What is not supported as stated
- That IR **phenotypes** carry decision-relevant prognostic information (Set A endocrinologist, citing Wagner 2021). Those clusters were built from imaging, genetics and OGTT variables, not an IR index.
- That "IR testing adds nothing to PCOS management." The guideline says not to test routinely; the stratified-metformin evidence is the PCOS IPD interaction (RR 1.03) and the DPP cluster result above, neither an outcome trial.
- **Grade "Moderate" for "don't test" verdicts** (Set B PCP). Use Set A's "Low"; the ceiling is set by absent trials.
- The claim that 1-hour OGTT glucose is better supported than insulin (the cited paper compares it with IFG, not insulin).
- Both sets' physiology claim that the clamp is muscle-weighted and HOMA-IR hepatic-weighted rests on weak or uncited sources. It is plausible and textbook-standard, but unsourced here, and the team never agreed whether surrogate indices represent whole-body or basal/hepatic IR.

## Corrections to carry forward
- "IR is not in the MASLD guideline" is wrong (see above). "High" evidence for a negative claim is the wrong kind of grade; it should be "not tested".
- The scientist's "TyG vs clamp AUC 0.86" is the top of a range (0.59-0.88) from an in-sample cutoff. The biostatistician's gloss of Bonora's kappa 0.63 as "substantial misclassification" reverses the authors' "good agreement"; whether 0.63 is adequate for a clinical threshold is open.
- PMID 12087016 is the San Antonio Heart Study, not IRAS; the association was only modestly reduced after adjustment.
- The ARIC Schmidt 2005 paper did not test insulin as a predictor and should not be cited as evidence about HOMA-IR increment.
- The EASL MASLD guideline appears as the full guideline in two journals (PMIDs 38851997, 38852583) plus an executive summary (38869512).
- The Isokuortti "HOMA-IR 2.0 in Helsinki reads 1.3-2.1 elsewhere" illustration is not about one patient and should not be cited as such.
