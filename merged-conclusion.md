# Merged conclusion: clinical utility of insulin resistance (IR) measurement (fasting-based and surrogate measures; see the scope note)

Sources: Set A (session reports), Set B (subagent reports), comparison.md, six skeptic reviews (ir-skeptic.md, subagents/ir-skeptic-session-review.md, subagents/ir-skeptic.md, ir-skeptic-new-roles.md, subagents/ir-skeptic-new-roles.md, ir-skeptic-merged.md), and one steelman run (ir-steelman.md) with its skeptic review (ir-skeptic-steelman.md), whose corrections are applied below. ## Bottom line
*Scope: the bottom lines are about fasting insulin and fasting-based indices (HOMA-IR) and the non-insulin surrogates TyG and TG/HDL-C. Post-load measures (OGTT insulin, Matsuda, OGIS, mixed-meal) were evaluated only through a few trials and use cases, and C-peptide only for niche uses; neither was weighed as a predictor here (of the two prediction results once counted as post-load, Health ABC measured no post-load insulin, and the Gutt index in Framingham was a null for incident type 2 diabetes, not cardiovascular disease). The confidence figures below were re-derived on 2026-10-23 after the rescoping and reviewed by a skeptic (`reviews/confidence-rederivation-2026-10-23.md`, `reviews/skeptic-stage3-2026-10-23.md`); they remain judgements. See `reviews/scope-audit.md`.*

1. **No established clinical utility today for routine fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG/TG-HDL-C measurement** in primary care, prediabetes, T2D, PCOS, MASLD or obesity. The same holds in cardiology for cardiovascular risk estimation and for treatment choice. The risk tools in current use take no fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, TyG or TG/HDL-C input: PREVENT (PMID 37947085), which the 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidaemia and 2026 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN CKM guidelines recommend, and SCORE2 (ESC 2021). Neither 2026 guideline was found to recommend measuring fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG (dyslipidaemia guideline: publisher-page excerpts only; CKM guideline, PMID 42265997: PMC full text regex-scanned, not read end to end). The CKM guideline calls high triglycerides and low HDL-C 'markers of insulin resistance' and covers them through the standard lipid panel, not through a TG/HDL-C ratio. The exception is the post-stroke IRIS niche in point 3. Specialty reviews (2026-10-24) found the same in hepatology, pregnancy and postpartum care, kidney disease and transplantation, oncology (including PI3K/AKT-inhibitor hyperglycaemia) and adult antipsychotic monitoring: the decision points read run on glucose, HbA1c, BMI, lipids, fibrosis tests, histology or kidney function, not on fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG. Most of these guideline negatives are excerpt- or term-scan-level. The exceptions are a paediatric antipsychotic guideline (CAMESA) that recommends fasting insulin to trigger an OGTT, and EASL passages that list insulin-resistance tests as options; neither ties a value to treatment (see 'What is well supported'). Post-load insulin, OGTT-derived indices and mixed-meal measures were examined in only a few studies and not weighed separately for routine use (the two post-load prediction results in the files are nulls); C-peptide and insulin are evaluated only for the uses in point 3. Confidence about 80-85%. This is a judgement of how likely the verdict is to survive further checking, not a measurement. The upper end comes from the two Set B skeptics (about 85%); the first-round skeptic gave about 80%. It is scoped to *routine* measurement and predates the guideline items listed below, which refine it and do not reverse it.
2. **"Shown not to add value" is a weaker claim, and it differs by measure: about 40% for fasting insulin and HOMA-IR, about 30% for TyG** (the earlier pooled figure was 45-50%; the steelman skeptic's own figure was 45-55%; DIETFITS used post-load insulin and no longer counts for fasting measures). It is a judgement of how likely the claim is to be true, not a computation. It covers a mixed set of measures. For fasting insulin/HOMA-IR there are several small or narrow incremental-value nulls (WHI, Hu, NHANES); for TyG the results conflict (ATTICA against Liu 2026, NHANES and Barzegar); and of the interaction tests found, DIETFITS (n = 609, P = 0.47; the insulin interaction was specified before data were examined but added after the trial began) used 30-minute post-glucose insulin and a weight-loss outcome, so it speaks to post-load insulin, while the MA.32 baseline fasting insulin and HOMA analyses (protocol-specified, distant recurrence-free survival, 72 unadjusted tests) found no consistent benefit at any level and are compatible with chance. What lowers it is the conflicting incremental-value evidence (ATTICA, with the qualifiers below, and one other cohort report gains over a current score) and decision-curve papers on IR indices that exist but were not screened and do not test the decision to measure. The oncology interaction trials, PERSON and TriMaster do **not** lower it: the two oncology trials point in opposite directions and are chance-compatible by their own authors' statements, PERSON's result contradicted its hypothesis, and TriMaster leans toward routine features. There is no adequately powered outcome-based test of measuring fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG, and the searches that would find one were incomplete. The figure is about cardiovascular and type 2 diabetes outcomes. For liver outcomes (no study of fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or TyG over FIB-4 or elastography found; one PubMed string, zero records) and for early-pregnancy prediction of GDM (no incremental-value study in four cohort abstracts), the claim is 'not tested', not 'shown'.
3. **Insulin (drawn during hypoglycaemia) and C-peptide (random, fasting or mixed-meal stimulated) have clinical roles, mostly not as IR screening:**
   - Hypoglycaemia/hyperinsulinism work-up (a hypersecretion question, not IR) and diabetes classification (C-peptide). The use-case review lists 50 uses in total: 11 established, 17 limited, 10 research-only, 10 unsupported and 2 not evaluated (most of the unsupported rows mean no use was found in the sources read, not that a use was tested and rejected), and all 11 established are secretion, hypersecretion or classification uses. Two notable ones not listed above: C-peptide of at least 0.2 pmol/mL on a mixed-meal test is an FDA label eligibility criterion for teplizumab in recently diagnosed stage 3 type 1 diabetes (ages 8-17, within 8 weeks, accelerated approval on a C-peptide surrogate), and C-peptide enters the Igls consensus classification of islet and pancreas graft function. The skeptic also found Japanese national C-peptide criteria for fulminant and slowly progressive type 1 diabetes (limited).
   - Severe or genetic IR syndromes: the Japan Diabetes Society 2022 criteria use fasting insulin above 30 µU/mL as a major sign (PMID 35463863; table text read verbatim in the use-case review, which notes the value "may depend on the course of treatment for diabetes", so it is not simply invalid on exogenous insulin; single-nation expert criteria, no accuracy data). The lipodystrophy guideline cited by the endocrinologist (PMID 27710244) uses no insulin assay for diagnosis or metreleptin eligibility; it diagnoses by clinical phenotype plus genetics and uses leptin, HbA1c and triglycerides, with a high insulin dose requirement (about 200 U/day or more) only as a clinical clue, so the insulin claim is resolved as unsupported and evidence there is low.
   - IR-threshold treatment selection: the IRIS entry criterion (HOMA-IR above 3.0) is already embedded in guideline wording. ADA Standards 2026 Rec 3.13 says pioglitazone "may be considered" in people with stroke history and evidence of IR and prediabetes, and AACE 2022 R 9.4 (Grade A, BEL 1) says it "should be considered" in people with IR, prediabetes or T2D and prior TIA/stroke (both read verbatim by the use-case skeptic; the ADA text describes IRIS HOMA-IR of 3.0 or more). Neither names an assay or threshold, so no guideline requires any insulin-resistance *measurement*.

## What is well supported
- **No adult guideline read makes a recommendation that requires a fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or OGTT-derived estimate, and no guideline read ties one to a change in treatment.** One paediatric guideline does recommend fasting insulin (fasting-based): the Canadian CAMESA guideline for children and youth on second-generation antipsychotics (2011; PMID 21804853, no DOI in the record; PMC3143700, full-text excerpts) grades fasting insulin at baseline and 3 months 'STRONG 1A' for olanzapine, 'WEAK 3' (risperidone 'WEAK 2B' at 12 months) for risperidone, quetiapine and clozapine, and 'Not recommended' for aripiprazole. Its grades rate the evidence 'that the specific side effect occurs', and its authors state 'there is no evidence on the outcome of monitoring for these side effects'. Its management paper (PMID 21804854, republished as PMID 23115501; no DOI; PMC3223901 excerpts) says that with normal fasting glucose, 'If the fasting insulin is above the upper limit of normal for the assay being used, consider the oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) and specialist consultation (Grade of recommendation: 3)'; metformin is tied to impaired fasting glucose, not to insulin. So a high value changes work-up, not treatment. NICE CG155 (children and young people) monitors fasting glucose or HbA1c and lipids only (excerpts). Among adult guidelines the nearest ties are EASL's weak 'may be considered' recommendation and its 'possible MASLD' passage (below) and the JDS fasting-insulin criterion for severe IR syndromes, which triggers genetic and antibody testing; neither changes treatment; the JDS criterion and the EASL passage change only the diagnostic work-up. C-peptide is covered in point 3 and the use-case list.
  - PCOS: the 2023 guideline (practice points 1.9.12 and 3.1.10, full text read by skeptics) says insulin assays are of limited clinical relevance and not recommended in routine care. They are practice points, a category the guideline defines as 'Evidence not sought' (Table 1), distinct from its consensus recommendations; so they record the guideline group's judgement that 'clinically available insulin assays are of limited clinical relevance', not an evidence review of testing. The guideline's Discussion adds that 'routinely available measures of insulin resistance are inaccurate and clinical measurement is not currently recommended' (prose, not a recommendation, no index named). Metformin is selected by BMI and metabolic-risk group (4.3.1, 4.3.3, 4.4.4) and glycaemic risk before and during pregnancy by 75-g OGTT glucose (1.9.9, 1.9.11, 4.10.7) (PMID 37580314, DOI 10.1210/clinem/dgad463, full text of the recommendations summary, read by the reproductive reviewer and checked by the specialties skeptic, 2026-10-24). The guideline does not specify fasting or post-load insulin, or an index.
  - Antipsychotic monitoring in adults: ADA/APA/AACE/NAASO 2004 Table 3 (PMID 14747245, DOI 10.2337/diacare.27.2.596; publisher-page and court-filed-copy excerpts) and APA 2020 Table 2 (PMID 32867516, DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2020.177901; excerpts; the local full text is the executive summary only, mentions no insulin, HOMA or C-peptide test and does not contain Table 2, so Table 2 is still not re-read) monitor weight, waist, blood pressure, fasting glucose or HbA1c and lipids, with no insulin, HOMA-IR or C-peptide test; insulin resistance appears in APA 2020 only as a risk factor and a mechanism. Adult NICE CG178 and CG185 were not retrieved. Children: see CAMESA above (psychiatry review and specialties skeptic, 2026-10-24).
  - Pregnancy and postpartum: in the excerpts read, ADA Standards 2026 section 15 (PMID 41358885, DOI 10.2337/dc26-S015; full text read: postpartum screening is a 75-g OGTT at 4-12 weeks, rec 15.30, and no fasting-insulin, HOMA-IR, TyG or C-peptide test is named) and IADPSG 2010 (PMID 20190296, DOI 10.2337/dc09-1848; full text read) define GDM detection, early abnormal glucose metabolism, treatment and postpartum follow-up by glucose (OGTT, fasting glucose, A1C) and name no insulin, HOMA-IR, TyG or C-peptide test (in IADPSG, cord-blood C-peptide above the 90th percentile appears only as one of the neonatal outcomes used to choose the glucose thresholds, not as a maternal test); ACOG Practice Bulletin 190 and its 2024 update were not read. Insulin sensitivity changes across gestation (ADA s15 full text: insulin resistance progresses in the second and third trimesters and drops rapidly after delivery; review PMID 38185282, DOI 10.1016/j.cca.2023.117753, abstract), so non-pregnant HOMA-IR cutoffs should not be applied in pregnancy.
  - Kidney disease and transplantation: KDIGO 2022 (PMID 36272764, DOI 10.1016/j.kint.2022.06.008; whole PDF term-scanned, not read end to end) and the 2024 international post-transplantation diabetes consensus (PMID 38171510, DOI 10.1093/ndt/gfad258; PMC full text term-scanned; opinion statements, GRADE judged 'flawed' by its authors) contain no recommendation to measure fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, C-peptide or TyG; the consensus recommends a glucose OGTT from the waiting list onward (nephrology review 2026-10-24; scans not re-checked by the skeptic).
  - MASLD: the EASL-EASD-EASO 2024 full text contains a weak ('may be considered'), 92%-agreement recommendation (level of evidence 3 as reported by earlier readers; the grading symbols are stripped in the PMC text) that HOMA-IR or OGTT-derived estimates "may be considered to clarify metabolic dysfunction" in suspected MASLD without T2D. It is not linked to a change in fibrosis work-up or therapy, which still runs FIB-4 then elastography. Two further passages mention insulin-resistance testing. The definition section says people with steatosis but no cardiometabolic risk factor and no other cause 'may have “possible MASLD” – requiring further tests of insulin-resistance – or cryptogenic SLD', a narrative statement with a citation, not a graded recommendation; it bears on a label, not on fibrosis work-up or therapy. And a 100%-agreement recommendation says comorbidity tests 'are recommended (Table 7)' at diagnosis and follow-up; Table 7 lists, for type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance, fasting glucose, HbA1c and OGTT glucose together with fasting insulin and/or C-peptide, HOMA-IR and OGTT- or mixed-meal-derived indices (fasting-based, C-peptide and post-load measures bundled), introduced as tests 'according to clinical evaluation and [a priori] probability'. Read as options, not a mandate (EASL full text, PMC11299976 = PMID 38852583, DOI 10.1159/000539371; keyword windows read by the hepatology reviewer and the specialties skeptic, 2026-10-24). Abstract-only readings missed this.
  - Paediatric obesity: the Endocrine Society 2017 guideline (PMID 28359099) Rec 1.7 recommends against measuring insulin concentrations in children and adolescents evaluated for obesity (Rec 1.7, strong, moderate quality), calling insulin "a research tool with no clinical value for evaluation of obesity" and saying fasting insulin to diagnose IR in general practice "should be abandoned" (the rationale names fasting insulin; OGTT insulin is not addressed in the text read). Read verbatim from the raw text in the use-case review, so no longer single-source; it is still one 2017 guideline, and the AAP 2023 guideline could not be retrieved.
  - Paediatric MASLD: the Italian paediatric endocrinology society 2024 position paper (PMID 39380079) says HOMA-IR is "clinically valuable" in suspected MASLD that meets none of the five criteria. It traces to the multisociety Delphi nomenclature consensus (PMID 37364790), which only says metabolic dysfunction "may be early MASLD and prompt additional testing (eg, HOMA-IR and OGTT)" at clinical-team discretion and excludes HOMA-IR from the criteria for cost and between-laboratory variability. The Italian paper overstates its source; both are weak and permissive and neither ties a measurement to a management change.
  - ADA/EASD 2023 precision-medicine consensus (PMID 37794253) (full text read) rates TyG (non-insulin surrogate) as having high predictive utility with moderate evidence in its systematic review of CVD prognosis in people with type 2 diabetes ("troponin T and triglyceride-glucose (moderate evidence)"). This is about prediction in people who already have diabetes, not reclassification, and not a recommendation to measure TyG; read it next to the Liu 2026 near-null and the conflicting ATTICA gain below.
  - Limits of "no recommendation found": Set A regex-scanned the full text of 16 documents (AASLD 2023, ADA 2026 sections 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 14, ADA 2025 section 2, others). Set B scanned only the first 100k characters of ADA 2026 sections 2, 3 and 10 (of 159k, 275k and 373k). The two sets used different ADA years. A negative is therefore regex- or window-limited, not read. Documents not read in full: ESC 2021, AHA 2023 (the CKM presidential advisory, DOI 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001184, PMID not found; publisher-page excerpts only; now followed by the 2026 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN CKM guideline, PMID 42265997, DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.03.056, PMC full text regex-scanned for insulin, HOMA, TyG, TG/HDL-C and eGDR terms, none found as a measurement), AACE 2026, ADA/EASD 2026 consensus, the 2018 cholesterol guideline (Set A; replaced by the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidaemia guideline, DOI 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001423 and 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.016, PMID not found, read only as publisher-page excerpts), and NICE and ISPAD (Set B). KDIGO 2022 (PMID 36272764, DOI 10.1016/j.kint.2022.06.008) was later term-scanned in full text (whole PDF, references included; not read end to end) by the nephrology reviewer: no recommendation to measure fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, C-peptide, TyG or eGDR was found (`reviews/specialty-nephrology-2026-10-24.md`).
- **Incremental prediction is thin where tested, for fasting insulin/HOMA-IR; results for TyG conflict; post-load insulin and C-peptide were not weighed as predictors in this conclusion.** The files hold two prediction nulls once counted as post-load, neither weighed here as a post-load insulin result. Health ABC (PMID 19700136, DOI 10.1016/j.cardfail.2009.03.001; full text read; heart-failure outcome in 2386 adults aged 70-79 without diabetes): fasting insulin, HOMA-IR and 2-hour OGTT glucose, each added to fasting glucose, did not improve prediction; this is a fasting insulin/HOMA null (fasting-based), and no post-load insulin was measured. Framingham Offspring (PMID 17533210, DOI 10.1001/archinte.167.10.1068; full text read): for 7-year incident type 2 diabetes (160 cases in 3140 mostly white adults), complex models with fasting insulin, the Gutt index (post-load: OGTT glucose and insulin) or HOMA-IR had AROC 0.850-0.854, not superior to the simple clinical model (0.85); the outcome is diabetes, not cardiovascular disease.
  - Hu 2024 (PMID 38191206; 5,578 workers, 94.7% male, one Japanese cohort): the base model had BMI, HbA1c and standard covariates; adding HOMA-IR and HOMA-β did not materially improve it, while adding fasting glucose raised AUC 0.79 to 0.84 (cNRI 0.61). The Set A full-text scan reports a small but statistically significant NRI/IDI gain for one HOMA model (NRI 0.13, IDI 0.04; not re-verified), so the wording is "much less than glucose, and nothing beyond it", not zero.
  - Liu 2026 (PMID 42569452, DOI 10.3389/fcvm.2026.1804411; full text read; non-insulin surrogate): in a meta-analysis of five cohorts (27,329 participants) that used different base risk models (including SCORE2-Asia, pooled cohort equations and Framingham-type scores), adding TyG changed the C-index by 0.0004 (-0.0092 to 0.0100), I2 99.1%, very low certainty (GRADE); without the Barzegar cohort, which reported an opposing effect, 0.0051 (0.0007-0.0094), which the authors call clinically trivial. In the authors' own CHNS cohort (n=4,665) continuous TyG added nothing (delta C 0.0010, -0.0014 to 0.0034); TyG quartiles gave IDI 0.0034 (P 0.023) and a reported event NRI of 0.0476 whose printed 95% CI (-0.0048 to 0.0913) includes zero although the text says P < 0.05.
  - Barzegar 2020 (age under 60 only) fits "small or no gain". **ATTICA does not** (PMID 41178693, abstract; corrected after the steelman run): TyG at or above 8.46 added to SCORE2 gave delta C 0.054 in the derivation cohort and 0.044 in an external validation cohort, NRI 0.67 and 0.47, composite CV events, n=1,677 and 1,237. The numbers were verified against the full text (skeptic). Qualifiers: the cutoff 8.46 was derived in the first cohort, so the 0.054 and NRI 0.668 are in-sample; the "validation" set is two pooled Athens hospital clinic cohorts (dyslipidaemia and hypertension units) with only 63 events and a delta C interval whose lower bound is 0.002; the marker is binary near the cohort median; SCORE2 was applied to a cohort with mean age 43.9 and a range below its validated 40-69; the NRI type is not stated in the main text (the supplement was not read); values of 0.47 to 0.67 are in the range usually seen with category-free NRI, which is not comparable with category-based NRI and can be large for modest markers (a skeptic inference from the values, not a statement of the paper); no calibration or decision-curve analysis; and TyG uses triglycerides and glucose, not insulin, so the gain may not be IR. For scale: adding urine albumin-creatinine ratio, HbA1c and social deprivation index together to PREVENT raised the C-statistic by 0.004 to 0.005 in external validation (PMID 37947085, abstract). The ATTICA TyG gains (TyG is a non-insulin surrogate) are about ten times larger. The base scores, cohort sizes and marker coding differ, and the validation set had 63 events, so this is only a plausibility check. The view that the gap reflects a weak base model (SCORE2 applied in a general-population cohort with mean age 43.9, partly below its validated range) rather than a large TyG effect is an inference, not a finding of either paper. A second cohort (PMID 39117485, n=1,970, 15-year MACE, continuous TyG over SCORE risk factors, not the SCORE2 score) gave delta C 0.005 (P = 0.042). Pooled Liu 2026 was against Framingham. These are different designs, scores and codings, so read them as "results disagree", not as one spread of a single quantity, and not as "the largest reported gain".
  - NHANES (PMID 42702103; non-diabetic adults 45 or older): outcome is cardiovascular and cerebrovascular **mortality**, not incident disease. Neither TyG nor HOMA-IR improved the C-statistic, and in Fine-Gray competing-risk tertile analyses higher HOMA-IR (fasting-based) had a *lower* subdistribution hazard (Model 3 HR 0.76, 0.62-0.93 for CVD; 0.69, 0.55-0.87 for cardiac mortality), with a J-shape for CVD and cardiac mortality. The authors (full text read) say this does not indicate a protective effect, that the subdistribution hazard differs from the cause-specific hazard and warrants cautious interpretation; "reverse causation" was the project's reading, not theirs.
  - WHI women (PMID 25944628): HOMA-IR and fasting insulin lost association after HDL-C adjustment. HDL-C is part of the IR phenotype, so read this as "redundant given HDL-C", not "unrelated to CVD".
  - Set A biostatistician also cites Framingham Offspring (PMID 17533210; full text read: insulin was included, see the incremental-prediction bullet above; outcome is incident type 2 diabetes) and Wang 2022 (PMID 35700159, DOI 10.2337/dc22-0202; full text read; association, not prediction): HOMA-IR (fasting-based) per SD HR 1.04 (0.92-1.18) in normal glucose tolerance (n=28,890), 1.12 (1.06-1.18) in prediabetes and 1.15 (1.09-1.21) in diabetes (P interaction 0.011); mean follow-up 3.6 years; models adjusted for age, sex, education, alcohol, smoking, physical activity and diet quality, not for BMI, lipids or blood pressure.
- **Association is real but modest.** HOMA-IR is linked to incident T2D (HR 1.87), non-fatal MACE (HR 1.46) and hypertension (HR 1.35) in a 38-study meta-analysis (PMID 36181637, DOI 10.1007/s40292-022-00542-5; full text read; I2 94% for incident T2D, 84% for non-fatal MACE, 16% for hypertension; fasting-based HOMA-IR); for fatal MACE the HR was 1.31 (0.96-1.77), not significant, so "not shown" rather than absent. Cut-offs differed between studies, and the authors describe clinical utility as questionable because predictive values are not standardised. It survives adjustment for waist and lipids in at least one cohort (San Antonio, PMID 12087016, DOI 10.2337/diacare.25.7.1177; full text read; 2,569 adults free of diabetes at baseline, 187 combined cardiovascular events, fasting HOMA-IR: OR 2.52 to 1.94, the fully adjusted 95% CI being 1.05-3.59, close to 1). For TyG, the Liu 2022 meta-analysis rated certainty moderate only for CAD, very low for other CVD outcomes, with no association with CV mortality (HR 1.10, 0.82-1.47) or all-cause mortality (HR 1.08, 0.92-1.27). In UK Biobank the highest-quartile TyG HR was 1.19, mostly mediated by dyslipidaemia, diabetes and hypertension. Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR are also associated with breast-cancer recurrence and cancer mortality in cohorts (PMIDs 11773152 and 22162568 are the same Toronto cohort; PMID 21115858 is a separate cohort of 527 women with 62 deaths, whose all-cause HOMA association was lost when HOMA was log-transformed; PMID 28390156 is all-cancer, not breast-cancer, mortality in NHANES adults, with hazard ratios that varied by insulin assay; all fasting insulin or HOMA; full text read for these four), but the risk findings conflict (PMID 39808164 found none in ER-positive breast cancer), the prognostic association in early breast cancer was confined to the first 5 years (PMID 22162568), and none of these papers tests incremental value over standard prognostic factors.
- **IRIS (PMID 26886418)** shows pioglitazone helps post-stroke patients selected by HOMA-IR above 3.0 (stroke/MI 9.0% vs 11.8% over 4.8 years, HR 0.76; absolute reduction 2.8 points, NNT about 36, against more weight gain, oedema and fractures). It had no non-IR comparator, so it cannot show testing is needed. Entry required a screening HOMA-IR above 3.0 (fasting-based), measured in central laboratories at least 14 days after the index event (full text). Baseline HOMA-IR was median 4.7 (interquartile range 3.8 to 6.2) with pioglitazone and 4.6 (3.7 to 6.2) with placebo (Viscoli 2017, PMID 29084736, DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.117.030458, Table 1, publisher-page excerpts). IRIS therefore says nothing about people at or below 3.0, as the Spence 2019 authors also state. Heart failure of NYHA class III-IV, or class II with reduced ejection fraction, was excluded from the start, and any heart-failure history was excluded later in the trial. Heart failure did not differ between arms (74 vs 71 patients, P = 0.80; full text). Among the 3,851 participants without prior heart failure, 5-year heart-failure risk was 4.1% with pioglitazone and 4.2% with placebo (PMID 29934374, DOI 10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.034763, abstract). The 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline (PMID 35363499, DOI 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001063, publisher-page excerpts) states in its stage B text that 'thiazolidinediones should be avoided in patients with reduced LVEF', and its drug table rates thiazolidinediones a major cause of heart-failure induction or exacerbation (level of evidence A). The trial had 13 prespecified subgroups with no significant heterogeneity (multiplicity-adjusted P = 0.94 per the figure legend; whether HOMA-IR is one of them is not stated in the text read. In the planned secondary analysis with stroke as the outcome (Viscoli 2017, PMID 29084736, publisher-page excerpts), no baseline feature significantly modified the effect except prior stroke (P interaction 0.02) and index event type (P = 0.05), both unadjusted for multiplicity. The Figure 2 legend defines 'HOMA', which suggests a HOMA subgroup, but its interaction P value was not seen. Any such null would apply to stroke, not to the primary stroke-or-MI outcome, and only within the enrolled range (HOMA-IR above 3.0)). Spence 2019 (PMID 30734043) is titled as a post hoc analysis of IRIS; its glucose, HbA1c and adherence subgroups were prespecified in the analysis plan, and prediabetes ITT HR was 0.70 (0.56-0.88), so glycaemic category may be a substitute selector.
- **Surrogates are modest.** TyG versus clamp r about -0.68 (in-sample cutoff, n=99; AUCs across studies 0.59-0.88). TG and TG/HDL-C (non-insulin surrogates) did not identify insulin resistance in 98 African American adults with BMI 25 or more (AUC 0.55 and 0.56; FSIVGTT minimal-model reference, lowest-sensitivity tertile as resistant); fasting insulin had AUC 0.85 in the same sample (PMID 15983289, DOI 10.1001/archinte.165.12.1395, full text read; one small sample). HOMA-IR versus clamp: r about -0.82 and category agreement kappa 0.63, which the authors called good. OGTT-derived indices are in the files but not weighed here (for example the post-load Matsuda composite index versus the clamp, r 0.73 in the 153-subject derivation sample, which was also the test sample, with no independent validation; the fasting-based ISI(HOMA) gave r 0.69 in the same sample; PMID 10480510, DOI 10.2337/diacare.22.9.1462, full text read). In CKD the evidence is thin: in 1,074 Swedish men aged 70 (495 with eGFR below 60, median 46), the vast majority of fasting-based and OGTT-derived indices agreed with the clamp at weighted kappa above 0.6 (OGTT-derived better in the abstract; agreement somewhat lower in CKD per the reviewer, not re-checked), the clamp-derived M and M/I predicted mortality with AUCs of only 0.56-0.58, and neither the clamp nor the indices independently predicted 10-year mortality (PMID 24476695, DOI 10.1038/ki.2014.1, full text read; one author is Baxter-affiliated); in dialysis two small clamp studies differ on HOMA-IR (fasting-based): 12 haemodialysis patients, HOMA-IR versus clamp r -0.58, judged a reasonable alternative (PMID 21441124, DOI 10.2215/cjn.08070910), and 10 peritoneal-dialysis patients, r -0.35, called weak (PMID 26526047, DOI 10.3747/pdi.2013.00296, which used earlier haemodialysis data as a comparison group, not a re-analysis); both are pilot-sized with no outcome data, and both were read at full text.

## Added by the evidence-searcher and clinical-chemist runs

**Measurement (clinical chemist, checked by three skeptics)**
- Within-person CV of insulin and HOMA-IR is about 25-30% (EuBIVAS 26.7%, ARIC 30.4%, minimum detectable change 2.3). The reading that QUICKI's 4.1% reflects its log transform and does not make it more informative is the project's inference; PMID 29940866 does not discuss within-person CV. HOMA-IR, QUICKI and FIRI (fasting-based indices) are one-to-one monotone transforms of one another, so rank-based statistics are identical (PMID 29940866, DOI 10.1186/s12874-018-0521-y, full text; simulation, not clinical data).
- Insulin assays disagree despite WHO 66/304 traceability claims: twelve immunoassays differed from an isotope-dilution MS reference by about -298 to +303 pmol/L (PMID 40802520). Fasting-based HOMA-IR varied up to twofold depending on the insulin assay across 11 assays in 97 fasting adults (Manley 2008, PMID 18535197, DOI 10.2337/dc08-0097, full text, partial; 12 normoglycaemic, 18 prediabetic, 67 T2D). The per-assay ranges previously listed here (0.8-2.0 in normoglycaemia, 1.9-3.2 in prediabetes, 1.5-2.9 in T2D) are unverified: the tables are not in the local text. Cutoffs are assay-specific: one assay's 97.5th percentile was 2.35, with systematic differences across three assays (Gutenberg, PMID 34380182).
- Inter-laboratory and inter-assay disagreement for insulin is large but is not one number; it depends on the kit set, the concentration and the study design (corrected after the round 2 skeptic review, with full texts read for Zhou and Rosli):
  - Zhou 2022 (PMID 35177563, full text): 94 Chinese laboratories, five pooled sera. Mean inter-laboratory CV 21.8% and inter-system CV 22.4%, but by pool the ranges were 8.4-64.3% and 10.7-58.3%: the lowest pool (below 60 pmol/L, the fasting range) was at about 58-64% and the other four averaged about 11-13%.
  - Marcovina 2007 (PMID 17272483, DOI 10.1373/clinchem.2006.082214, full text): 12 assays from 9 manufacturers were evaluated and results reported for 10, with 40 patient samples; among-assay CV 12-66%, median 24%, greatest at low insulin concentrations; a common reference preparation did not lower it (older-generation assays).
  - Tohidi 2017 (PMID 28150502, DOI 10.1080/00365513.2016.1278260, full text): 8 assays on 165 serum samples, lowest and highest median differing by a factor of 1.8; it reports quality-control CVs per assay (intra-assay 0.8-10.1%, inter-assay 1.0-12.9%) but no among-assay CV.
  - Rosli 2022 (PMID 35622611, full text): seven kits on one pooled serum. The 1.7-23.2% is the RSD within each kit (replicate precision), not between-kit disagreement. Between kits the pool read from about 18 to 150 pmol/L (8.3-fold); the ratio of inter-run means in Table 1 is 5.8-fold (28.38 vs 4.88 uIU/mL). The three automated CLIA kits (Siemens, Beckman, Roche; hospital platforms) span about 1.5-fold; the large spread comes from two ELISAs traceable to WHO IS 83/500 rather than 66/304. Manufacturers' unit conversion factors differed (6.0, 6.945, 7.0).
  - Read these as differently designed studies, not as one 20-25% value, and do not cite a single universal inter-laboratory CV. Scope: this disagreement matters for cutoff transport and for pooling studies; for a patient followed at one laboratory and assay, within-person variation (EuBIVAS 26.7%) plus that laboratory's own imprecision is what matters.
  - For HOMA-IR specifically, only one small study (Isokuortti 2017, PMID 28660493, DOI 10.1007/s00125-017-4340-1; 25%, ten samples, seven labs) gives a figure.
- C-peptide recalibration cut inter-system CV from 24.5% to 5.7% (Zhou), but its own within-person CV is also about 24% (n=12, young, older RIA-era assay). Insulin therapy and analogue cross-reactivity make insulin- and C-peptide-based indices uninterpretable; LAMP excluded patients who had received insulin in the week before the event because exogenous insulin affects fasting insulin and HOMA-IR (fasting-based; the LAMP paper does not mention analogue cross-reactivity or C-peptide). Liver and kidney disease add a further limit to fasting insulin and HOMA-IR (fasting-based) and to C-peptide. In compensated cirrhosis, insulin clearance was about 40% lower (correlated with shunt size) and minimal-model insulin sensitivity about half that of controls (n = 9 per group; stimulated iv glucose tolerance test with C-peptide deconvolution and minimal model, not a fasting measure, so clearance is calculated rather than measured; PMID 8077319, DOI 10.1210/jcem.77.5.8077319, full text, PDF partly garbled). After TIPS, fasting insulin rose from 123 to 193 pmol/L and the C-peptide-to-insulin ratio fell 21% while clamp sensitivity did not change; OGTT insulin AUC rose 53%, clamp-calculated insulin clearance was reported unchanged, and the authors suggest the rise reflects less insulin reaching the liver through the shunt (n = 11, uncontrolled before-after study at 6 months; PMID 20489042, DOI 10.1152/ajpgi.00375.2009, full text). In NAFLD an OGTT-derived hepatic insulin clearance index was lower with severe fibrosis (n = 37; the index is the C-peptide-to-insulin AUC ratio over a 75 g OGTT, a surrogate rather than a measured clearance; the same direction in 74 hepatitis C patients; HOMA-IR is not reported in the text read; PMID 27150853, DOI 10.2169/internalmedicine.55.5288, full text, PDF partly garbled). In nondiabetic CKD (mean eGFR 37.6, n = 59), clamp insulin sensitivity and insulin clearance were both lower than in controls (876 versus 998 mL/min), partly explained by lifestyle and body composition, and within CKD neither correlated with eGFR; the adjusted difference in clearance was -85 mL/min (95% CI -160 to -10), and clearance tracked insulin sensitivity and lean mass; people with diabetes were excluded (PMID 26823551, DOI 10.1681/ASN.2015070756, full text). It is plausible, but not shown by these studies, that in compensated cirrhosis, in liver fibrosis and in nondiabetic CKD a high fasting insulin or HOMA-IR (fasting-based) mixes reduced clearance with reduced sensitivity; the evidence is small studies of stimulated, post-load or clamp indices, and the IRIS, MAFLD and JDS cutoffs were not derived in these groups. No study found quantifies the error by fibrosis stage or eGFR (inference from small abstract-level studies; C-peptide in CKD: use-case row 28).
- Reference change value (RCV) must be asymmetric for a log-normal index. Skeptic recomputation (Z = 1.96, within-person CV 26.7% from EuBIVAS, PMID 38987271): about +107% / -52% with no analytical CV, about +110% / -52% with an assumed analytical CV of 5%, and about +86% / -46% one-sided (Z = 1.645). For QUICKI (within-person CV 4.1%) about +12% / -11% if analytical CV is ignored or propagated through the log transform; applying a 5% analytical CV directly gives about +20% / -16%. Assumptions: log-normality, stationarity over the interval, and the same laboratory and assay. The empirical type 2 diabetes figure (+90% / -47%; Jayagopal 2002, PMID 12401750, DOI 10.2337/diacare.25.11.2022; abstract) comes from 12 diet-controlled women, so its similarity to the computed value is a coincidence of populations, not confirmation, and Jayagopal's own non-diabetic controls were far less variable. One chemist run (Set B) applied a symmetric 70-80% figure; the other wrote the asymmetric form. These are derived numbers, not published ones.
- Single-sample misclassification depends on distance from the cutoff (analytic error ignored): a true value 20% above the cutoff reads below it about 24% of the time, 1.5x above about 6%, 2x above under 1%. Classification is unreliable near a cutoff and acceptable far from it. "A fixed cutoff cannot be stable" overstates it, and no published study quantifies cutoff crossing.
- Not established: "no reference measurement procedure for insulin exists", and "LC-MS/MS is no better than immunoassay" (different metrics on different samples). Wording after the peer exchange and the round 2 skeptic review (not yet re-reviewed):
  - Isotope-dilution MS is called "a reference measurement procedure for insulin" in Staten 2010 (PMID 20040676, a short commentary, full text) and was the comparator in Miller 2009 (PMID 19325009, abstract).
  - Against that, the 2024 LC-MS/MS paper (PMID 38549041, full text) says there is no reference measurement procedure available; Rohlfing 2025 (PMID 40802520, full text) says there is "still no JCTLM-listed reference method for insulin" and no listed certified reference materials, and calls its own comparator "not an established reference method"; Liu 2025 (PMID 40353876, abstract) presents a candidate procedure; Zhou 2022 independently states the JCTLM position. These are author statements. The JCTLM database itself was not queried by anyone, and "no commercial assay is standardised to a reference procedure" is our inference from them. For C-peptide, a University of Missouri procedure is JCTLM-listed (2024 paper, full text).
  - The same 2024 paper reports a median inter-laboratory insulin CV of 22.2% for an LC-MS/MS protocol (three laboratories, leftover clinical samples, single-point calibration), so standardising to MS would not by itself remove variability.
  - Current-platform data, Rohlfing 2025 (full text; 12 immunoassays on 38 analysed sera spanning 2-1364 pmol/L; anonymised, not independent): differences from the MS comparator ranged from about -298 to +303 pmol/L. The +303 came from the lowest samples (below 10 pmol/L, n=3) and the -298 from high concentrations. "1 of 12 in full agreement" is a Passing-Bablok criterion on 38 samples; four more assays agreed on intercept only.
- Pre-analytical: haemolysis lowers insulin; delayed centrifugation loses insulin and C-peptide (small studies). Not covered: TyG repeatability (none found), current-platform proinsulin cross-reactivity, variation in adults with obesity and in PCOS, and Matsuda reproducibility in adults. Post-load (OGTT-derived, not fasting-based): WBISI, which has the Matsuda index formula by naming convention (the paper's own formula was not seen), was more reproducible than HOMA-IR and QUICKI in 257 children and adolescents aged 8-17 without known diabetes (PMID 39726936, DOI 10.1155/2024/2136173, full text; Spearman comparison, ICC table not read; not better than FGIR or 1/fasting insulin in the 71 normal-weight participants). In 89 adults, as reported by that paper's authors, fasting indices had smaller coefficients of variation than OGTT-derived indices (Hudak, PMID 34679116, not read for this point). Test-retest data for other post-load indices exist in the files and are not weighed here: OGIS CV about 6.5-7.8% (PMIDs 34679116, 21945562), Stumvoll ISI 14.4% (21945562) and 2-hour insulin (8721775).
- Net: a single fasting insulin or HOMA-IR cannot reliably classify an individual near a cutoff or detect individual change. This is the strongest measurement conclusion in the project; it is established for fasting measures. For post-load indices, reproducibility has been measured and is mixed (OGIS good in adults; WBISI better than fasting indices in children, with adult data pointing the other way; Stumvoll moderate, 2-hour insulin worse than fasting insulin in Mooy: 95% of test-retest differences below 125% of the median versus 61%), but classification at a cutoff and detection of individual change were not tested.

## Added by the steelman run (reviewed by a skeptic; PMIDs checked; no retractions; Crossref lists corrections for Hopkins 2018 and Claxton 2015, not read)
The steelman searched for the strongest case that measuring insulin resistance (fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, surrogates and post-load indices) is useful. It confirmed that routine measurement of the fasting and surrogate measures still has no established utility and found one error of fact in this file (ATTICA, corrected above). Its skeptic then found several overstatements in the steelman's own additions, which are corrected here. Read levels: ATTICA, PERSON, the MA.32 insulin/HOMA analysis, the presurgical metformin follow-up and the MAFLD criteria table were read in full text by the skeptic; everything else below is abstract-level.
- **Oncology.** The two breast-cancer metformin RCTs are in the trials table with their caveats. Other metformin cancer trials add no selection evidence: in preoperative window studies in breast and endometrial cancer (for example PMIDs 22933030, 24972190; full text read) fasting insulin or HOMA (fasting-based) was an outcome that metformin lowered; in the breast study baseline insulin and HOMA were not significantly associated with Ki67 change, while exploratory subgroups linked baseline fasting glucose at or above the 5.3 mmol/L median (p=0.02) and an on-treatment insulin fall (TUNEL, p=0.001) to tumour response, in small subgroups the authors say must be interpreted with caution; and the STAMPEDE metformin arm in metastatic prostate cancer (PMID 40639383, DOI 10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00231-1; full text read) was null for overall survival (HR 0.91, 0.80-1.03), showed a progression-free survival signal in high-volume disease (HR 0.76, 0.64-0.90; reported interaction p=0.012) and tested no insulin or C-peptide subgroup. They are discordant (benefit at high HOMA in one; none at high HOMA and possible harm at low in the other), exploratory, chance-compatible and surrogate- or multiplicity-limited. They show that such analyses exist, not that insulin or HOMA changes a treatment effect. Do not read them together as "oncology is an opening".
- **PI3K/AKT inhibitors (drug-induced insulin resistance).** These drugs cause on-target insulin resistance with compensatory hyperinsulinaemia; in mice the insulin feedback partly reactivates tumour PI3K signalling and limits efficacy (Hopkins 2018, PMID 30051890, DOI 10.1038/s41586-018-0343-4, full text; murine and in vitro only). In patients, risk stratification and monitoring use age, BMI, HbA1c and fasting glucose, not an insulin assay (alpelisib Delphi consensus, PMID 38297009, DOI 10.1038/s41523-024-00613-x, full text per the oncology reviewer). Human reports that measured insulin or C-peptide have 14 and 4 patients (abstracts). Mechanism only. What would make it viable: a prespecified analysis in a PI3K/AKT-inhibitor trial testing whether baseline or on-treatment insulin or C-peptide predicts progression-free survival beyond HbA1c and BMI; none was found.
- **Psychiatry.** TRIO-BD (PMID 35120288, n=45): the published primary is a post-randomisation split of metformin-arm "converters" vs non-converters; the randomised metformin-vs-placebo MADRS contrast is not in the abstract and not checked. Entry required HOMA-IR of 1.8 or more (fasting-based). The registered primary outcome (NCT02519543, record excerpts) is whether metformin's effect on MADRS at week 14 'is mediated by conversion of IR to insulin sensitivity', while the published primary compares converters with non-converters pooled across arms; the registry planned 110 participants and records 50 enrolled (45 randomised). With a within-person CV of about 27% for HOMA-IR, conversion across 1.8 on one repeat sample is partly measurement noise (inference). Pioglitazone in depression (PMID 26602230, DOI 10.1016/j.psychres.2015.10.013, full text; n = 37 completers): the overall comparison was null (HDRS decline 24% versus 23%, P = 0.23). An a priori comparison by baseline status found a 7.15-point greater decline with pioglitazone than placebo in the insulin-resistant group (cells of about 9 and 6; no interaction test or P value in the text; the paper's multiplicity threshold was P = 0.006) and none in the insulin-sensitive group. Insulin resistance was defined by any of fasting glucose 100 mg/dL or more, 2-hour OGTT glucose 140 mg/dL or more, fasting insulin 15 mIU/L or more, or HOMA-IR above 3.8 (mixed glucose and insulin criteria), and fasting insulin, fasting glucose, OGTT glucose and HOMA did not change significantly in either arm. The result cannot be attributed to insulin measurement. Hypothesis-generating at most.
- **MAFLD definition.** The 2020 international consensus (PMID 32278004, DOI 10.1016/j.jhep.2020.03.039; criteria table read in full) lists a HOMA-IR score of 2.5 (fasting-based; the "or more" sign is not recoverable from the extracted text) as one of seven optional metabolic risk criteria (waist, BP, triglycerides, HDL, prediabetes by fasting glucose, 2-hour post-load glucose or HbA1c, HOMA-IR, hs-CRP); lean or normal-weight adults need at least two, so an insulin assay is never required. Definitional, not a management use; Korean authors criticise it as rarely used in practice.
- **Mendelian randomization** (PMID 32398352; numbers verified): genetically predicted IR phenotypes (instruments are fasting insulin, HDL-C and triglycerides) carry OR 1.79 for CAD and 1.80 for small-artery stroke (DOI 10.1136/bmjdrc-2020-001217; full text read). In the authors' sensitivity analyses the association was not observed for CAD or MI with 5-SNP instruments for gold-standard measures of IR (p=0.17, 0.12), and they conclude that the major contribution to CAD/MI is via lipids; a 28-SNP instrument outside HDL-C and triglyceride loci gave similar-magnitude estimates in the weighted-median analysis (CAD 1.97, 1.37-2.83), so the sensitivity picture is mixed. The lipid instruments have their own pathways, so this does not separate IR from lipid biology, and causal plausibility does not imply that measuring fasting insulin, HOMA-IR or the lipid surrogates is useful.
- **Far from the cutoff, the variability argument weakens, but only for severe or syndromic IR and lipodystrophy work-up.** It does *not* hold for IRIS-type entry: that is a threshold decision at HOMA-IR 3.0, so people with true values from 3.0 up to about 4.5 are close to it and a meaningful share would be misclassified. The steelman's statement that HOMA-IR above 3.0 marks the top quartile in people without diabetes is sourced: the IRIS authors write that the threshold "was chosen because it identifies the highest quartile among populations without diabetes", citing two references (use-case review).
- **Other settings, mostly constructs other than an insulin assay:**
  - Bariatric remission scores (PMIDs 22963817, 32873586): the Diabetes Surgery Score includes C-peptide with BMI, duration and age, with no incremental C-peptide analysis; diabetes duration alone had AUROC 0.84 at 2 years against 0.79-0.88 for the five scores and was no worse at 10 years. Not support for C-peptide utility.
  - eGDR (non-insulin surrogate from waist-hip ratio, hypertension and HbA1c) in type 1 diabetes (PMID 40841970, DOI 10.1186/s13098-025-01900-8; full text read, observational meta-analysis): pooled HR per unit 0.79 (0.72-0.87) for MACE; in studies with 1,000 or more participants HR 0.83 (0.65-1.06), not significant, although the authors' tests for subgroup differences were non-significant; the authors suggest considering routine eGDR monitoring, which association data do not show to change outcomes.
  - Longitudinal HOMA-IR change tracking NAFLD fibrosis score (PMID 34674510, DOI 10.3803/enm.2021.1110; full text read; n=15,728, 86% men, retrospective, fasting-based; fibrosis judged by NFS and APRI scores only, and the two scores did not agree on whether BMI change or HOMA-IR change fitted better): exposure and outcome share components (glucose; BMI, glucose, AST/ALT), so some association is mechanical. It supports an association between two changes only, not monitoring value. Lower hepatic insulin clearance with advancing liver disease may be a second reason why HOMA-IR and fibrosis scores move together (see Measurement; inference from small abstract-level studies).
  - ICU and neonatal model-based insulin sensitivity (STAR) and a commercial fasting test (Quantose IR): titles only, leads.
- **Points that go the team's way:** TODAY (insulin sensitivity did not differ by race and ethnicity), LIRA-ADD2SGLT2i, TriMaster's routine-features model, and PCOS predictor studies all favour clinical features over IR.
- **Not tested by anyone** (searches were too weak to count as absence): early-pregnancy IR measures for predicting gestational diabetes have association evidence only: four single- or two-centre cohorts measured fasting HOMA-IR at 6-14 weeks (PMIDs 26754418, 34449903, 36675721, 38017369; abstracts), three report AUCs of 0.62-0.81 with in-sample cutoffs from 1.43 to 3.13, none tests added discrimination or reclassification over fasting glucose and BMI, and in one HOMA-IR had no association with GDM in women of advanced maternal age; a 2026 meta-analysis of early-pregnancy fasting TyG, a non-insulin surrogate (PMID 42110441, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1811358; full text read: 220,985 subjects, mostly Asian cohorts, GDM OR 2.51, 95% CI 2.18-2.90) reports associations only, although its authors say TyG can be used for early diagnosis; no decision or outcome study was found, and the benefit of treating glucose-defined early GDM is mixed across outcomes (TOBOGM, PMID 37144983, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2214956, full text read; glucose OGTT criteria, no insulin or C-peptide test: fewer adverse neonatal outcomes with immediate treatment, 24.9% vs 30.5%, adjusted risk difference -5.6 percentage points, 95% CI -10.1 to -1.2, with no difference in pregnancy-related hypertension, 10.6% vs 9.9%); randomised trials selecting or stratifying by fasting insulin or HOMA-IR in dementia (the closest item is a within-arm correlation between baseline insulin resistance and response to 40 IU intranasal insulin detemir, r = 0.54 in 19 people; Claxton 2015, PMID 25374101, DOI 10.3233/JAD-141791, full text read; insulin resistance was HOMA-IR from fasting values, with an OGTT insulin AUC as a second (post-load) index, and the passages read do not say which index the correlation uses; APOE-4 status, not insulin resistance, moderated the main memory effect; correction notice not read; not a stratified or interaction test) and HFpEF (MET-HFpEF, which uses FIRI, a fasting-based index, as one of three entry routes, is recruiting; post hoc analyses of heart-failure trials stratified by eGDR, a non-insulin surrogate, exist, for example FINEARTS-HF and FIDELITY in CKD with type 2 diabetes (PMID 38151465; null interaction for cardiovascular and kidney outcomes)); STAR-type or closed-loop RCTs of insulin-sensitivity measurement; PCOS (a 12-record string not fully screened as described). Several steelman "survived" claims rested on zero-hit or top-5/8 screens of 286-305 records and belong here, not under surviving absences.
- **Steelman limits:** top 5-10 titles per search were screened; ICTRP, CTIS, Embase, Cochrane and Chinese databases were not searched; the ADA/EASD 2026 consensus, AACE 2026 algorithm and AHA 2023 CKM advisory could not be retrieved; it did not try to find more evidence against utility. Not read in full by anyone: TriMaster, MR, TRIO-BD, pioglitazone-depression, the NAFLD cohort, the two C-peptide papers.
- **Two different studies would help, answering two different questions.** (1) Routine verdict: the pre-specified incremental-value study already listed as open gap 2 (delta C, category-based and category-free NRI, calibration, decision curve, over PREVENT, SCORE2 or PCE, external diverse cohort), plus any trial of measurement-guided care. (2) One niche: the steelman's proposed post-stroke/TIA pioglitazone trial enrolling all HOMA-IR strata with a second randomization of "measure and treat if HOMA-IR is above 3.0" against "treat by glycaemic category". The niche trial would answer whether the IR value or the glycaemic category identifies responders (Spence 2019 suggests the latter may), but it would leave the routine verdict unchanged, needs a sample several times IRIS's size with no obvious sponsor, and its requirement of an assay traceable to an isotope-dilution reference is hard to meet (no JCTLM-listed insulin reference method exists, though an isotope-dilution MS comparator has been used in research).

## What is not supported as stated
- That IR **phenotypes** carry decision-relevant prognostic information (Set A endocrinologist, citing Wagner 2021). Those clusters were built from imaging, genetics and OGTT variables, not an IR index.
- That "IR testing adds nothing to PCOS management." The guideline says not to test routinely; the stratified-metformin evidence is the PCOS IPD interaction (RR 1.03) (PMID 31647106, DOI 10.1093/humupd/dmz029, full text read: an exploratory interaction between baseline serum insulin and the effect of clomiphene plus metformin versus clomiphene on live birth, in a comparison of 8 RCTs and 1,039 women of which 5 RCTs and 907 women had live-birth data; main live-birth effect RR 1.08, 0.87-1.35, low certainty; the full text shows the 1.03 is per U/mL of fasting insulin on 4 RCTs and 741 women, not the 907-woman live-birth set, and that the subgroup analyses were labelled exploratory; the 2023 guideline recommends this combination without insulin stratification and makes letrozole first line) and the DPP cluster result above, neither an outcome trial.
- **Grade "Moderate" for "don't test" verdicts** (Set B PCP). Use Set A's "Low"; the ceiling is set by absent trials.
- The claim that 1-hour OGTT glucose is better supported than insulin (the cited paper compares it with IFG, not insulin).
- Both sets' physiology claim that the clamp is muscle-weighted and HOMA-IR hepatic-weighted rests on weak or uncited sources. It is plausible and textbook-standard, but unsourced here, and the team never agreed whether surrogate indices represent whole-body or basal/hepatic IR.

## Corrections to carry forward
- "IR is not in the MASLD guideline" is wrong (see above). "High" evidence for a negative claim is the wrong kind of grade; it should be "not tested".
- The scientist's "TyG vs clamp AUC 0.86" is the top of a range (0.59-0.88) from an in-sample cutoff. The biostatistician's gloss of Bonora's kappa 0.63 as "substantial misclassification" reverses the authors' "good agreement"; whether 0.63 is adequate for a clinical threshold is open.
- PMID 12087016 is the San Antonio Heart Study, not IRAS; the association was only modestly reduced after adjustment.
- The ARIC Schmidt 2005 paper did not test insulin as a predictor and should not be cited as evidence about HOMA-IR increment.
- The EASL MASLD guideline appears as the full guideline in two journals (PMIDs 38851997, 38852583) plus an executive summary (38869512).
- The Isokuortti "HOMA-IR 2.0 in Helsinki reads 1.3-2.1 elsewhere" illustration is not about one patient and should not be cited as such.
- DOIs for PMIDs cited without them (verified against PubMed): IRIS 26886418 = 10.1056/NEJMoa1506930; Spence 2019 30734043 = 10.1001/jamaneurol.2019.0079; MR 32398352 = 10.1136/bmjdrc-2020-001217; ATTICA 41178693 = 10.1111/dom.70270; Liu 2026 42569452 = 10.3389/fcvm.2026.1804411; Health ABC 19700136 = 10.1016/j.cardfail.2009.03.001.

## Open gaps (no run closed them)
1. Re-screen the searcher's large result sets (decision curve, delta-C over PREVENT, SCORE2 or PCE, CV-outcome-trial subgroups), and search Embase, Cochrane CENTRAL, Scopus/Web of Science, EU CTIS, ChiCTR/CTRI/JPRN directly, the EFLM Biological Variation Database, and short-string queries for assay variability (two chemist "gaps" were found by two-word queries). A MeSH-based PubMed search (Insulin Resistance with Randomized Controlled Trial[pt] and patient-selection or interaction terms) was run once by the steelman (305 and 286 records) with only the top 5 and 8 screened, so it does not close this; screen the full sets.
2. A pre-specified incremental-value study (delta C, NRI by events and non-events, calibration, decision curve) of HOMA-IR (fasting-based) and TyG or TG/HDL-C (non-insulin surrogates) over PREVENT (US) or SCORE2 (Europe) in an external, more diverse cohort. It should report category-based reclassification at the treatment thresholds now in use: PREVENT-ASCVD 10-year risk under 3%, 3 to under 5%, 5 to under 10% and 10% or more (2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidaemia guideline), and PREVENT-CVD, which includes heart failure, at 7.5% (2026 CKM guideline). PCE can be kept as a secondary comparator for older cohorts. The same question for post-load insulin or OGTT-derived indices (Matsuda, insulin AUC, insulinogenic index) over fasting glucose, HbA1c, 2-hour glucose and BMI has had only a handful of search strings, and C-peptide as a predictor or effect modifier (about 138 records) was never screened.
3. Adequately sized, prespecified treatment-by-baseline-measure interaction evidence with hard outcomes, stated per measure (fasting, post-load, C-peptide) (tests found: DIETFITS, post-load insulin, specified before data were examined but added after the trial began, null, weight-loss outcome; and the MA.32 baseline fasting insulin and HOMA analyses, protocol-specified with distant recurrence-free survival as their outcome, 72 unadjusted tests, null for invasive disease-free survival and compatible with chance, in non-diabetic early breast cancer only; TZD response signal in Dennis 2019 is unreplicated)..
4. Assay-harmonised, clamp-anchored, ethnicity-specific cutoffs, and a study quantifying how often people cross a cutoff on repeat testing.
5. Read, not scan, the guideline texts that are only regex- or window-checked (ADA 2025 and 2026 sections not yet read in full), and the ones not read at all (list above). ## How to use the two sets
Take Set B's scenarios and abstract-level numbers with Set A's evidence grades and its guideline and trial leads. Prefer Set A for the biostatistician role and add Set B's WHI, NHANES and TyG meta-analysis findings. Treat the comparison.md "more reliable set" rankings as reliable only for the biostatistician.
